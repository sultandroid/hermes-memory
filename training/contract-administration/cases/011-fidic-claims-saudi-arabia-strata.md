## Direct Quote
> "The most frequent error is failing to serve notice within the required 28-day window. Contractors often believe they can wait until impacts are fully quantified before raising a claim, but FIDIC requires notice when an event occurs, not when its consequences become financially evident. In Saudi projects involving multiple parties and complex coordination, it's easy for crucial dates to slip away unnoticed."

> "Many contractors focus on daily work execution and fail to contemporaneously record delays, variations, and changed circumstances. When a dispute arises months or years later, reconstructing events from memory is unreliable and unconvincing to arbitrators. Saudi mega-projects with thousands of workers and multiple shifting priorities make meticulous documentation even more critical."

> "Variations are commonplace in Vision 2030 projects, but disputes frequently arise regarding whether work is a variation entitling the contractor to additional time and cost, or whether it's part of the original scope."

## Summary
Strata's article is a practical risk-management guide for contractors on Saudi Vision 2030 mega-projects (NEOM, The Line, Red Sea Project). It maps the FIDIC Cl. 20 procedure (notice → detailed claim within 42 days → Engineer's fair determination under Cl. 3.5/3.7 → DAB → arbitration) and identifies the five most common Saudi-contractor pitfalls: missed 28-day notice periods, inadequate documentation, variation disputes, causation challenges, and weak technical substantiation. The article is useful as a *current* (2024-vintage) Saudi-perspective overlay to the older Al Tamimi cases.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** The 28-day clock starts on the contractor becoming "aware (or should have become aware)" of the event — not on quantification. The "should have become aware" formulation is the same one the Symms article (Case 01) flagged; it converts a subjective test into an objective one.
2. **Evidence lesson:** Vision 2030 mega-projects have *thousands* of workers and complex coordination — the contemporaneous record (daily diaries, monthly reports, RFIs, weather logs, EIs) is the only way to reconstruct the awareness date and the causal link.
3. **Procedural lesson:** The DAB process is *not* optional under most FIDIC 1999 contracts — but under FIDIC 2017 the DAAB is the mandatory standing body. Saudi contractors should know which edition governs their contract before assuming the DAB route is available.
4. **Strategic lesson:** Variation disputes on Vision 2030 projects often turn on whether the change was "part of the original scope" — the contractor should keep a "scope baseline" document, signed by the Engineer at the start of the project, listing what was *excluded* from the scope as much as what was included.
5. **The principle:** *"The complexity of Vision 2030 mega-projects makes procedural discipline (notice + records) more important, not less."* A contractor who relies on the size of the claim to substitute for procedural compliance is making a bet the FIDIC clauses do not allow.
