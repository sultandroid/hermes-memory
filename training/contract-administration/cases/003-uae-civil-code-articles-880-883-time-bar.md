## Direct Quote
> "Article 883: No claim for compensation shall be heard after the expiration of three years from the collapse or the discovery of the defect."

> "If a defect is discovered on 1 January, 2015 [within the 10-year DL period starting 1 January 2010], the employer would have only until 1 January, 2018 to bring a claim against the consultant or contractor. If he fails to do so, the employer's claim would be time barred after 1 January, 2018."

> "The application of Articles 880 and 883 of the UAE Civil Code was addressed in the judgment of the UAE Federal Supreme Court (Court of Cassation) Case No. 267 of Year No. 17 in the Civil Circuit, dated 28 January 1996. The facts of the case are as follows: a contract was entered into between the appellant and the defendant, whereby the appellant engaged the defendant to construct a building project. The final handover of the building (works) was on 11 December 1982; and the appellant discovered a defect in the building (works) that threatened its safety and notified the defendant, on 8 December 1986 of the existence of such defect and requested the defendant to carry out the necessary [remedial works]."

## Summary
This article explains the dual-track limitation regime that overlays every UAE construction contract: a 10-year *decennial liability* (Article 880) during which the contractor and consultant are jointly liable for collapse or safety-threatening defects, and a 3-year limitation (Article 883) running from the date the defect is *discovered* (or the collapse occurs). The combined effect is that the practical window for an employer to bring a defect claim is the *shorter* of (a) the remaining DL period and (b) 3 years from discovery — and crucially, a defect discovered in year 10 must be litigated within 3 years or it is lost. The cited Federal Supreme Court case applied that rule to dismiss an employer's claim.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** *Decennial liability* (المسؤولية العشرية — Article 880) is the substantive 10-year liability of the contractor + supervising engineer; *limitation under Article 883* is the 3-year procedural cut-off from discovery. Both must be pleaded.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The trigger document is the *date of discovery* of the defect, not the date of completion or handover. A contemporaneous "defect notification letter" sent the day the employer spots the issue is the single most valuable document.
3. **Procedural lesson:** The time-bar must be raised as a *defence at the Court of First Instance*. If the defendant fails to invoke Article 883 there, it cannot be raised at the Court of Appeal or Court of Cassation. Procedural discipline matters.
4. **Strategic lesson:** A contractor defending should immediately check the discovery date; an employer plaintiff should send a *defect notification* on the day of discovery (even before quantifying damages) to lock the start of the 3-year clock and create a clear evidentiary record.
5. **The principle:** *"The 10-year decennial period is the outer wall; the 3-year Article 883 period is the inner wall that the employer must climb over before the wall itself expires."* A defect discovered in year 9 must still be litigated by year 12 or it's lost.
