## Quick Facts
- **Forum / doc type:** Consultancy / claims-practice article
- **Subject:** Whether the FIDIC 1999 Red Book Cl. 13.5 (Provisional Sums) allows a contractor to recover *time* (EOT) when a PS is expended, given that Cl. 8.4 does not list PSs as an EOT ground
- **Outcome for contractors:** The Middle East position is *unclear* — the silence in the standard form creates a loophole; contractors have an argument under Cl. 8.4(a) (variation / substantial change in quantity) and Cl. 1.9 (delayed drawings/instructions) but the EOT entitlement is not automatic

## Theme Mapped
**5. Provisional sum / Daywork disputes**

## Direct Quote
> "From recent experience when using the FIDIC Red Book (1999) Standard form of Contract, there is a significant lacuna when applying Clause 13.5 – Provisional Sums. The FIDIC Red Book does not make any distinction between so called 'Defined' and 'Undefined' provisional sums, but does at least provide a definition of what a Provisional Sum can contain and how they are to be valued."

> "The FIDIC Red Book at Clause 8.4, the extension of time clause, does not specifically mention Provisional Sums as grounds for an extension of time. Is the inference therefore that the Contractor has accepted the programming risk of such expenditure. Possibly so in that Clause 4.11, Sufficiency of the Accepted Contract Amount, states: 'Unless otherwise stated in the Contract, the Accepted Contract Amount covers all the Contractor's obligations under the Contract (including those under provisional sums, if any)…'"

> "But what is to happen where a stated value for a Provisional Sum proves to be understated? For example, what if an AED 2 Million Provisional Sum is let at say AED 5 Million. …if the additional cost is a result of additional work then what is the Contractor's position. The answer may well be the use of Clause 8.4(a) which lists grounds for extension of time and states: 'a Variation… or other substantial change in the quantity of an item of work included in the Contract'."

> "The practice of inserting provisional sums into contracts is commonplace, but doing so introduces risks for both parties, and it is time (no pun intended) that the contractual provisions in FIDIC were amended to introduce clarity into the use of provisional sums."

## Summary
Rimmer highlights a real drafting gap in the FIDIC 1999 Red Book: the PS regime in Cl. 13.5 is silent on whether the *expenditure* of a PS by Engineer's instruction can give rise to an EOT. UK practice (under SMM7) distinguishes "Defined" PSs (programming risk on the contractor) from "Undefined" PSs (programming risk on the employer); the FIDIC form does not adopt this distinction, and the Middle East has not developed a consensus workaround. Rimmer argues that the contractor has a route via Cl. 8.4(a) — a substantial change in quantity of an item of work, treated as a variation — and via Cl. 1.9 (delayed drawings or instructions) if the Engineer fails to instruct the PS in accordance with the approved programme. He recommends amending the Particular Conditions to close the loophole.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** "Provisional sum" ≠ "contingency" ≠ "prime cost sum" — the FIDIC 1999 PS is an estimate of work *not yet fully defined*; the contractor is entitled to be paid for the actual work done but the time consequence is not automatic.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The approved programme at the time of tender is the linchpin — if the PS work was shown in the programme at the tender stage, the contractor is presumed to have allowed the time; if it was not, the contractor has the Cl. 8.4(a) argument.
3. **Procedural lesson:** A PS expenditure must still be instructed under Cl. 13.5 / Cl. 3.3 (instruction of variations) and the contractor must give the Cl. 20.1 28-day notice *if* it intends to claim additional time — the time bar applies to PS claims just as it does to any other claim.
4. **Strategic lesson:** On a Saudi Vision 2030 mega-project with substantial PS allowances for ground conditions or authority-driven scope, *amend the Particular Conditions* to make the EOT entitlement explicit. Do not rely on Cl. 8.4(a) to be interpreted in your favour by an Engineer.
5. **The principle:** *"The FIDIC 1999 Red Book has a genuine lacuna on PS programming risk; the contractor who fails to flag this at tender bears the risk of an unfunded time extension."* The fix is contractual, not forensic.
