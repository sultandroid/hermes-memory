## Quick Facts

- **Subject:** Distinction between *limitation periods* (مدة التقادم) and *time bars* (مواعيد سقوط) under Egyptian law as applied to FIDIC notice clauses
- **Outcome for contractors:** A missed FIDIC 28-day notice is a *time bar* — it extinguishes the contractual right itself, not merely the remedy; under Egyptian law, contractual time bars are generally upheld (unlike mandatory limitation periods).

## Theme Mapped
**3. Time-bar (سقوط الحق) cases** — missing the contractual notice deadline

## Direct Quote
> "Where limitation periods were introduced for the stability of transactions, time bars are introduced in order to set a specific duration during which a person is entitled to exercise the right to claim. At the expiry of such duration, that person is barred from such claim."

> "According to the FIDIC forms of contract, the Contractor's failure to comply with such notice requirement would render that the Contractor shall not be entitled to additional payment or extension of the Time for Completion and the Employer is discharged from all liability in connection with such claim."

## Summary
El Haggan draws the under-appreciated doctrinal line between limitation periods (governed by Article 374 of the Egyptian Civil Code — 15 years, suspendable, of *public order* and not amendable by contract) and *contractual* time bars (the FIDIC 28-day notice under Cl. 20.1). The latter are a creature of the contract: they are not of public order, are generally upheld, and operate as an *extinctive* condition rather than a procedural bar. The article maps the consequences for Saudi / GCC practitioners handling Egyptian-law-governed contracts (common in Cairo-region industrial and tourism projects), and confirms that, in contrast to the UAE/Qatari position discussed in Case 01, the Egyptian courts are far less likely to read down a FIDIC time bar.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** Time bar = *موعد سقوط* (extinctive, contractual). Limitation = *مدة تقادم* (procedural, statutory). The Arabic terms must not be used interchangeably in any pleading.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The article flags the importance of "when the contractor became aware (or should have become aware)" as the trigger — the same wording as FIDIC 20.1, so the awareness evidence rules from Case 01 apply with extra force.
3. **Procedural lesson:** Under Egyptian law, a contractual time bar can be raised by either party even if the underlying limitation period has not yet expired — and the bar operates as a *defence to the substantive right*, not just the remedy.
4. **Strategic lesson:** Drafters of bespoke GCC contracts (especially those choosing Egyptian or Egyptian-influenced governing law) cannot rely on common-law "manifest injustice" escape routes the way UAE courts might allow — the FIDIC 28-day bar will hold.
5. **The principle:** *"Time bars are creatures of contract, not public order; the parties' agreement to extinguish a right by inaction is enforceable, but only if the wording is precise."* Always check whether the relevant clause says "shall be a condition precedent" or merely "the Contractor shall give notice as soon as practicable" — only the former bites as a time bar.
