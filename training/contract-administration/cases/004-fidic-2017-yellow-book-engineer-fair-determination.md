# Case 04 — New FIDIC 2017 Yellow Book: Engineer's Fair Determination (Sub-Clause 3.7)

## Citation
- **Title:** "New FIDIC 2017 Yellow Book | A New Claims Procedure"
- **Author:** Al Tamimi & Company (Construction & Infrastructure)
- **Year:** March 2017
- **URL:** https://www.tamimi.com/law-update/march-10/articles/new-fidic-2017-yellow-book-a-new-claims-procedure/

## Quick Facts
- **Forum / doc type:** Law-firm article analysing FIDIC's pre-release Second Edition
- **Subject:** The shift from Cl. 3.5 (1999) to Cl. 3.7 (2017): the Engineer now acts *neutrally*, not as the Employer's agent, when making a "fair determination" of disputed claims
- **Outcome for contractors:** Materially improved — but only if the engineer-determination window is actively managed (42-day amicable-settlement period, 28-day Notice of Dissatisfaction to challenge)

## Theme Mapped
**7. Engineer's fair determination (FIDIC 2017 Cl. 3.7) — can contractor refuse it?**

## Direct Quote
> "Among the new features is that the Engineer is to act neutrally under Sub-Cause 3.7. 'Neutrally' has not been defined but can probably be taken to require the Engineer to act impartially. This interpretation is strengthened by the fact that Sub-Clause 3.2 states that the Engineer may issue determinations under Sub-Clause 3.7 without reference to the Employer. This is a significant change from Sub-Clause 3.1 of the First Edition which provided that the Engineer is deemed to act as the agent of the Employer."

> "If no agreement can be reached between the parties (notwithstanding the Engineer's efforts to reach an amicable settlement), the Engineer is required to make 'a fair determination'. This is consistent with the terminology of Sub-Clause 3.5 of the First Edition."

> "As with the First Edition, the Engineer's determination will be binding on the parties unless either party issues a Notice of Dissatisfaction within 28 days. This is the first of several time bars to the claims and disputes procedure under the Second Edition."

## Summary
The 2017 FIDIC Yellow Book (Second Edition) restructures the Engineer as a neutral quasi-arbitrator on disputed claims, with a 42-day window during which the Engineer must *encourage* (and document) joint discussions before issuing a "fair determination." Once issued, the determination is *binding but not final* — either party may issue a Notice of Dissatisfaction (NOD) within 28 days, after which the dispute escalates to the DAAB. The article emphasises that under Cl. 3.7 the Engineer is expressly empowered to act *without reference to the Employer* — a deliberate FIDIC policy choice intended to break the 1999-era "Engineer is Employer's agent" pattern. The AfiTaC commentary (Case 04 supplement) confirms this: "FIDIC now clearly establishes that payments shall anyway be made in the next Statement / Payment Certificate" even where the determination is challenged.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** "Fair determination" is not "correct determination" — it is a determination the Engineer can stand behind as impartial and reasonable on the materials before them. Contractors should not attack the *correctness* in the NOD; they should attack the *process* and the *evidence* the Engineer failed to consider.
2. **Evidence lesson:** Sub-Clause 3.7.1 requires the Engineer to consult the parties *jointly*. The minute notes of those joint consultations are the most valuable pre-arbitration document — they fix what the Engineer was told and what the Engineer failed to investigate.
3. **Procedural lesson:** Failure to issue a Notice of Dissatisfaction within 28 days *converts* the determination into a binding final award. There is no equity-style relief for missing this 28-day NOD. The 28-day clock is the *first* time bar in the 2017 chain; missing it forfeits the right to escalate.
4. **Strategic lesson:** A contractor can refuse to be bound *only* via a timely NOD. But there is no tactical upside in *refusing to participate* in the joint-consultation phase under 3.7.1 — silence is read as acquiescence and becomes grist for the Engineer's determination.
5. **The principle:** *"The Engineer is no longer the Employer's agent when determining claims; the contractor is no longer required to accept whatever the Engineer says."* But this neutrality comes with strict 28-day discipline — the 2017 suite traded a biased decision-maker for a tightly-timed procedure.
