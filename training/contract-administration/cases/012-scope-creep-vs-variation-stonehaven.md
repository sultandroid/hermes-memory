## Theme Mapped
**4. Scope creep vs legitimate variation — case lines**

## Direct Quote
> "Scope creep in construction project management is the uncontrolled expansion of project requirements after the original scope has been agreed. It usually happens when changes are introduced without a formal change order, impact review, or re-baselining of time, cost and resources."

> "In practice, scope creep is not the same as a controlled variation. A controlled variation is documented, priced, reviewed, approved and incorporated into the live baseline. Scope creep is what happens when that governance breaks down."

> "On live construction projects, uncontrolled changes rarely stay small for long. In the UAE and wider GCC, scope creep is especially risky because many projects run on compressed schedules, multiple stakeholder approvals and tightly coordinated procurement packages."

## Summary
Stonehaven draws the operative line between *controlled variation* (under a FIDIC Cl. 13 variation order: documented, priced under Cl. 12.3, time-evaluated under Cl. 8.4, approved by the Engineer, and incorporated into the revised programme) and *scope creep* (any change that is *not* processed through that discipline — even if the change is small and the parties informally agree to it on site). The article's contribution is the *practical* list of triggers (poorly defined scope at the outset, late client requests, weak change control, late stakeholder approvals) and the *consequence* catalogue (budget overruns, delayed milestones, rework, contractual disputes, strained relationships, reduced quality). On a UAE Vision / KSA Vision 2030 project, where authority approvals can move the design basis mid-construction, the *governance* (or lack of it) is the line between variation and scope creep.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** *Scope creep* (تمدد النطاق) is uncontrolled, undocumented expansion; *variation* (أمر تغيير) is controlled, priced, approved expansion. The contract only recognises the second.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The "scope baseline" document — a signed statement at project commencement of what is and (crucially) is *not* included — is the most valuable evidence when a later dispute arises as to whether the change was scope or variation.
3. **Procedural lesson:** A site instruction that is *not* followed by a Cl. 13.1 / 13.3 variation order within a contractually-stipulated window is the textbook scope-creep red flag. The contractor should refuse to execute the change until a VO is issued, or issue a written protest.
4. **Strategic lesson:** Late stakeholder requests are inevitable on mega-projects. The fix is a *pre-priced catalogue* of common client upgrades (e.g., finish upgrades, façade revisions) that can be selected from a menu with a known cost and time impact — converting scope creep into controlled variation.
5. **The principle:** *"Scope creep is not a planning problem; it is a governance failure."* A robust change-control procedure, applied without exception, is the only reliable prevention.
