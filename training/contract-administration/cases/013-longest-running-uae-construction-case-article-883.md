# Case 13 — The Longest-Running Construction Case in UAE Legal History (1993–2015): Article 883 Time Bar Defence

Case identifiers: Abu Dhabi Court of First Instance Case No. 304/1993; Court of Appeal decisions 17 March 2015; UAE Federal Supreme Court (Court of Cassation) Appeals No. 319/2015 and 320/2015, judgment 9 December 2015.

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** Abu Dhabi Court of First Instance → Court of Appeal → UAE Federal Supreme Court (Court of Cassation)
- **Project:** Sewage system in Abu Dhabi, completed 1983
- **Claim value:** ~US$100 million (against the original Defendant, an engineering consultancy); subsidiary actions against the main contractor
- **Outcome for the main contractor (defended by Al Tamimi):** **Won at every level** on Article 883 (UAE Civil Code) time-bar defence

## Theme Mapped
**3. Time-bar (سقوط الحق) cases** — Article 883 application + the procedural rule that the bar must be raised at first instance

## Direct Quote
> "The First Joined Defendant argued that the Plaintiff and the First and Second Defendants discovered the last defects in the Project in 1991 but the subsidiary action against the First Joined Defendant in connection with the defect did not commence until 1996, i.e. after the expiration of the 3-year limitation period imposed by Article 883. The First Joined Defendant adopted also the same legal defence when it was joined to the case as a co-defendant in 2001 at the Plaintiff's request."

> "In March 2013, the Court of First Instance issued its judgment in case No. 304/1993 with respect to the Plaintiff's claim and the First and Second Defendant's subsidiary actions. It dismissed both claims against the First Joined Defendant on the basis that the First Joined Defendant was not joined in the case as a co-defendant until after three years had passed from the discovery of the defect in the Project and, therefore, the claim and the subsidiary action against the First Joined Defendant was statute barred pursuant to Article 883 of the UAE Civil Code."

> "…the collapse in the works of both agreements occurred during the warrantee period and the First Appellant did not file its sub-claim during the three years prescription period (time-limitation), therefore, it shall not be heard." — Court of Cassation, 9 December 2015.

## Summary
A 1983-completed Abu Dhabi sewage system developed substantial defects. In 1993 the Abu Dhabi government sued the design consultant and its insurer. In 1996 the consultant brought a subsidiary action against the main contractor (Al Tamimi's client). In 2001 the Court joined the main contractor as a co-defendant at the plaintiff's request. The main contractor's defence at every stage was the same: *Article 883 of the UAE Civil Code* (3-year limitation from discovery of the defect) — the defects were discovered by 1991, the subsidiary action did not commence until 1996, the joinder as a co-defendant in 2001 was *also* outside the 3-year window. The Court of First Instance (March 2013) and Court of Appeal (March 2015) both accepted the time-bar defence; the Federal Supreme Court (9 December 2015) rejected the plaintiff's appeals. Total duration: 22 years.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** *Joinder* (إدخال طرف) as a co-defendant in 2001 did *not* reset the Article 883 clock — the court looked back to the date the claim was first asserted (1996), not the date the formal joinder occurred. The clock runs from when the cause of action accrued, not from procedural manoeuvres.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The Court-appointed expert's report (2005, supplementary 2011) was the technical linchpin. The expert confirmed that the *last defects* were discovered in 1991 — this fixed the start of the 3-year clock. Always commission a contemporaneous defect-discovery log; never rely on a plaintiff's pleaded discovery date.
3. **Procedural lesson:** The Article 883 time bar must be raised at the *first* available opportunity. In this case, the main contractor raised it in its very first defence filing in 1996 and re-raised it at the 2001 joinder. Procedural diligence across 19 years preserved the defence.
4. **Strategic lesson:** Even when a plaintiff's case has obvious technical merit, an *unanswered* time-bar defence is a complete defence. The plaintiff was put to proof of the 1991 discovery date, but lost on the documentary record.
5. **The principle:** *"A 3-year time bar in a 10-year decennial-liability regime can be a complete defence — but only if raised at the first opportunity and supported by contemporaneous evidence of the actual discovery date."* The Article 883 defence is paper-thin without a defect-discovery log.
