## Summary
The paper addresses the "critical need for standardising" BIM Execution Plans (BEPs) across the AECO sector. The authors analysed 36 BEP documents from international organisations, ran a scientometric analysis of 2020-2024 BEP literature, and surveyed 87 industry professionals to evaluate which sections of a BEP are perceived as most important for collaboration, document management and alignment with local standards. Their framework consolidates the canonical BEP sections — project overview, stakeholder roles, BIM goals/uses, LOIN / LOD / LOI specifications, model standards, volume/federation strategy, collaboration strategy (meeting schedule, CDE), technical requirements (software / hardware / IT security / training) — into a single adaptable structure. The Saudi connection is direct (Alnaser at King Saud University; Abdelalim registered with the Saudi Council of Engineers) and the paper responds to the recurring dispute type where the appointed party's BEP is found to be inconsistent with the appointing party's EIR or local standards.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "This study addresses the critical need for standardizing building information modeling (BIM) execution plans (BEPs) in the architecture, engineering, construction, and operations (AECO) sector. Through the analysis of 36 BEP documents from international organizations, we have identified crucial components and put forth a comprehensive framework with the objective of improving [BIM execution]."

> "The results unveiled a significant degree of agreement regarding the fundamental essence of BEPs, with a particular emphasis on the significance of management and project objectives. This was accompanied by an acknowledgment of the need to incorporate BEPs into project cycles at an earlier stage to optimize their efficacy. Furthermore, the study emphasized the need for substantial enhancements in aligning BEPs with local standards, improving document management, and promoting collaborative practices. These observations emphasize the need for a standardized and adaptable strategy for BIM execution plans."

## Lesson Extracted
1. **A BEP that is not aligned to the EIR is a contract breach**: The paper finds that BEPs are routinely inconsistent with local standards. In a contractual dispute, the appointed party's failure to align its BEP with the appointing party's EIR is a discharge-of-duty issue independent of model quality.
2. **BEP-mobilisation timing decides entitlement**: BEPs incorporated "at an earlier stage" optimise efficacy. Late-stage BEPs (post-tender award, mid-construction) evidence a contractor that is failing to discharge its BIM-design obligations and can shift responsibility for downstream clash, LOD and federation failures.
3. **The 87-survey baseline is a reasonableness benchmark**: With 87 practitioners' views behind it, the framework's enumeration of BEP sections (BIM goals, LOIN, LOD/LOI, model standards, federation strategy, CDE, technical requirements) sets the industry standard of care for what a "competent BEP" must contain. A BEP missing any of these is below standard.
4. **The BEP must quantify the CDE workflow**: Section 4.4.8 of the paper's framework prescribes collaboration strategy as a six-part sub-clause covering exchange formats, software/hardware needs, security, IT upgrades and training. A contractor's BEP that does not specify the CDE configuration in those terms cannot discharge the corresponding EIR information-management requirements.
5. **Disputes crystallise around the LOIN/acceptance-criteria gap**: A BEP that does not formalise a Level-of-Information-Need matrix per discipline and per stage loses to a SCCA tribunal that asks "what was the contractor's measurable obligation at each RIBA stage?" The framework's explicit LOIN specification is the contractual firewall.

## How to use this in a BIM-coordination playbook
Use the 36-BEP analysis as the cross-check standard: if a tendered BEP does not address the canonical sections listed, it is below the academic and industry consensus of competent discharge. In expert testimony, the framework is citable for the proposition that "a competent BEP must contain, at minimum, sections covering …" — a methodology the SCCA tribunal would accept.
