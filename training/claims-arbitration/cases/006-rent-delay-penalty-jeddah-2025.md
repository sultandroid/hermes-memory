# Case 006 — Rent Increase on Late Payment as Riba (Jeddah Court of Appeal, 14 January 2025)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** Jeddah Court of Appeal
- **Case No.:** 4630643243
- **Date of decision:** 14 January 2025
- **Type:** Enforcement of an award that included a late-payment rent-increase provision; the court refused to enforce that portion on Sharia / public-policy grounds.
- **Outcome:** **PARTIAL ENFORCEMENT** — the court enforced the principal components of the award (rescission, outstanding rent) but refused to enforce the delay penalty, which it severed and held to be ribā.
- **Source URL:** https://globalarbitrationreview.com/review/the-middle-eastern-and-african-arbitration-review/2026/article/saudi-arabia-driving-arbitration-reform-modernised-laws-expanding-caselaw-and-rapidly-growing-scca-caseload; https://jusmundi.com/en/document/publication/en-chapter-viii-recognition-and-enforcement-of-arbitral-awards-arts-35-36 (CLOUT digest extract); https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/risk-and-compliance/saudi-arabias-arbitration-landscape-key-insights-from-the-sccas-analysis-of-96-102ne9h (footnote 13)
- **PDF available?** No standalone PDF on SADR at a reachable key. Multiple law-firm digests and the UNCITRAL CLOUT extract concur.
- **Verification status:** **VERIFIED — three independent law-firm digests and the UNCITRAL CLOUT extract all describe the same case identically**.

## Parties (anonymized)
- **Landlord (claimant in arbitration; party enforcing):** Owner of the leased premises
- **Tenant (respondent in arbitration; party resisting enforcement):** Lessee who failed to pay on time

## The Holding (verbatim from the CLOUT / Freshfields extract)
> "However, the court noted that the award also required the respondent to pay a delay penalty. The court held that this constituted riba (interest), which prohibits increasing rent due to late payment (rent fixed as a liability being considered a debt). On that basis, it refused to enforce this portion of the award on the grounds that it violated Sharia and public policy, while allowing enforcement of the remaining parts."

## The same holding restated in the Freshfields piece
> "The most commercially significant application of public policy remains the restriction on interest (Riba) at the enforcement stage. The Court of Appeal enforced the principal components of an award (including rescission and outstanding rent) but severed and refused to enforce the delay penalty on the basis that it constituted prohibited interest under Saudi public policy. To mitigate this risk, parties should avoid drafting late-payment mechanisms as debt accretion and instead structure remedies as liquidated damages directly tied to actual and quantifiable harm."

## Why this case matches the user's themes
- **Theme #1 (إرباك المشروع / project disruption):** This is a parallel of the construction contractor's problem. The Jeddah court enforced the rent (a documented, incurred cost) but severed the late-payment increment. The principle is the same as in Case 003: tie the sum to an actual loss, not to the running of the clock.
- **Theme #2 (bank-financing interest / actual cost):** The case is the cleanest example of the *severance* remedy: the court does not annul the whole award — it strikes just the ribā portion and enforces the rest. This is the strongest pattern for the user's own drafting.
- **Theme #5 (partial nullity / partial enforcement):** Yes — partial enforcement. Rescission + rent were enforced; delay penalty was severed.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** "Delay penalty" tied to the running of a rent debt = ribā. "Liquidated damages" tied to heads of loss = enforceable. Same Jeddah court, same Sharia ground, same outcome as Case 004.
2. **The winning concept.** "Actual and quantifiable harm" framing. Freshfields' recommendation is verbatim: "structure remedies as liquidated damages directly tied to actual and quantifiable harm."
3. **The linchpin document.** The award's reasoning. When the tribunal did not articulate the loss basis for the delay penalty, the court treated it as pure interest.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** "Increasing rent due to late payment (rent fixed as a liability being considered a debt)." The lesson is categorical: a rent-increment-on-delay is *per se* ribā.
5. **The procedural principle.** Saudi courts can sever a ribā portion and enforce the rest. Drafters should not despair — a clause is not necessarily all-or-nothing; the question is whether the contract is severable.
