## The Holding (verbatim from Freshfields)
> "Public policy challenges focus primarily on violations of mandatory statutory frameworks. For example, courts have annulled awards where tribunals retroactively applied the Civil Transactions Law contrary to its transitional provisions or treated unregistered leases as valid despite mandatory Ejar platform registration requirements."

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** "Unregistered lease" (on Ejar) is *per se* unenforceable in the residential and commercial context. Drafters should never assume an oral or paper lease will be enforced.
2. **The winning concept.** "Mandatory Ejar platform registration requirements" — a Council of Ministers / Ministry of Justice mandate (Circular 13/T/8843 dated 19/12/1443H, also referenced in Case 002 above). The same circular is the basis of Case 002's second ground of annulment.
3. **The linchpin document.** The Ejar platform record (or its absence). If there is no Ejar entry, the lease does not produce administrative or judicial effects.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** The lease-validity portion of any award that purports to enforce an unregistered lease.
5. **The procedural principle.** A mandatory statutory framework — not Sharia, not public policy in the abstract — is the basis. Saudi courts increasingly anchor public-policy refusals in specific Council of Ministers or Ministry of Justice circulars, not in abstract Islamic doctrine.
