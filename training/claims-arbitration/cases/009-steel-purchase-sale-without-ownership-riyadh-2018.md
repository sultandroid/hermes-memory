# Case 009 — Steel-Purchase Award Set Aside for "Sale Without Ownership" (Riyadh Court of Appeals, 9 August 2018)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** First Commercial Circuit of the Riyadh Court of Appeals
- **Case No.:** 4876 of 1438H
- **Date of decision:** 9 August 2018
- **Type:** Nullity action under Article 50(2) — Sharia / public-policy ground (the foundational "no sale without ownership" case)
- **Outcome:** **FULL ANNULMENT** of the SAR 27 million award
- **Source URL (primary):** http://sadr.org/public/upload/media-center/bulletin/SCCA-Saudi-Case-Law-Study-Three-Years-in-Review-En.pdf (SCCA 3-Year Case Law Study, Section 3.4 "No Sale Without Ownership")
- **PDF available?** No standalone PDF; the SCCA primary source is the J. Int'l Arb. article.
- **Verification status:** **VERIFIED via SCCA primary source**.

## Parties (anonymized)
- **Defendant in arbitration / plaintiff in nullity:** Award debtor (in a steel-purchase contract context)
- **Claimant in arbitration / defendant in nullity:** Award creditor (received SAR 27M from the tribunal)

## The Holding (verbatim from SCCA 3-Year Study)
> "In 2018, the Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals considered a plaintiff's motion to annul an award under which the defendant was awarded SAR twenty-seven million in the context of a contract for the purchase of steel. The purchase was partially paid for by transferring two buildings. However, in a judgment back in 2016, the Riyadh General Court (lower court) already ruled that one of the buildings did not belong to the defendant. That judgment was later upheld by the court of appeals. The plaintiff argued that the award violated a Sharia principle that prohibits the transfer of ownership of property one does not own. The plaintiff argued that the tribunal ordered him to pay SAR twenty-seven million on the understanding that the plaintiff had received SAR ten million and SAR seventeen million in the form of the two buildings, one of which the tribunal ordered to be transferred to the plaintiff, when in fact the Riyadh General Court ordered it does not belong to the defendant. As such, this court held that the award violated Sharia, and consequently violated public policy under Article 50(2) because it ordered the plaintiff to pay the defendant SAR twenty-seven million for a building the defendant did not own and could not make use of. Thus, the court annulled the award."

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** "Sale without ownership" (بيع ما لا تملك) is the Sharia category. The Arabic term matters: this is not a Sharia debate about ribā or gharar; it is the foundational ownership rule. Drafters should ensure that the seller's title chain is uncontested before any property-based settlement.
2. **The winning concept (for the nullity plaintiff).** A prior court judgment (here, the 2016 Riyadh General Court ruling that one of the buildings did not belong to the defendant) that contradicts a key fact the tribunal relied on. Without that contradiction, the Sharia ground fails.
3. **The linchpin document.** The 2016 Riyadh General Court judgment — its existence is what made the Sharia ground a *contradiction of a settled judicial finding*, not just a doctrinal argument.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** The entire SAR 27 million award. Property-based settlements carry the highest Sharia-nullity risk if title is contested.
5. **The procedural principle.** Article 50(2) is a *back-end* filter: it operates when the tribunal's output itself requires the plaintiff to do something Sharia-prohibits (here, pay for property the defendant did not own). Drafters of mixed-cash-and-property settlements must obtain title confirmation before arbitration.
