- Sided with Binladin on most of the monetary claims
- Assigned all delay responsibility to Munshaat
- Rejected the technical expert's opinion and substituted its own technical judgment
- Awarded itself additional fees beyond the contract cap

## The Tribunal's Reasoning (per the PDF)

**Accepted by tribunal (upheld on nullity review):**
- Initial delivery certificate date (14/02/1437 H)
- Most monetary heads (a, b, c, d, e, f, g above)
- Extension of execution period by 891 days beyond original contract

**Rejected by tribunal (struck or modified):**
- Munshaat's argument that Binladin was notified of the award date properly
- Munshaat's argument about *bank-financing interest being ribā* (tribunal called this characterization "ambiguous" and "doubt-inducing" — and reframed as "actual cost compensation")

**Awarded to itself over Munshaat's objection:**
- Additional arbitration fees — but this is what the court annulled

## The Court's Ruling on Nullity

The court applied **Saudi Arbitration Law Art. 50/2** and analyzed the case under multiple grounds of nullity:

| Ground of nullity raised | Court's finding |
|---|---|
| Tribunal excluded agreed legal rules (Arts. 52/4, 68) | Did **not** annul — within tribunal's interpretive authority |
| Tribunal assigned all delay responsibility to Munshaat | Did **not** annul — within tribunal's factual findings |
| Tribunal rejected technical expert opinion and substituted own judgment | Did **not** annul — within tribunal's discretion |
| Tribunal's additional fees exceeded Art. "عاشراً" of arbitration deed | **ANNULLED** — exceeded contractual cap |
| Award language was tentative ("رأي الخبير") not definitive | Did **not** annul — within acceptable margin |
| Award contained ribā (bank-financing interest) | Did **not** annul — reframed as actual cost compensation |

**Operative clauses of the court ruling (from PDF page 6):**

1. Annulled the 4th and 5th operative clauses of the arbitration award (the additional fees)
2. Ordered the arbitration tribunal to return the certified check (شيك مصدق) for **SAR 902,558** to Munshaat's counsel (محمد بن محمد حريري حريري)
3. Dismissed all other grounds of nullity (رفض ما عدا ذلك)

## The Lessons (the 5 things to internalize)

### Lesson 1: **Vocabulary discipline is the difference between winning and losing**

The contractor **won** because the bank-financing interest was framed as:
- ✅ "Compensation for actual costs incurred" (تعويض عن تكاليف فعلية)
- ✅ "Out-of-pocket expenses" (not financial abstraction)
- ✅ "Cost of project disruption, tied to specific bank facilities"

If the same 10.8M had been claimed as "late-payment interest" (فوائد تأخير) or "financing penalty," it would have been struck as ribā.

**Rule:** Before drafting any claim head, ask: *"Is this riyal tied to an actual bank statement?"* If no, don't claim it that way.

### Lesson 2: **"Project disruption" (إرباك المشروع) is the winning concept**

The contractor won **61.1M SAR** in indirect admin costs because the court accepted the framing of "إرباك المشروع" — i.e. the contractor's project operations were *materially disrupted* by the respondent's conduct, causing actual indirect costs.

This is stronger than:
- ❌ "Lost profits" (أرباح فاتتة) — speculative
- ❌ "Delay damages" (تعويضات تأخير) — ribā-adjacent
- ❌ "Inflation" (تضخم) — market-rate, hard to prove

**Rule:** When claiming delay-related costs, use the "إرباك المشروع + تكاليف فعلية" framing, not "تعويضات تأخير" framing.

### Lesson 3: **"Initial delivery certificate date" locks the timeline**

The court accepted the tribunal's finding of **14/02/1437 H** as the initial delivery date. This single fact:
- Established when the 891-day extension started counting
- Anchored the contract balance claim (45.5M)
- Made the 9.5M initial delivery value claim solid

**Rule:** A signed initial delivery certificate (شهادة استلام ابتدائي) is the single most valuable document in a construction dispute. Protect it; verify the date; don't let it sit unsigned.

### Lesson 4: **Additional arbitration fees will get struck**

The tribunal tried to award itself extra fees. The court struck it under Art. "عاشراً" of the arbitration deed, which limits extra fees to **new requests by a party** — not "increased workload" or "longer proceedings."

**Rule:** In any arbitration deed (وثيقة تحكيم), Art. 10th-style caps on tribunal fees are not theoretical. Tribunals know this. The court enforces it.

### Lesson 5: **Document the technical expert, even if you reject him**

The tribunal **rejected the technical expert's opinion** but was overturned on appeal? No — actually, the court **upheld** the rejection. The lesson is different: the court accepted that a tribunal may reject a technical expert's opinion and substitute its own reading, **as long as the reasoning is articulated** in the award.

**Rule:** If you're a tribunal: when rejecting a technical expert, say so explicitly in the operative clauses, and explain the basis. A silent rejection is a ground for nullity.
