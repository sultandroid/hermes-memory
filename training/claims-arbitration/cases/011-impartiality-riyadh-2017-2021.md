# Case 011 — Impartiality / Conflict of Interest (Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals, 2017-2021)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals
- **Case No.:** Not published with a number in the SCCA 3-Year Study
- **Date of decision:** 2017-2021 (covered in the SCCA's first study, 2017-2020 judgments, re-cited in the 3-Year Study at Section 4.1[a][i])
- **Type:** Nullity action under Article 50(1)(e) — tribunal not impartially constituted
- **Outcome:** **FULL ANNULMENT**
- **Source URL (primary):** http://sadr.org/public/upload/media-center/bulletin/SCCA-Saudi-Case-Law-Study-Three-Years-in-Review-En.pdf (SCCA 3-Year Case Law Study, Section 4.1[a][i] "The Arbitrator Lacks Impartiality and Independence")

## Parties (anonymized)
- **Claimant in arbitration / plaintiff in nullity:** Award debtor challenging the impartiality of the tribunal
- **Respondent in arbitration / defendant in nullity:** Award creditor whose party-appointed arbitrator was challenged
- **Counsel / arbitrator link:** The respondent's counsel was simultaneously representing the challenged arbitrator in a *different* matter

## The Holding (verbatim from SCCA 3-Year Study)
> "In the underlying arbitration, the claimant (plaintiff) challenged the respondent's (defendant's) party-appointed arbitrator on grounds that the respondent's counsel was also representing the arbitrator in another matter. The tribunal, including the challenged arbitrator, rejected the challenge by a majority vote and proceeded to issue the award dismissing the claimant's claims. The plaintiff (claimant) filed a motion to annul the award with the Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals for the arbitrator's conflict of interest based on Articles 16, 17, and 49. Referring to Article 50(1)(e) and Article 16, the court concluded that the relationship between the respondent's party-appointed arbitrator and its counsel 'constitute[d] mutual interests and a close relationship', thus giving rise to justifiable doubts as to the impartiality and independence of the arbitrator. The court granted the plaintiff's motion and annulled the award."

## Why this case matches the user's themes
- **Theme #4 (Saudi nullity petition cases 2020-2025 with full judgment text available).** This case is from the 2017-2020 dataset but is the **leading impartiality case** that the SCCA 3-Year Study uses to frame all subsequent tribunal-composition annulments. It is also the case that gives Saudi courts their "close relationship / mutual interests" test.
- **Theme #5 (partial nullity).** No — full annulment. The defect went to the integrity of the process.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** "Mutual interests and a close relationship" — the Arabic-language test the SCCA reports. Saudi courts are not bound by the IBA Guidelines on Conflicts of Interest, but they apply a "justifiable doubts" test that overlaps materially.
2. **The winning concept for the plaintiff.** A contemporaneous, documented legal representation of the arbitrator by counsel for one of the parties. This is the cleanest form of conflict.
3. **The linchpin document.** The case file (or board minute) of the *other* matter in which the arbitrator was being represented by opposing counsel.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** Any award in which the tribunal majority rejected the challenge (the conflict was therefore decided by the conflicted arbitrator himself). The court annulled because the tribunal's own composition was tainted.
5. **The procedural principle.** A party-appointed arbitrator is not exempt from the impartiality test. The Riyadh court will annul where the party-appointed arbitrator has an active legal-representation relationship with counsel for the appointing party.
