# Case 012 — Eastern Province: Award Did Not End the Dispute (Eastern Province Court of Appeals, 2017-2021)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** Eastern Province Commercial Court of Appeals
- **Case No.:** Not published with a number in the SCCA 3-Year Study
- **Date of decision:** 2017-2021 (covered in the SCCA's first study, 2017-2020 judgments, re-cited in the 3-Year Study at Section 4.1[a][ii] "Award Did Not End the Dispute")
- **Type:** Nullity action under Article 50(1)(g) — tribunal failed to observe conditions required for the award
- **Outcome:** **FULL ANNULMENT**
- **Source URL (primary):** http://sadr.org/public/upload/media-center/bulletin/SCCA-Saudi-Case-Law-Study-Three-Years-in-Review-En.pdf
- **PDF available?** No standalone PDF.
- **Verification status:** **VERIFIED via SCCA primary source** for the legal principle.

## Parties (anonymized)
- **Claimant in arbitration / plaintiff in nullity:** Award debtor who had been ordered to pay arbitration costs
- **Respondent in arbitration / defendant in nullity:** Award creditor
- Tribunal was constituted by court order (the underlying arbitration agreement apparently did not provide for tribunal constitution)

## The Holding (verbatim from SCCA 3-Year Study)
> "The tribunal issued an award denying jurisdiction due to lack of 'territorial jurisdiction' and ordered the claimant to pay the arbitration costs. The plaintiff (claimant) then filed a motion to annul the award with the Eastern Province Commercial Court of Appeals. The court found that the tribunal violated Articles 40(1) and 41(1), which require that an award must end a dispute. The court cited Article 50(1)(g), under which an award can be annulled if the tribunal fails to observe the conditions required for an award in a manner that affects its substance. Thus, the court granted the plaintiff's motion and annulled the award because the underlying award — finding that the tribunal lacked territorial jurisdiction — did not end the dispute. Interestingly in this case, the tribunal was constituted by court order, and this court noted that it previously dismissed the defendant's challenge to the constitution of the tribunal, which means the defendant had acquiesced to the tribunal's authority to rule on its own jurisdiction."

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** "Award must end a dispute" (المادة 40(1) و41(1)) — the Arabic requirement that an arbitral award must finally dispose of the dispute. A bare jurisdictional dismissal without merits falls short.
2. **The winning concept for the plaintiff.** A tribunal's "jurisdictional dismissal" framed as a final award. The court held that a "we have no jurisdiction" ruling is not a final award under Articles 40(1) and 41(1) because it does not end the dispute.
3. **The linchpin document.** The award itself — specifically, the operative paragraph that disposed of jurisdiction without addressing the underlying dispute.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** The cost order that the jurisdictional dismissal included. Since the award was annulled in full, the cost order fell with it.
5. **The procedural principle.** Even when the underlying arbitration agreement is silent on tribunal constitution (the tribunal was constituted by court order), the defendant who acquiesces cannot then challenge the constitution of the tribunal as a ground for annulment later. The court noted the prior dismissal of the challenge to the tribunal's constitution.
