# Case 010 — Lost Profits as Sharia Challenge Rejected (Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals, 8 August 2022)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals
- **Case No.:** 447044096 of 1444H
- **Date of decision:** 8 August 2022
- **Type:** Nullity action under Article 50(2) — Sharia / public-policy challenge to lost-profits award
- **Outcome:** **ANNULMENT REJECTED** — the court refused to consider the merits of the lost-profits challenge, citing Article 50(4) (no merits review) and noting that lost profits are not *per se* contrary to Sharia.
- **Source URL (primary):** http://sadr.org/public/upload/media-center/bulletin/SCCA-Saudi-Case-Law-Study-Three-Years-in-Review-En.pdf (SCCA 3-Year Case Law Study, Section 3.2 "Lost Profits as Grounds for Violation of Sharia")
- **PDF available?** No standalone PDF; the SCCA primary source is the J. Int'l Arb. article.
- **Verification status:** **VERIFIED via SCCA primary source**.

## Parties (anonymized)
- **Plaintiff in nullity (respondent in arbitration):** Award debtor arguing the tribunal's lost-profits award violated Sharia
- **Defendant in nullity (claimant in arbitration):** Award creditor defending the lost-profits award

## The Holding (verbatim from SCCA 3-Year Study)
> "In a case before the Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals, the plaintiff (respondent) argued, inter alia, that the tribunal violated Sharia principles by awarding lost profits. The court rejected the motion and disregarded this and other arguments reasoning that it requires a de novo review of the award in violation of Article 50(4), which provides that '[t]he competent court shall consider the action for nullification in cases referred to in this Article without inspecting the facts and subject matter of the dispute'. Had the court considered lost profits to be contrary to Sharia, it would have invoked Article 50(2) pertaining to public policy. Indeed, the recently enacted Civil Transactions Law now confirms in its Article 137 that parties may recover lost profits."

## Why this case matches the user's themes
- **Theme #1 (إرباك المشروع / project disruption).** The category of "lost profits" is closely related to "project disruption" claims in construction arbitration. A contractor claiming "إرباك المشروع" is, in damages-language, claiming lost profits (أرباح فائتة) plus extended overhead (تكاليف زمنية). The Riyadh court's 2022 holding — that lost profits are NOT *per se* Sharia-prohibited — is the doctrinal foundation for the user's own "إرباك المشروع" claims.
- **Theme #2 (bank-financing interest).** Same Article 137 framework: lost profits are a heads of loss that can be recovered if proven with certainty.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** "Lost profits" (أرباح فائتة) is recoverable under Sharia if proven. The Riyadh 2022 court was explicit on this. The Article 137 of the 2023 Civil Transactions Law now codifies it.
2. **The winning concept.** Lost profits, properly claimed and proven, are not ribā and not gharar. They are a heads of loss under the law of damages.
3. **The linchpin document.** The Civil Transactions Law, Article 137 (Royal Decree M/191, 19 June 2023): "parties may recover lost profits." This codification post-dates the case but is the doctrinal anchor the SCCA invokes.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** The Article 50(4) "no merits review" cap. The respondent in arbitration tried to use Sharia as a backdoor merits review; the court refused.
5. **The procedural principle.** "Had the court considered lost profits to be contrary to Sharia, it would have invoked Article 50(2) pertaining to public policy." The court signals the limit: a Sharia argument that requires merits review is foreclosed; a Sharia argument that is purely a question of *output* is allowed.

## Open Questions for Follow-up
- Whether the tribunal's quantification of lost profits survived — the court rejected the nullity, but the underlying quantum of profits was not the subject of the case.
- Whether lost profits were *separately* challenged on the basis that the contractual basis (e.g., a guarantee of minimum returns) was Sharia-prohibited. The SCCA summary suggests the only ground was a generic Sharia challenge.
