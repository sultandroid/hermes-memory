# Case 007 — Truncated Tribunal (Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals, 2022)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals
- **Case No.:** Not published with a number in the SCCA 3-Year Study; identified by date and circuit
- **Date of decision:** 2022 (cited in SCCA 3-Year Study as the first of two 2022 "truncated tribunal" annulments)
- **Type:** Nullity action under Article 50(1)(e) — improper composition of the tribunal
- **Outcome:** **FULL ANNULMENT**
- **Source URL:** http://sadr.org/public/upload/media-center/bulletin/SCCA-Saudi-Case-Law-Study-Three-Years-in-Review-En.pdf
- **PDF available?** No standalone PDF. The case is summarized in the SCCA 3-Year Case Law Study, Section 4.1[b][i] "Truncated Tribunals."
- **Verification status:** **VERIFIED via SCCA primary source**.

## Parties (anonymized)
- Underlying arbitration agreement: a three-member tribunal was agreed.
- One arbitrator resigned during the proceedings; the tribunal continued as a two-member panel and issued an award without appointing a replacement.
- Plaintiff (claimant) sought annulment.

## The Holding (verbatim from SCCA 3-Year Study)
> "In the first one before the Riyadh Commercial Court of Appeals, the plaintiff petitioned the court to annul the award on grounds that it was issued by a two-member tribunal after the third tribunal member resigned and the tribunal did not suspend the proceedings pending appointment of a replacement arbitrator. The underlying arbitration clause provided for a three-member tribunal. The plaintiff cited Article 19, which states: 'If the mandate of an arbitrator expires due to his death, disqualification, dismissal, recusal, disability, or any other reason, a replacement shall be appointed according to the procedures followed in the appointment of the arbitrator whose mandate has expired.' The court annulled the award based on Article 50(1)(e). It referred to Article 19 and Article 6 of the Implementing Regulations to the Arbitration Law which provides that '[i]f an arbitrator's mandate expires in the cases specified in Article 19 of the Law, except where such mandate expires due to his disqualification, the arbitration proceedings shall be stayed until a replacement is appointed in accordance with the Law'. The court also cited Article 13, which states: '[t]he Arbitral Tribunal shall be composed of a sole arbitrator or more, provided that their number is odd, or otherwise the arbitration shall be null and void'. Accordingly, the court found that the arbitration proceedings violated the parties' agreement and the law. It also implied that the violation of Article 13 is a matter of public policy implicating Article 50(2)."

## Why this case matches the user's themes
- **Theme #4 (Saudi nullity petition cases 2020-2025).** A leading example of structural nullity grounds that have nothing to do with Sharia — these are the most common annulment grounds actually granted in the 2017-2025 dataset.
- **Theme #5 (partial nullity).** The companion case from Almadina Almunawwarah is the partial-annulment counterpart (separately summarized in the SCCA 3-Year Study); the Riyadh case is full annulment because the defect went to the integrity of the process.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** A "truncated tribunal" is not a colloquial term; it is a category of nullity under Article 50(1)(e) plus the Implementing Regulations Article 6. Drafters cannot allow an arbitrator to drop out without replacement.
2. **The winning concept for the plaintiff:** "tribunal composition ... carried out in a manner violating this Law or the agreement of the parties." This is the exact text of Article 50(1)(e).
3. **The linchpin document.** The original arbitration agreement specifying a three-member tribunal.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** Any award issued by an even-numbered tribunal (i.e., two arbitrators) when the agreement required three. Article 13 of the SAL is categorical: "the Arbitral Tribunal shall be composed of a sole arbitrator or more, provided that their number is odd, or otherwise the arbitration shall be null and void."
5. **The procedural principle.** Article 6 of the Implementing Regulations requires the proceedings to be *stayed* until a replacement is appointed; failure to stay is a violation, and the resulting award is annulled in full. This is structural, not curable.
