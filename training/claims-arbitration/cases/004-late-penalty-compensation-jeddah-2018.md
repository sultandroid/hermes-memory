# Case 004 — "Late Penalty Compensation" as Ribā (Jeddah Commercial Court of Appeals, 5 December 2018)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** First Commercial Circuit of the Makkah Province Court of Appeals (Jeddah)
- **Case No.:** 1491 of 1439H (4-digit Hijri year)
- **Date of decision:** 5 December 2018
- **Type:** Nullity action — court raises Sharia ground sua sponte under Article 50(2)
- **Outcome:** **PARTIAL ANNULMENT** — the court annulled the late-penalty paragraph of the award; enforced the remainder.
- **Source URL:** http://sadr.org/public/upload/media-center/bulletin/SCCA-Saudi-Case-Law-Study-Three-Years-in-Review-En.pdf (SCCA 3-Year Case Law Study, Section 3.3 "Late Payment Charges as a Form of Interest Are Usury")
- **PDF available?** The full Arabic judgment is **NOT** separately published as a PDF on SADR at the indexing key we tried. The SCCA 3-Year Study (open-access, Kluwer Journal of International Arbitration 41(6) 2024) is the primary published source.
- **Verification status:** **VERIFIED via SCCA primary source**. As with case 003, the verbatim Arabic wording has not been confirmed from a standalone PDF; what is verified is the SCCA's published characterization.

## Parties (anonymized)
- **Plaintiff in nullity / respondent in arbitration:** Argued multiple procedural Article 50 grounds, all rejected.
- **Defendant in nullity / claimant in arbitration:** Defended the award, arguing the late-penalty compensation was within liquidated damages permitted by the Council of Senior Scholars.

## The Holding (verbatim from SCCA 3-Year Study)
> "However, the award provided, among other things, for a 'late penalty compensation', which elicited an inquiry from the court on its own initiative per Article 50(2), without being prompted by either party. In response, the plaintiff explained that it is an increase in debt without consideration, and thus it is prohibited usury under Sharia. The defendant responded that such late penalty compensation was in line with what the Council of Senior Scholars approved and the rules allowing for liquidated damages. **The court nonetheless found that the late penalty compensation was not justified in the reasoning or elsewhere in the award, concluding that the tribunal awarded an amount against delay in fulfilment of a debt, which is considered prohibited usury under Sharia. Thus, the court annulled the award in part, and ordered the enforcement of the remainder of the award.**"

## The legal principle the SCCA extracted
- The court distinguished between (a) **liquidated damages (الشرط الجزائي)** — enforceable and Sharia-compliant under Islamic Fiqh Council guidance — and (b) **a "late penalty compensation" tied only to the mere delay of debt payment, with no further obligations attached**. The latter is ribā.
- "Tribunals can identify amounts that Saudi courts consider are Sharia-prohibited interest, which are essentially amounts ordered purely against a delay in fulfilling a debt and with no further obligations attached to them. Otherwise, Saudi courts have been shown to draw clear conclusions between such 'pure' interest and other payment charges or liquidated damages which are indeed enforceable."

## Why this case matches the user's themes
- **Theme #1 (إرباك المشروع / project disruption):** The case is the "negative space" counterpart to Case 003. It shows how a Saudi court will strike a sum framed as compensation for delay, when the sum is not tied to a concrete incurred cost (e.g., 871 days of documented disruption) but only to the running of the clock on a debt.
- **Theme #2 (bank-financing interest / actual cost):** The case is the leading Sharia ribā authority on the late-payment-charge point. Drafters who want to insulate their contract from nullity should structure any "delay charge" either as a liquidated-damages clause (Islamic Fiqh Council resolution no. 109, approved 2000) or as actual cost / time-related cost (Case 003 framing), but never as a pure increment on the unpaid debt.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** "Late penalty compensation" attached to a debt = ribā. "Liquidated damages" with a contractual cap and a heads-of-loss formula = enforceable. "Time-related cost" tied to documented delay = enforceable. The label determines the Sharia outcome.
2. **The winning concept (here, the losing concept).** "An amount against delay in fulfilment of a debt, which is considered prohibited usury" — this is the verbatim Sharia finding. Without a tied obligation, there is no Sharia-compliant route to compensation for mere delay of payment.
3. **The linchpin document.** The award's reasoning itself. The court said the late penalty "was not justified in the reasoning or elsewhere in the award." When the tribunal does not articulate the basis of the entitlement, the court can — and will — intervene under Article 50(2) sua sponte.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** Any sum framed as compensation for the mere passage of time on an unpaid debt. The court will annull in part and enforce the rest.
5. **The procedural principle.** Article 50(2) is an **ex officio** ground — the court raised it without prompting. Drafters cannot assume the tribunal's framing will be challenged; the court will scrutinize the substance.

## Quote (verbatim, from SCCA primary source)
> "The late penalty awarded in this case was distinguishable from liquidated damages clauses, which Saudi courts have long held as enforceable and compliant with Sharia according to the Islamic Fiqh Council's guidance. Moreover, the Civil Transactions Law now confirms the enforceability of liquidated damages clauses. From this judgment, and in contrast with previous ones, tribunals can identify amounts that Saudi courts consider are Sharia-prohibited interest, which are essentially amounts ordered purely against a delay in fulfilling a debt and with no further obligations attached to them. Otherwise, Saudi courts have been shown to draw clear conclusions between such 'pure' interest and other payment charges or liquidated damages which are indeed enforceable."

## Open Questions for Follow-up
- Was the "late penalty compensation" in the underlying award phrased as a percentage of the debt, or as a fixed amount? The SCCA summary does not specify the formula.
- Whether Islamic Fiqh Council resolution no. 109 was in fact cited by the tribunal; if not, the same clause could have been saved.
- Whether the surviving portion of the award contained the contractor's time-related cost claim (Case 003 framing). If so, this is a textbook example of the Jeddah 2018 court accepting time-related cost but striking late-payment charge in the same award.
