# Case 003 — Time-Related Costs vs. Sharia-Interest (Jeddah Commercial Court of Appeals, 4 October 2022)

## Quick Facts
- **Forum:** First Commercial Circuit of the Makkah Province Court of Appeals (Jeddah)
- **Case No.:** 4430103807 of 1444H
- **Date of decision:** 4 October 2022
- **Type:** Nullity action — Sharia / public-policy challenge against arbitral award
- **Outcome:** **ANNULMENT REJECTED** on the Sharia ground; award upheld. The court accepted the respondent's framing that the awarded amounts were **"time-related costs" / "indirect administrative expenses"**, not prohibited interest.
- **Source URL (primary):** http://sadr.org/public/upload/media-center/bulletin/SCCA-Saudi-Case-Law-Study-Three-Years-in-Review-En.pdf
- **Source URL (law-firm digest):** https://www.linkedin.com (multiple law-firm notes) and the SCCA "Three Years in Review" article (Journal of International Arbitration 41(6), 2024, pp. 723-744)
- **PDF available?** The original judgment text is **not separately published as a standalone PDF**; it is summarized in the SCCA 3-Year Case Law Study (open-access PDF). Case 4430103807 is referenced in the article as the "time-related costs do not violate Sharia" leading case.
- **Verification status:** **VERIFIED via SCCA primary source** (Sahab, Dara, "SCCA Saudi Case Law Study: Three Years in Review," J. Int'l Arb. 41(6), 2024). The full judgment text is not yet on the SADR public judgments portal at the indexing-key we tried.

## Parties (anonymized)
- **Respondent in arbitration / plaintiff in nullity:** Contractor party that received the award and sought to *uphold* it (a respondent in nullity is the party that filed the nullity action to overturn; here, the plaintiff was the *respondent* in arbitration trying to strike down the award — so the procedural posture is reversed, which is what makes this case interesting)
- **Claimant in arbitration / defendant in nullity:** The other party to the construction/contract dispute

## The Holding (verbatim from SCCA 3-Year Study)
> "In a case before the Jeddah Commercial Court of Appeals, the plaintiff (respondent) petitioned the court to annul the award on grounds, inter alia, that the arbitral tribunal awarded Sharia-prohibited interest. The court rejected the 'interest' claim and clarified that the amounts awarded were time-related costs, particularly defined as **indirect administrative expenses, for 871 days of delay for which the plaintiff was responsible. Thus, these amounts were not Sharia-prohibited interest.**"

## What the SCCA identified as the legal principle
- Saudi courts draw a bright line between **(a) time-related / overhead costs for delay (compensable as actual cost) and (b) "pure" interest on late payment of a debt (prohibited ribā)**.
- The framing matters: when the tribunal quantifies the award as "indirect administrative expenses for X days of delay," Saudi courts treat it as actual cost compensation, not as ribā.
- The 871-day delay here is a **concrete factual anchor** — the award was tied to a specific duration of disruption, not to a percentage of the debt.

## Why this case matches the user's themes
- **Theme #1 (إرباك المشروع / project disruption):** This is the closest Saudi published case to the "project disruption" / "time-related cost" concept the user wants to find. Although the published summary uses the English term "time-related costs" / "indirect administrative expenses" (not the Arabic "إرباك المشروع"), the substantive concept is identical: it is a contractor's cost arising from the employer's delay, framed as overhead absorption rather than as a financial abstraction.
- **Theme #2 (bank-financing interest / actual cost):** The case is one side of a coin with the same Jeddah 4 Oct 2022 case that ALSO ruled a "late penalty compensation" was ribā (see Case 004 in this research batch). The Jeddah Commercial Court of Appeals in 2022 is therefore where Saudi courts drew the operative line between compensable time-related costs and prohibited late-payment interest.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline.** Call the cost what it is: "time-related costs / indirect administrative expenses" (تكاليف زمنية / مصاريف إدارية غير مباشرة) — NOT "interest" (فوائد), NOT "late payment penalty" (غرامة تأخير), and NOT "compensation for delay of debt" (تعويض عن تأخير الوفاء بالدين). The choice of noun changes the Sharia outcome.
2. **The winning concept.** "Time-related costs" (a contractor-overhead absorption concept) survives Sharia review; "late payment interest" does not.
3. **The linchpin document.** The expert / tribunal quantification of the delay — here, **871 days for which the plaintiff was responsible**. Without that factual hook (a number, a duration, a documented cause), the same amount could be recharacterized as ribā.
4. **The cap that gets struck.** Pure ribā — any sum calculated purely on the late payment of a debt, with no tied obligation. See Case 004 for the Jeddah 2018 case where the same court struck a "late penalty compensation" on exactly that basis.
5. **The procedural principle.** The court will go behind the label the tribunal puts on the sum to determine its true nature: "these amounts were not Sharia-prohibited interest" because they were tied to documented delay.
