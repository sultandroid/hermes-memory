## Parties
- **Claimant (in arbitration; plaintiff in nullity action):** Arab individual claimant, sub-tenant under the tripartite contract (names redacted by SCCA)
- **First respondent (defendant 1):** Saudi national, original owner/lessor (the "first party" in the lease)
- **Second respondent (defendant 2):** Saudi national, prior tenant replaced by the claimant under the 2016 tripartite contract
- **Tribunal:** Five-member arbitral tribunal — presiding arbitrator, umpire, and three co-arbitrators (all named in the redacted original)
- **Counsel:** Plaintiff and first defendant represented by attorneys; second defendant failed to appear despite personal notification

## Underlying Contract
- A tripartite agreement styled as a "Real Property Lease Contract" dated 8/2/1438H (November 8, 2016), by which the second respondent was replaced as tenant by the claimant, with the first respondent as lessor.
- Expressly contained **Clause 9**: a contractual precondition requiring prior attempts at amicable settlement before initiating arbitration.
- The contract is a sub-lease arrangement; the principal lease between the first respondent and the second respondent (dated 7/6/1434H) allowed subleasing only with the owner's consent.
- No party addressed the determination of arbitrator fees or the arbitration procedure in writing.

## Dispute Trigger
- The claimant filed for arbitration seeking termination of the lease and restoration to the pre-contractual position; the contract had been partly performed (rent paid, possession transferred) but relations had broken down.
- General Court proceedings on related eviction matters were also in progress, but did not join all parties as against one another.

## The Award — Operative Paragraphs
The arbitral tribunal, by majority, ruled as follows:
- **Operative paragraph 1 (annulled in part):** "Based on the foregoing reasons, the tribunal, by majority, holds that the contract, concluded on November 8, 2016 (8/2/1438H) between the claimant […], a Saudi national, holder of National ID No. […]; the first respondent […], a Saudi national, holder of National ID No. […]; and the second respondent […], a Saudi national, holder of National ID No. […], is null and void."
- **Operative paragraph 2 (UPHELD):** Dismissal of the claimant's claims and requests against the first respondent and his discharge from the case.
- **Operative paragraph 3 (UPHELD):** Order that the second respondent pay the claimant SAR 121,667.
- **Operative paragraph 4 (UPHELD):** Dismissal of the remaining requests.
- The award also made the operative determination on the parties' "loser-pays" agreement re arbitration costs (Clause 3 of the parties' agreement, as found by the court: the losing party should bear the arbitration costs as determined by the tribunal).

## The Nullity Action — Grounds Raised
The plaintiff (claimant in the arbitration) raised **fourteen substantive grounds and two procedural grounds**. The court found fourteen of them to be matters of fact or merits outside the scope of Article 50(4) review. Only the **Sixth substantive ground** succeeded:
- **Sixth ground:** The arbitral award, in its preamble, ruled that the tripartite contract was null and void — but nullity had not been requested by either party (the claimant had asked for termination, not nullity). The court held this violated Article 200(1)(d) of the Law of Civil Procedure ("If the judgment awards what the litigants did not ask for or more than what they asked for") and constituted grounds for partial annulment under Article 50(1)(f) of the Arbitration Law (ruling on matters not included in the arbitration agreement).

The court separately addressed the formal Article 42(2) complaints (omission of arbitrators' addresses, nationalities, fees and costs allocation) and rejected them as going beyond the annulment grounds.

## Court Ruling on Nullity — Operative Part
> "The Circuit hereby rules as follows:
> First: The annulment action is admissible in form.
> Second: On the merits:
> (1) The annulment action is dismissed with respect to the following paragraphs of the arbitral award […]. The Circuit further rules to uphold the arbitral award in these paragraphs and orders its enforcement.
> (2) The following part of the arbitral award is declared null and void: 'Based on the foregoing reasons, the tribunal, by majority, holds that the contract, concluded on November 8, 2016 (8/2/1438H) between the claimant […] and the respondents […] and […], is null and void.'
> This judgment of the Court of Appeal, insofar as it dismisses the annulment action with respect to the aforementioned paragraphs, is final and not subject to appeal by any method of challenge.
> The ruling of nullity set out in paragraph 2 of the 'Second' ruling above is subject to objection before the competent court within 30 days from the date of its issuance, pursuant to Article 55 (3) of the Arbitration Law."

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline (the principle).** Saudi courts treat Article 50(4) as a strict "no merits review" rule. Allegations of contractual mischaracterization, breach of Article 38(1)(c) (the tribunal to take into account the terms of the contract), or violation of amicable-settlement preconditions are NOT grounds for annulment — they require re-examining the facts. Only Article 50(1)(f) (exceeding scope) gave this claimant a foothold.
2. **The winning concept for partial annulment here was "excess of relief not requested."** Where a claimant asks for termination and the tribunal sua sponte grants nullity, the court will strike the extra paragraph. This is the cleanest path to a partial annulment that preserves the rest of the award.
3. **The linchpin document was the General Court eviction judgment** (which did not establish nullity and did not join all parties). It demonstrated the tribunal's nullity ruling was unsupported and not a "necessary legal consequence" of rejecting termination.
4. **The cap that gets struck: relief beyond the parties' requests** — Article 200(1)(d) of the Law of Civil Procedure, read with Article 50(1)(f) of the Arbitration Law. Drafters should ensure relief claims align precisely with what is sought; tribunals must not grant more.
5. **The procedural principle:** "Since the legal effects of nullity differ from the effects of rescission, termination, or annulment, and since such relief had not been requested by the parties, it cannot be argued that rejection of the claimant's request for termination necessarily entails a ruling of nullity." Direct, verbatim from the judgment.

## Quote (verbatim, English as published by SCCA)
> "Such partial annulment is inconsistent with the nature of the annulment action, as the plaintiff has no legal interest in this regard given that the ruling benefits him."
> "Since the legal effects of nullity differ from the effects of rescission, termination, or annulment, and since such relief had not been requested by the parties, it cannot be argued that rejection of the claimant's request for termination necessarily entails a ruling of nullity. The legal position is confined either to continuation of the contract as valid and effective or to its nullity, and thus nullity constitutes relief exceeding the parties' requests in the arbitration proceedings."
