# Case 06 — CII RT-034: Constructability Is a Programme, Not a Pre-Construction Review

## Citation
- **Title:** "Project-Level Model and Approaches to Implement Constructability" (CII Research Report RT-034, Academic Document)
- **Author:** Construction Industry Institute (CII), University of Texas at Austin — Research Team RT-034
- **Year:** October 1992 (Publication Date); questionnaire survey of 262 organisations; 62 in-depth interviews
- **URL:** https://www.construction-institute.org/project-level-model-and-approaches-to-implement-constructability/
- **Verification status:** VERIFIED — CII Research Team RT-034 publication page extracted via Tavily on 31 July 2026; the 224-page academic document is the foundational constructability reference in the industry.

## Quick Facts
- **Forum / doc type:** CII Research Team academic report (industry-funded, peer-reviewed; the canonical constructability reference)
- **Subject:** Survey + interview-based research on how construction-knowledge input is integrated into the design process across 262 organisations
- **Outcome for design managers:** The foundational benchmark that distinguishes *ad hoc late-stage design review* from a *formal constructability programme* integrated across all project phases

## Theme Mapped
**8. Constructability reviews** (this is *the* primary case file for that theme) + **1. RIBA Plan of Work** (the CII constructability concept is the operational mechanism for delivering the RIBA Information Exchanges)

## Direct Quote
> "Implementing construction knowledge during the design of a project can be an effective tool in achieving project success in regards to cost and schedule. A constructability program that strives to create an interdependence between the designer and contractor at all phases of the facility delivery process while also recognizing schedule constraints and new construction technologies could produce benefits well beyond the cost of the additional effort."

> "However, many problems stand in the way of creating a constructability program. Oftentimes, projects are conceived with the construction phase completely separate from the rest of the facility delivery process. The possible benefits are many times ignored because they may be difficult to quantify in order to justify the cost of the program. Also, various ideas regarding the scope of constructability among the construction industry can create misconceptions about the extent of the benefits."

> "The level of a constructability program, if any, within an organization depends on their perception of the breadth of the program. Information collected through questionnaires and interviews show these perceptions can range from **design reviews just prior to release of construction documents to formal constructability programs with extensive documentation of costs and benefits**. This shows that while many organizations may feel they are implementing constructability, in fact their efforts are too late in the facility delivery process to achieve significant benefits."

## Summary (2–3 sentences)
CII RT-034 is the *canonical* constructability reference, and the message that has survived 30+ years of subsequent practice is unambiguous: there is a spectrum from "design reviews just prior to release of construction documents" (which is what most projects actually do) to "formal constructability programmes with extensive documentation of costs and benefits" (which is what high-performing projects do), and only the *latter* produces significant benefits. The report's seven constructability concepts and eight implementation approaches are still the basis of the CII Constructability Implementation Guide and the ASCE CI Construction Glossary. For a museum Tech Office, the practical translation is that a *one-off* pre-IFC constructability review is a check-the-box exercise; a *programme* that runs concept → detailed design → procurement → construction constructability reviews is the only intervention that has measurable cost/schedule impact.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** *Constructability* (قابلية البناء) is *not* a synonym for "buildability review" — it is a structured programme per CII: "the optimum integration of construction knowledge and experience in planning, engineering, procurement, and field operations to achieve overall project objectives." The keywords are *integration* and *programme*, not *review*.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The trigger for value is the *timing* of the constructability input — late (post-IFC) input catches obvious clashes but cannot change the fundamental design decisions; early (concept-stage) input shapes the design for buildability. The Tech Office should log which *phase* each constructability comment was raised in, and which *phase* it was closed in; the gap is the value.
3. **Procedural lesson:** Adopt the CII "eight general approaches" hierarchy. The weakest is *ad hoc* design review; the strongest is a *formal programme with cost/benefit documentation*. The first action is to write a *Constructability Implementation Plan* (CIP) at project mobilisation that names the constructability manager, the review cadence, the documentation standard, and the cost/schedule tracking mechanism.
4. **Strategic lesson:** A museum project has constructability hotspots that the CII general survey does not specifically cover — exhibition fit-out, gallery HVAC zoning, high-finish concrete, custom joinery, art-handling logistics, vibration-sensitive audio-visual. The Tech Office should add a *museum-specific* constructability overlay to the CII base programme, with named reviewers for each hotspot.
5. **The principle:** *"Many organizations may feel they are implementing constructability, in fact their efforts are too late in the facility delivery process to achieve significant benefits."* Self-assessment trap: if the only constructability touchpoint is a pre-IFC review, the project is *not* running a constructability programme — it is running a sanity check. The label does not match the work.

## Open Questions for Follow-up
- Does the Aseer Museum (or comparable Saudi giga-project) have a *written* Constructability Implementation Plan, or is the constructability review being run as an ad hoc process by the PMC?
- Are the contractor's proposed construction-method statements (CMS) being captured as *constructability input* back into the design, or being treated as post-design execution documents? The former is a constructability programme; the latter is procurement paperwork.
- The CII RT-034 was published in 1992; the CII Best Practices Guide has been updated several times since. What is the *current* (2024 or 2025) CII position on constructability in BIM-mandated projects?
