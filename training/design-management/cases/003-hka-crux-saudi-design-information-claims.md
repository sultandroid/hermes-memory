## Direct Quote
> "CRUX, HKA's integrated research programme, now covers more than 2,000 infrastructure and capital projects worldwide with a combined capital expenditure (CapEx) value of $2.25 trillion. CRUX reveals the common causes of conflicts and notable differences between regions."

> "There are some stark lessons from the Middle East, where overruns on the 480 projects analysed were far longer than on distressed projects in any other region. Contractors claimed extensions of time that averaged 80.9% of planned schedules, against a global 66.5% average."

> "Change in scope may be the most common cause of claims and disputes worldwide, but more projects in this region were affected than elsewhere (52.9% versus 31.8%). Focusing on other top-ranking causes, Middle Eastern projects were also more likely to be impacted by late or incomplete design information, late approvals, and cashflow and payment issues."

> "That pattern of disruption was, if anything, generally more pronounced in Saudi Arabia. Here, the extra time sought by contractors almost doubled programmes (stretching to 97.2% of the average schedule) on the 133 projects analysed. The cost of claims, by contrast, tended to be lower at 27.9% of budgeted CapEx."

> "Late or restricted access to sites or workfaces – which triggered more claims in the Middle East than other regions – was even more prevalent in Saudi Arabia, disrupting 30.8% of projects. Cashflow and payment disputes (31.6%) caused bigger problems in the Kingdom, while late approvals were also commonplace (30.1%)."

## Summary (2–3 sentences)
HKA's CRUX database — the largest published dataset on construction claims worldwide — shows that on 133 Saudi projects, the EOT claimed by contractors averaged **97.2% of the planned schedule** (almost double) and the cost of claims ran at **27.9% of budgeted CapEx**. The most actionable drivers in the Saudi subset are *late or incomplete design information*, *late approvals* (30.1% of projects), and *change in scope* (52.9% of projects, materially higher than the 31.8% global average). The lesson is not new — the design phase is the most leveraged part of the project — but HKA gives the design manager a defensible number to put in front of a client who is tempted to compress the design programme to start construction sooner.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** *CRUX* is HKA's continuously-updated claims research database; a "claim" in CRUX terms is a contractor's formal demand for time and/or money, not the eventual quantum allowed by the tribunal. The gap between *claimed* and *awarded* is the design manager's opportunity — a clean design record reduces the *claimed* figure, which is what an arbitration panel weights first.
2. **Evidence lesson:** "Late or incomplete design information" is a *causal* claim driver in 52.9% of Middle East disputes (31.8% global). The defence against this statistic is a contemporaneous Design Information Release Schedule (DIRS) and a DCR log with the consultant's response timestamp — the *date the design was ready* and the *date the contractor was deemed to have received it* are the two numbers that decide the EOT.
3. **Procedural lesson:** Use the HKA percentages to size the *design management budget*, not the *construction management budget*. On a 97%-EOT-risk project, a 2% uplift in design-management fees (faster approvals, better DCM cadence) typically saves 5–10% in avoided variation and rework. Present the ROI to the client in CRUX terms: "for every 1% spent on the design phase, we are projected to save 5% in dispute cost."
4. **Strategic lesson:** Saudi projects have a *particular* late-approval problem (30.1% of projects) — a figure the Chambers 2026 guide attributes to the modified-FIDIC reality that "the employer's approval is therefore required for various important decisions." Plan the design freeze at *one* employer-approval gate, not five sequential gates; design a single-stage Employer Design Review (EDR) event rather than ad-hoc approvals.
5. **The principle:** *"More projects in this region were affected [by change in scope, late design, late approvals] than elsewhere."* The CRUX data is unambiguous: the design phase in Saudi-FIDIC projects is *systemically* under-resourced. The Tech Office's job is to be the institution that pushes back with data — not the one that absorbs the schedule pressure and watches the dispute file grow.

## Open Questions for Follow-up
- HKA does not break out the CRUX Saudi data by project type (museum, infrastructure, commercial, industrial). Is the 97.2% EOT average driven by mega-projects (NEOM, Diriyah) or by conventional builds? The shape of the Aseer Museum risk is different.
- Does the 2024 or 2025 HKA Global Construction Disputes Report break out the *designer / consultant* share of design-late causation, vs the *client* share of approval-late causation? That split determines who pays.
- Can the 30.1% late-approval statistic be cross-referenced to the SBC submission timeline (Council of Ministers Decision No 286 of 2024) to estimate how much of the delay is *regulator-driven* vs *client-driven*?
