# Case 01 — RIBA Plan of Work 2020: Design Information Exchanges and the Stage-Gate that Locks the Design

- **Year:** 2020 (Plan of Work) / ongoing NBS updates through 2024

## Quick Facts
- **Forum / doc type:** Professional body framework (RIBA) + specifier knowledge base (NBS)
- **Subject:** Eight-stage process map for building design and construction (Stages 0–7), with explicit information-exchange taskbars and a Design Responsibility Matrix
- **Outcome for design managers:** A single shared reference grid that locks *who* delivers *what* at *which* gate — the contractual lever for design freeze, IFC release, and design responsibility disputes

## Summary (2–3 sentences)
RIBA's 2020 Plan of Work organises building delivery into eight numbered stages (0 Strategic Definition → 7 In Use) and, critically for design management, attaches an **Information Exchanges** taskbar to every stage. NBS's companion article explains that the *Responsibility Matrix* and *Information Requirements* delivered at the end of Stage 1 are "critical to the success of the project" — they are the contractual mechanism by which a multi-firm design team (architect, structural, MEP, landscape, exhibition, museum specialist) agrees who produces which deliverable at which LOD/LOI. For a Saudi-delivered museum project (e.g. Aseer, where NRS is design lead), the practical translation is: align the consultant appointment, the Saudi Building Code (SBC) submission gate, and the FIDIC design liability cut-offs to a RIBA-anchored stage map, then lock Stage 4 (Technical Design) as the formal "design freeze" milestone.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** *Stage 4 — Technical Design* (المرحلة 4 — التصميم الفني) is the *technical design submittal* (TDS) gate in RIBA terms; downstream of Stage 4, only changes processed through a formal change-control mechanism should be permitted. LOD (Level of Detail) and LOI (Level of Information) are *separate* axes on the same Responsibility Matrix cell.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The Information Exchanges taskbar turns a vague "design is complete" claim into a checklist of *deliverables produced at the end of each stage* — and what evidence is in the Information Requirements file at the end of Stage 1 is what later determines whether the consultant or contractor owned a particular design element.
3. **Procedural lesson:** Adopt the RIBA Plan of Work Toolbox (XLSX) as the *single source of truth* for who-does-what. Without a populated Responsibility Matrix, a multidisciplinary museum team (architect + structural + MEP + exhibition + landscape + lighting + acoustics + wayfinding) has no contractual anchor for design responsibility disputes at handover.
4. **Strategic lesson:** Map the Saudi Building Code (SBC) approval gate and the client's PMC review gate to RIBA Stage 4 (Technical Design) — that is the moment when the design becomes "frozen enough" to anchor a FIDIC Cl. 13.1 Variation Instruction and stop scope creep on the contractor. Before Stage 4 completion, VOs are a design-development cost; after Stage 4 approval, they are a contract-change cost.
5. **The principle:** *"The information produced at the end of a stage then influences the decisions and the work required throughout the next stage."* A design manager who treats RIBA as bureaucratic paperwork rather than a contractual lever has already lost the freeze-dispute argument that will arrive 18 months later.
