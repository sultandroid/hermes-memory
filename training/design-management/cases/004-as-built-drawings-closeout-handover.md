## Direct Quote
> "At the end of a construction project, owners want to feel confident that all work was completed to their satisfaction and that they are set up to assume control of the building's future. This is why the as-built drawings are some of the most important documents owners receive during closeout."

> "As-built drawings are a final set of drawings that capture all changes made during construction and depict the project in its completed state. These drawings are often used to prove contractors fulfilled their contractual obligations and are usually necessary for an owner to manage, renovate or sell their building."

> "As-built drawings depict a building's final construction, including all notable changes made throughout the project. Some construction professionals use the terms 'redline drawings' and 'as-built drawings' interchangeably — but technically there is a difference as the marked-up plans known as redline drawings are used to create the official as-built drawings."

## Summary (2–3 sentences)
Procore's library article draws the operational line between *redline drawings* (the marked-up plans the contractor maintains on site during construction) and *as-built drawings* (the cleaned-up, official set of drawings that captures *all changes made during construction* and depicts the project in its completed state). The as-built set is the document the owner uses for the *next 30 years* of operation, renovation, and sale — and it is the document a consultant's PI insurer asks for first when a latent defect claim arrives. For a Saudi museum project, the as-built also interfaces with the **occupancy certificate** requirement (Council of Ministers Decision No 286 of 9 October 2024, per Chambers Case 02), making late or missing as-builts a hard blocker to energisation and opening.

## 5 Lessons Extracted
1. **Vocabulary lesson:** *As-built* (كما بُني) is the *final* drawing set; *redline* is the *interim* marked-up set the contractor maintains during construction; *record drawings* is a third term sometimes used for the consultant's recompiled version. In a contract, only *as-built* has the legal force of capturing the *constructed reality* — redlines and records are intermediate working documents.
2. **Evidence lesson:** The trigger document for the consultant's design liability close-out is the *accepted* as-built set. A contract that does not specify the format, medium (2D CAD, BIM federated model, IFC file), or acceptance process for the as-built set will produce a dispute at handover — typically the contractor argues the design consultant should re-model from the redlines, the consultant argues the contractor should produce the as-builts.
3. **Procedural lesson:** Build the as-built deliverable as a *continuous* process, not a close-out scramble. Two operational rules: (a) the contractor updates redlines *weekly* during construction, not monthly; (b) the consultant audits the redlines *monthly* against a sampling of the installed works, so discrepancies are caught before they calcify. The as-built is then a recompile, not a reconstruction.
4. **Strategic lesson:** For a museum project, the as-built is the input to the *facility management* (FM) handover — not just a contractual artefact. The Chambers 2026 guide's note on the *occupancy certificate* being a prerequisite for full electrical activation means the as-built set must be in a form that the SBC inspection authority will accept (PDF + IFC + native CAD) *and* a form that the FM team can use. Design these two audiences into the deliverable from the start.
5. **The principle:** *"As-built drawings depict a building's final construction, including all notable changes made throughout the project."* The as-built set is the *only* drawing the building will ever have that reflects *what was actually built*; design intent drawings are aspirational, shop drawings are manufacturing intent, and as-builts are reality. A museum Tech Office that treats the as-built as a back-end chore is signing up for a 30-year liability tail.

## Open Questions for Follow-up
- What is the *statutory* form of the as-built under the SBC implementing regulations? Does it need to be in IFC for BIM-mandated projects (e.g. Aseer-scale museum work) or is a stamped 2D PDF still acceptable?
- How does the consultant's PI policy interact with the as-built delivery date? Most policies extend the limitation period but require the consultant to have *delivered* the as-built — late delivery can void the cover.
- Does the 10-year decennial liability regime (or its NCTL 2023 equivalent — see Case 02) in Saudi Arabia require the as-built set to be *deposited* with MOMAH, or held only by the owner?
