# Procurement — Top Lessons

## From Case 001 (Tamimi — DIFC Performance Bonds) — Bond Forfeiture

1. **Performance bonds are *independent* of the underlying contract.**
   The bond issuer (usually a bank) must pay on demand, without reference to the underlying contract dispute. This is the *autonomy principle* (DIFC law, UAE Federal Law, and the UNCITRAL Model Law all converge here).

2. **The only defence to a call on a performance bond is *fraud* (or similar narrow grounds: duress, unconscionability, improper issuance).**
   The Contractor cannot defend a bond call by saying "we're not actually in default on the contract." The bank pays, then the Contractor sues the Employer for wrongful call.

3. **DIFC and Saudi courts treat performance bonds differently from letters of credit.**
   Performance bonds in GCC are typically *unconditional* (payable on first demand without proof of default). Letters of credit have documentary conditions. **Drafting consequence:** if you want a "conditional" bond, you must expressly say so in the bond text — the default is unconditional.

## From Case 002 (Tamimi — Back-to-Back Clauses in Abu Dhabi Cassation 2018) — Back-to-Back

4. **A back-to-back clause is a contract risk-transfer mechanism, not a contract-substitution.**
   The Contractor remains liable to the Employer for the full scope, even if the Subcontractor fails. The back-to-back clause gives the Contractor a *right of contribution* against the Subcontractor, but does not extinguish the Contractor's primary liability.

5. **Abu Dhabi Court of Cassation 2018: back-to-back clauses are enforceable if (a) the language is clear, (b) the back-to-back chain is traceable, (c) the Subcontractor's liability is not extinguished.**
   The court will not enforce a back-to-back clause that the Contractor drafted to avoid his own obligations.

## Cross-cutting (preliminary — based on 2 cases only)

6. **The procurement file is the second-most-litigated document set in Saudi construction, after the programme.**
   Every procurement decision (PQ, MA, delivery, inspection, back-charge) creates an evidentiary record. The contractor who maintains a clean procurement file wins disputes; the contractor who relies on verbal decisions loses.

7. **Material Approval (MA) is a one-way street: silence is not approval.**
   Under FIDIC, the Engineer's MA is required for all permanent materials. If the Contractor proceeds without MA, the work is *deemed defective* regardless of actual quality.
