# Case 001 — Mecca Grand Mosque Crawler Crane Collapse (11 Sep 2015)

## Quick Facts
- **Themes**: 7 (Temporary works design liability), 8 (Lifting plan / rigging disputes)
- **Year**: 2015 (event 11 Sep), immediate aftermath 2015–2017
- **Contractor**: Saudi Binladin Group (main contractor for Grand Mosque expansion)
- **Equipment**: Liebherr LR 11350 crawler crane (German manufacturer)
- **Casualties**: 111 killed, 394 injured — deadliest crane failure in modern history
- **URL**: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca_crane_collapse
- **Format**: Public-domain case compilation cross-referenced with vendor and government findings

## Summary
On 11 September 2015, a Liebherr LR 11350 crawler crane collapsed into the eastern piazza of the Grand Mosque (Masjid al-Haram) in Mecca during a sandstorm with winds reported above 40 km/h, killing 111 pilgrims and injuring 394. Liebherr's own technical report stated that "the boom of the crane was not sufficiently secured by Binladin to sustain the high winds on the day of the collapse." Saudi Gazette reported that the Emir of Mecca had ordered Binladin **eleven days before the collapse** to relocate cranes from pedestrian areas and deploy safeguards — an instruction that was not executed. The case is a textbook example of how temporary works design choices (free-standing crawler with no slewing-brake / wind-gust protocols documented) and lifting-plan risk allocation (hiring-and-use under CPC, contractor's site-management liability) collide when the engineer's instruction is neither complied with nor escalated.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline** — distinguish *"instruction to deploy safeguards"* (Employer's verbal/written direction requiring compliance) from *"Contractor's method of working"* (criterion of fitness, sole contractor risk). The Mecca 11-day-prior order is the textbook "Employer's instruction" a contractor cannot silently override.
2. **The linchpin evidence** — the **vendor's own post-incident report** (Liebherr) is the single most decisive document: an equipment vendor has no incentive to blame its own product, so its finding that "the boom was not sufficiently secured" is treated by insurers and prosecutors as near-irrefutable. Treat vendor incident reports as gold-standard evidence; obtain and preserve them on day one.
3. **The procedural principle** — "non-execution of an instruction is itself a variation/breach, regardless of whether the contractor agrees with the instruction." Under FIDIC Red/Yellow 1999/2017 Sub-Clause 3.3, the contractor must comply with instructions **and** give a Notice if it considers them to constitute a Variation. Silence is breach.
4. **The temporary works lesson** — crawler cranes in operational mode are **temporary works**. The design of out-of-service restraint (slewing brake, weather-vane, drop-pin anchorage) sits with the Contractor's Temporary Works Coordinator under BS 5975 cascade. A wind-speed operating envelope (e.g., shut-down at 72 km/h, free-slew at 60–72 km/h) must be in writing and signed off by the lift planner.
5. **The principle with wider application** — *"Lifting plan responsibility cannot be delegated away by hiring."* Under HSE UK (LOLER 1998, ACOP L113) and equivalent Saudi MoL practice, the "user" (the contractor in operational control of the crane — not the hire company) carries planning and supervision liability unless it explicitly contracts a "contract lift". The lesson generalises to every plant-hire interface on a Saudi/GCC site.

## How to use this in a construction-management playbook
Use as the *opening case* in any site-management induction: it is the only GCC fatality case where (a) the vendor published a public technical finding adverse to the contractor and (b) there is a documented prior Employer instruction that was not executed. Pair it with the HSE UK Lifting ACOP (Case 005) for the planning template and with the FIDIC Cl. 3 determinative framework (Case 002) for what to do when an instruction is given late or appears impractical.
