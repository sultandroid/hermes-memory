## Summary
The Sixth Edition of the **GAR Guide to Construction Arbitration** is the principal international practitioner reference for the *evidentiary framework* of construction arbitration. The *Documents and other types of evidence in construction disputes* chapter establishes the canonical five-record taxonomy: (i) **programme records** (baseline, updated, revised and as-built); (ii) **progress records** (daily reports, site diaries, inspection reports); (iii) **technical documentation** (engineering reports, photographs, non-conformance reports); (iv) **correspondence** (emails, memos, meeting minutes); and (v) **commercial/financial records** (payment applications, invoices, variation orders). The chapter confirms that **contemporaneous records are essential for tracking progress and resolving disputes** and cites the Scottish case *Fes Limited v. HFD Construction Group Limited* [2024] CSOH 20 for the principle that **"such notification permitted contemporaneous investigation and the early ascertainment of the [delaying event] . . . and its effect. This, in turn, enabled timely and orderly settlement of any claims by the contractor."** Crucially, the chapter catalogues the documents required to support three classes of construction dispute most commonly seen in GCC arbitration: **variation disputes** (where the *order to vary the works* in writing — letter, email, or response to submittal — is the threshold document, with oral orders to be confirmed in correspondence confirming the order), **delay disputes** (where the contemporaneous daily record and the programme narrative carry the heaviest weight), and **defect disputes** (where the *detailed notes and photographs* from regular inspections are the primary record).

It is therefore unsurprising that respondents in a 2019 survey identified the two most defining features of international construction arbitration as the 'factual and technical complexity' (73 per cent) and the 'large amounts of evidence involved' (66 per cent)."

> "It is also important for the parties to keep detailed records of all project-related activities. These records include baseline programmes and schedules, correspondence (emails, memos and meeting minutes), site-specific records (site diaries and progress reports) and technical documentation (inspection reports, engineering reports and photographs). These records are essential for tracking progress and resolving disputes."

> "Programme records: programme records include the contractor's baseline programme, updated programmes recording progress, revised programmes to take account of re-sequencing or other acceleration measures and records that assist in understanding these programmes, such as programme narratives. These records, if accurately and properly maintained, enable the claimant party to retrospectively map out the as-planned and as-built critical path of a project."

> "Order to vary the works: the contractor will have to establish that the employer (or the contract administrator or engineer) issued an order to vary the works. This order will typically be issued in writing, such as in a letter, email or in response to a submittal; however, if the order is issued orally, the contractor should ensure that the order is properly documented and recorded in writing (i.e., through meeting minutes or in correspondence confirming the order)."

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline** — distinguish *"programme narrative"* (a contemporaneous written statement explaining what the planner meant by an updated bar chart) from *"programme"* (the bar chart itself) from *"as-built programme"* (the bar chart of what actually happened). Tribunals treat the *narrative* as the contemporaneous record; treat the *bar chart* as the illustrative product. Without the narrative, the bar chart is silent when the dispute crystallises.
2. **The linchpin evidence** — the **contemporaneous daily record** combined with the **email confirmation of oral instructions**. *Fes v. HFD* is authority that **a contemporaneous notification channel** is the trigger for the contractor's whole claim environment. The minimum-daily-deliverable set: site diary entry, weather record, plant-and-labour return, photograph of the workface, and an end-of-day email to the Engineer. Skip any one and the gap is exploitable on cross.
3. **The procedural principle** — *"the order to vary, the order to suspend, the order to change the method of working — all must be in writing, or confirmed in writing if oral."* Under both the 1999 and 2017 FIDIC Red and Yellow Books, oral instructions are valid for compliance but a contemporaneous email or letter confirming the oral order is the **evidence threshold** for the cost claim. The contract-admin reflex (engineer's office, not the contractor's site) must confirm all oral instructions in writing within 7 days.
4. **The strategic lesson for the snag-list / punch-list moment** — the **inspection-and-test records at the taking-over stage** are part of the same evidentiary family. The contractor's project record on the day the Engineer takes possession (FIDIC SC 10.1) is what determines whether the snag list is a *deliverable* (complete and remedied) or a *liability* (deferred defects). Build the taking-over record to be reviewed as if it were a court exhibit — *before* it goes to the Engineer.
5. **The principle with wider application** — *"what is not in the contemporaneous record does not exist."* This is the unifying principle across variation disputes, delay disputes, defect disputes, and snag-list disputes. The contractor (and the engineer, and the employer) who maintain a contemporaneous record *consistently through the project* — including the "no-incident" days — have the strongest position across every subsequent dispute category. The discipline is the same for all of them.

## How to use this in a construction-management playbook
Adopt the GAR 6th Edition five-record taxonomy as the **file-naming convention** on every site: `01_PROGRAMME/`, `02_PROGRESS/`, `03_TECHNICAL/`, `04_CORRESPONDENCE/`, `05_COMMERCIAL/`. Train the Site Engineer to update all five daily, with mandatory email confirmation of all oral instructions. The discipline at handover (practical completion / taking-over) is the natural *summary* of the same five folders; the snag-list becomes the next *iteration* of `02_PROGRESS/03_PUNCH_LIST.xlsx`.
