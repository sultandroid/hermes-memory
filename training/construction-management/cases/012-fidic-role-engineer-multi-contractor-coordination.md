## Summary
Dr. Munther Saket's 2010 FIDIC presentation articulates a structural question that defines multi-contractor coordination in modern mega-projects: *"In Projects where a number of Contractors may be awarded portions (packages) of the construction PROJECT, the issues of SITE POSSESSION, SITE SECURITY and CARE OF THE WORKS should be approached DIFFERENTLY. Consequent COORDINATION ISSUES arise."* His answer is the **bifurcation of the Engineer's role**: an Employer may split the single-FIDIC-Engineer's role into a *Supervision Consultant* (SC, executing the design-and-supervision function under SC 1.1.2.4) and a *Construction Manager* (CM, executing the site-management function) — both appointed under the FIDIC construct and *notified to the Contractor under Sub-Clause 3.4 [Replacement of the CM or the SC]*. This approach "may necessitate SPECIAL EXPERTISE to undertake administrative and coordination aspects of construction." The presentation enumerates the seven aspects of the Engineer's role that the CM/SC split must preserve: **definition, communications, interpretation, instruction & determination, site possession & multi-contractor, quality control, and measurement of work**. For a Saudi/GCC multi-contractor site, the bifurcation is operationally significant: the contractor must know which of the SC or the CM is the *decision-maker* for each aspect (cl. 3.1 in the standard 1999 form designates the Engineer, and cl. 3.4 governs replacement). A contractor that sends a Notice to the wrong role loses the time-bar.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "The ENGINEER'S ROLE is central to the FIDIC approach. In Projects where a number of Contractors may be awarded portions (packages) of the construction PROJECT, the issues of SITE POSSESSION, SITE SECURITY and CARE OF THE WORKS should be approached DIFFERENTLY. Consequent COORDINATION ISSUES arise."

> "This may necessitate SPECIAL EXPERTISE to undertake administrative and coordination aspects of construction. As such, the ENGINEER'S ROLE may be performed by TWO ENGINEERING CONSULTANTS: TECHNICAL SUPERVISION & CONSTRUCTION MANAGER."

> "The Engineer's definition is incorporated in Sub-Clause 1.1.2.4, and also is defined as one of the Employer's Personnel under Sub-Clause 1.1.2.6. To perform the Engineer's role, the Construction Manager and the Supervision Consultant need also to be defined in a similar fashion in the above Sub-Clauses."

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline** — distinguish *"Engineer"* (the contractually-named role under FIDIC SC 1.1.2.4) from *"Supervision Consultant / SC"* (performs the design-and-supervision function) from *"Construction Manager / CM"* (performs the site-management and coordination function). On a multi-contractor Saudi/GCC mega-project, the *contractual* Engineer is typically the SC; the *operational* Engineer (the one who issues site instructions) is the CM. Wrong address = wrong notice = lost time-bar.
2. **The linchpin evidence** — the **appointment letter and the Appendix to Tender entry** that names both the Engineer (SC) and the Construction Manager. For each Notice under FIDIC Cl. 20, the contractor must send the Notice *to the role that has authority over the matter in question*. The contract's Appendix to Tender / Appendix A is the *map* — keep a printed copy on every project manager's desk.
3. **The procedural principle** — *"SC 3.4 governs replacement."* When the Employer replaces either the SC or the CM, the Contractor is notified under SC 3.4 — and from that date, Notices must go to the replacement. A contractor that continues to send notices to the *former* SC/CM has misdirected the procedural step; in a contested arbitration, the tribunal will treat this as a *procedural defect* even if the underlying claim is substantively sound.
4. **The strategic lesson for multi-contractor coordination** — the **coordination of multiple contractors on a single site is not the Engineer's default role under the 1999 FIDIC Red.** Where the FIDIC contract is silent, the contractor must run the interface matrix (as in Case 011) as its own discipline. The Site Manager's coordination log is the documentation that protects the contractor against the *default* "no coordination mechanism in the contract — therefore no responsibility for the coordinating contractor" line.
5. **The principle with wider application** — *"FIDIC structures can be adapted to local practice without losing the underlying procedural sequence."* The CM/SC bifurcation is the practitioner consensus mechanism to use FIDIC in mega-project settings where a single Engineer cannot physically administer. Saudi/GCC mega-projects (KAFD, KAEC, NEOM packages) routinely use this pattern; the lesson generalises: the *procedural sequence* (FIDIC) is preserved, the *role allocation* (FIDIC SC 1.1.2.4 → SC + CM) is adapted.
