## Summary
Construction Week Online (CW) reported that **five workers were killed when scaffolding collapsed on the BP 10 portion of the King Abdullah Financial District (KAFD)** in Riyadh, on a part of the site being constructed by Saudi Binladin Group. The article was contemporaneous and short on root-cause detail — quoting a source who said: *"I have no more details right now as the matter is still under investigation as they are trying to find out what caused the accident."* Other named contractors on KAFD at the time included Saudi Oger. The site is one of the largest ever developed in the Kingdom. The BP-10 scaffolding failure is significant because it was *announced* at the industry HSE-in-Construction conference in Doha on the same day, signalling that the incident was already informing HSE policy dialogue — and because it occurred on the same contractor (Binladin) that suffered the Mecca crane collapse 24 days earlier, sharpening the systemic-readiness argument in subsequent reform debates.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline** — distinguish *"scaffolding"* (a temporary structure, but arguably also "Temporary Works" under BS 5975 / FIDIC SC 4.7) from *"shoring / falsework / formwork"* (each has its own design class). Scaffolding failure ⇒ look first at (a) ties to the permanent structure, (b) base-plate bearing capacity, (c) loading-rack / outrigger stability, and (d) erection-sequence under the contractor's TW Coordinator.
2. **The linchpin evidence** — the **erection-and-dismantling method statement and its tie-pattern drawing**, signed by the Temporary Works Coordinator and approved by the Project Manager. On KAFD-scale sites, the TWC must be *named and empowered* under BS 5975 cascade; without that, the contractor has no answer to the systemic-readiness allegation that follows a fatality.
3. **The procedural principle** — *"design check ≠ independent design check."* BS 5975/CTA requires that Class A temporary works receive an independent design check by a second, competent engineer. A contractor who relies on the supplier's design alone has not discharged the duty under SC 4.7 of FIDIC Red/Yellow 2017.
4. **The strategic lesson for HSE regulators and counsel** — when a single contractor suffers two near-simultaneous major fatal-site incidents (Mecca 11 Sep 2015, KAFD ~Oct 2015), the **systemic readiness** narrative shifts from "individual site failure" to "organisational control failure" — triggering director-duty / CID intervention and elevating the case from civil to quasi-criminal liability. Counsel should track contemporaneous reports across the same contractor rather than treating each site as an island.
5. **The principle with wider application** — *"HSE failures on mega-projects redefine the regulator's enforcement posture."* KAFD plus Mecca were the proximate catalyst for the tightening of Saudi workplace H&S law subsequently chronicled by Pinsent Masons (Case 006). The lesson generalises: any major-projects fatality becomes public-policy ammunition within weeks.

## How to use this in a construction-management playbook
Pair with Case 001 (Mecca) to show the *pattern* — same contractor, same calendar quarter, two separate temporary-works fatalities. Use when arguing (or defending) an *organisational control* allegation in a Saudi/GCC mega-project: the systemic-readiness case pivots on (a) the Temporary Works Coordinator appointment documentation, (b) the design-check independence, (c) the inspection register, (d) the site HSE manager reporting line into Head Office. Cross-reference to FIDIC SC 4.7 and Saudi MoL Executive Regulations 2025 (per Pinsent Masons, Case 006).
