## Summary
Three converging sources establish the present-day evidentiary standard for **photographs, drones, and site diaries in international construction arbitration**. The IBA / Vinson & Elkins article explains that drones are now used "from digitising construction sites for the purposes of improving planning and design, operations and recording progress on projects, to performing contractual obligations" and that "Employers may also use drone data to maintain their own records, particularly if there are concerns about a dispute over performance or delay" because drone imagery carries "date/time-stamps for accuracy, which may be persuasive to the other party." The GAR sixth-edition chapter on *Documents and other types of evidence* (2026) catalogues the canonical record classes — "baseline programmes and schedules, correspondence (emails, memos and meeting minutes), site-specific records (site diaries and progress reports) and technical documentation (inspection reports, engineering reports and photographs)" — and confirms that contemporaneous records are **essential for tracking progress and resolving disputes.** The Kluwer Arbitration Blog (2025) adds the *counter-weight*: built-in smartphone camera defaults (auto-enhance of brightness, contrast and colour balance; auto-tagging by object recognition; compression artifacts) can distort evidence without human intervention — and arbitral tribunals must carefully assess authenticity for enhanced video and photo evidence.

## The 5 Lessons
1. **Vocabulary discipline** — distinguish *"contemporaneous record"* (made at the time, by a person with direct knowledge, in the routine course of business — the IBA Rules 2023 / GAR 6th Ed consensus on admissibility) from *"evidence prepared for the dispute"* (back-analyses, expert reconstructions, post-incident narrative). The first carries weight without corroboration; the second requires an expert and is discounted for bias. **Site diaries, daily progress reports, drone flights, and inspection photographs** are all contemporaneous if maintained in the routine business; they are not contemporaneous if generated ad hoc after a dispute crystallises.
2. **The linchpin evidence** — a **dated, time-stamped, geographically-tagged drone photograph log with metadata preserved** (raw + processed versions). The metadata is the *authenticator*; strip the EXIF (Exchangeable Image File Format) and the photo downgrades to an "evidentiary illustration" that the tribunal may discount. Pair every drone flight with a flight-log file (.csv) and a separately-stored raw image archive.
3. **The procedural principle** — *"preserve, then disclose."* The first procedural reflex on any incident or variation site must be to *lock* the contemporaneous record — copy it offline, log the chain of custody, and disclose with privilege intact under IBA Rules Art. 9(2)(b). Failure to preserve early converts contemporaneous records into "evidence that should have existed" — a far weaker pleading point.
4. **The strategic lesson** — for **defending a delay or disruption claim**, the site diary entry that does *not* mention the issue will be cited against the contractor. Train site engineers to write what they saw (weather, plant breakdown, late delivery, instructions received), not what they *intend to claim*; advocacy belongs in the contractual Notice, not in the contemporaneous record. Conversely, for **offence / prosecution defence** of an HSE fatality, the *absence* of a corresponding site diary entry (or a drone sweep showing non-compliance) is the prosecutor's strongest exhibit.
5. **The principle with wider application** — *"modern cameras and smartphones default to altering the evidence."* The Kluwer article makes this concrete: built-in enhancement (brightness, contrast, colour balance) can change scene appearance; auto-tagging (Google Photos 2015) can misclassify; OCR errors and compression artifacts degrade readability. The IBA Rules 2023 (and Saudi-seated SCCA Rules 2023) require disclosure of the **unaltered original** alongside any enhanced version. The principle generalises to CCTV, body-cam footage, and any automated site-monitoring system.

## How to use this in a construction-management playbook
Stand-up checklist for any new site:
1. Lock chain-of-custody for all site diaries, daily progress reports, and inspection records (paper + digital).
2. Standardise drone-flight protocols: log file + raw image archive + processed image + geometric reference.
3. Disable default smartphone enhancement on site-issue photographs if those photos may later be forensically relied on.
4. Maintain a single site-diary register (electronic or bound) — one per shift, signed by the engineer's and contractor's representatives on the day.
5. On any incident, preserve *first* (the scene) and *notify* second — not the other way round.

Pair with IBA Rules 2023 document-production protocols and FIDIC SC 4.7 (records access).
