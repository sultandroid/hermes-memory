## 2–3 sentence summary with direct quote
Baker Botts' article, published in *Gulf Construction*'s regular "Legally Bound" column, is the most widely cited contemporaneous Gulf-focused treatment of the English-law "Malmaison principle" and how it diverges from local-law application of concurrent delay. It explicitly states that under English Law "the contractor would expect to get an extension of time for the full effect of the employer risk delay (to the extent that it is critical to completion on time) without discount for the contractor's own culpable delay. This is 'the Malmaison principle' (from Henry Boot Construction (UK) Ltd vs Malmaison Hotel (Manchester) Ltd) set out more than 20 years ago." The piece warns Gulf practitioners that contracts written in English-law style "might not be treated the same way in the Middle East" and that the SCL Protocol's "True concurrent delay" formulation is the rare case, not the typical Gulf fact pattern.

> "Concurrent delay has no official definition. Probably the most widely accepted source is the (UK) Society of Construction Law Delay and Disruption Protocol (Protocol). Even the Protocol doesn't attempt to impose a single definition: it mentions that there can be 'True concurrent delay' which is 'the occurrence of two or more delay events at the same time, one an Employer Risk Event, the other a Contractor Risk Event, and the effects of which are felt at the same time'. The Protocol acknowledges that this will be a rare occurrence: realistically happening only at commencement, when the contractor has not mobilised resources and the employer has not allowed access to the site." (Baker Botts, *Gulf Construction*, Dec 2021)

## 5-lesson extraction
1. **"True concurrent delay" is rare; "concurrent effect" is the working pattern** — most Gulf fact patterns are sequential events whose consequences overlap. Plan delay analyses around windows, not point-in-time coincidence.
2. **The Malmaison principle does not automatically export to Gulf law** — drafting your Saudi contract to assume full English-law EOT for concurrent employer risk is a risk; verify the governing-law clause and the SCCA / Board of Grievances case-law trend before tender.
3. **Each side of the concurrent pair must be critical to completion** — proving one event is "off the critical path" collapses the concurrency defence. CPM evidence quality is decisive.
4. **Bespoke contract wording overrides default principles** — Saga Cruises v Fincantieri (cited in the article) shows that an express clause referring to "any reason whatsoever for which the Contractor is ... responsible" can extinguish the Malmaison protection. Read every clause, not just Cl. 8.4.
5. **Train planners and contract administrators together** — concurrent-delay analysis is a hybrid CPM/legal exercise; lawyers who cannot read a programme and planners who cannot read a clause will lose the case before it is filed.

## Cross-references
- **Construction-management (CPM):** CPM critical-path evidence is the foundation of any concurrent-delay defence.
