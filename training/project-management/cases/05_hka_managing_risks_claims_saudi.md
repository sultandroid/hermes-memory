## 2–3 sentence summary with direct quote
HKA's 2025 contractor-focused Saudi-claims guide gives the most quantitatively rigorous risk-disruption pattern data for KSA: contractors' EOT claims averaged 97.2 % of planned schedule (133 projects), claimed costs were 27.9 % of CapEx, and "Late or restricted access to sites or workfaces – which triggered more claims in the Middle East than other regions – was even more prevalent in Saudi Arabia, disrupting 30.8 % of projects. Cashflow and payment disputes (31.6 %) caused bigger problems in the Kingdom." It introduces the explicit warning that "There is an increasing trend for employers in Saudi Arabia to include punitive clauses expressly extinguishing any entitlement to an extension of time or financial compensation for periods of concurrent delay" — the modern Saudi anti-Malmaison contractual weapon.

> "The extra time sought by contractors almost doubled programmes (stretching to 97.2 % of the average schedule) on the 133 projects analysed. The cost of claims, by contrast, tended to be lower at 27.9 % of budgeted CapEx. Late or restricted access to sites or workfaces – which triggered more claims in the Middle East than other regions – was even more prevalent in Saudi Arabia, disrupting 30.8 % of projects. Cashflow and payment disputes (31.6 %) caused bigger problems in the Kingdom, while late approvals were also commonplace (30.1 %)." (Zheng, HKA, 2025)

> "Agreements or awards that allow contractors extra time but not money for concurrent delays can be a significant financial risk. There is an increasing trend for employers in Saudi Arabia to include punitive clauses expressly extinguishing any entitlement to an extension of time or financial compensation for periods of concurrent delay. The contractor faces both damages and having to pay for their own indirect costs during the period when the employer is also in delay." (Zheng, HKA, 2025)

## 5-lesson extraction
1. **Treat the Saudi concurrent-delay clause as a deal-breaker, not a footnote** — an express anti-Malmaison clause ("no EOT for concurrent delay") is becoming the Saudi norm; price for it or refuse the contract.
2. **The site-access problem is structural, not project-specific** — 30.8 % of KSA projects are disrupted by access issues. Bake an early-warning trigger and a Cl. 8.4 / 2.1 notice process into the mobilisation plan.
3. **Cashflow and late-payment (31.6 %) kill more KSA contractors than LDs** — finance the project assuming 30 % of interim payments are delayed, and reserve the right to suspend under FIDIC Cl. 16.1.
4. **Programme integrity is the first line of defence** — HKA's programme-checks list (logic, leads, lags, hard constraints, high float, negative float, critical path tests, CPLI, BEI) is the audit you run before tendering, not after signing.
5. **Choose the delay-analysis method to fit the audience, not the data** — "Always consider the message you wish to get across and the audience." A Time Impact Analysis is for live EOT submissions; Time Slice Windows / As-Planned vs As-Built are for final disputes.
