## 2–3 sentence summary with direct quote
IQRM's NEOM-The-Line QSRA is the cleanest publicly available case study of why mega-projects in KSA need Monte Carlo-based schedule risk analysis rather than deterministic critical-path dates, and it sets out the empirical rationale: deterministic baseline dates "typically sit between P5 and P20 on the cumulative probability distribution. That means there is only a 5 to 20 percent chance of finishing on or before the planned date. For a $500 billion programme, making decisions based on a P10 date is not project management; it is gambling." The article is a step-by-step walkthrough of how a QSRA would have been run on The Line, including schedule-integrity checks (constraint removal, lag resolution), risk categorisation (uncertainties vs discrete events), and simulation settings — making it both a case study and a methodology reference.

> "NEOM's The Line was supposed to redefine how cities are built. A 170-kilometre linear city with no cars, no streets, and no carbon emissions. By early 2026, the project has been scaled back to a 2-kilometre initial phase, construction has been suspended pending a strategic review, and roughly $50 billion has already been spent. The question every risk professional should be asking is not whether the schedule slipped, but whether a Quantitative Schedule Risk Analysis could have forecast the magnitude of that slip before a single foundation was poured." (IQRM, 2026)

> "Quantitative Schedule Risk Analysis (QSRA) is a statistical method that stress-tests project timelines by modelling the impact of uncertainties and discrete risk events using Monte Carlo simulation. It replaces deterministic single-point schedules with probabilistic forecasts, producing a range of possible completion dates at defined confidence levels such as P50, P80, and P90." (IQRM, 2026)

## 5-lesson extraction
1. **First-of-a-kind (FOAK) projects cannot be baselined deterministically** — The Line has no historical comparables, so deterministic durations sit at P5–P20 confidence. Without a QSRA, every report to the PIF is "gambling."
2. **Schedule integrity is a precondition for risk modelling** — soft constraints, excessive lags, and missing logic must be cleaned up before Monte Carlo produces anything defensible: "A Monte Carlo simulation is only as good as the schedule it runs on."
3. **Separate "uncertainties" from "discrete risk events"** in the QSRA model — uncertainties propagate through the network (productivity, weather), discrete events are binary (permit denial, scope change). Modelling them in one bucket hides the schedule float they consume.
4. **Replace hard constraints with soft constraints before modelling** — date constraints fix durations and freeze risk propagation; they must be removed so that risks can flow through the network logic.
5. **The QSRA is the audit trail the PIF will demand** — by 2026 NEOM has spent $50bn and suspended construction; a QSRA would have produced a probabilistic range that the PIF could have used to make an informed re-scoping decision rather than a public suspension. The absence of one is the case study.
