## 2–3 sentence summary with direct quote
Strata's contractor's guide is the most comprehensive publicly available Saudi-specific treatment of FIDIC Clause 20 claims tied to Vision 2030 mega-projects (NEOM, The Line, Red Sea, Qiddiya), and it explicitly characterises Saudi practice as moving away from ad-hoc Fixed-Price / Cost-Plus arrangements toward "rigorous procedures for raising claims, including strict notice requirements, documentation standards, and escalation protocols." It catalogues the practical risks Vision 2030 mega-projects carry (scale, technical novelty, regulatory evolution, international teams, commercial pressure to compress timelines) and explains why FIDIC's standardised framework both attracts international contractors and traps unwary ones who miss procedural conditions precedent.

## 5-lesson extraction
1. **In Saudi mega-projects, FIDIC is a procedural minefield, not a friend** — every notice under Cl. 20 is a condition precedent. Strata's framing: "Contractors who fail to navigate these procedures correctly risk losing legitimate entitlements to time and cost adjustments, even when circumstances clearly warrant them."
2. **The "twin pressures" of FOAK engineering and tight Vision 2030 milestones are the root cause of acceleration claims** — recognise that the design is incomplete at award and the completion date is aspirational; build variation and acceleration entitlement into the programme from day one.
3. **Clause 20 is the most consequential clause in any KSA FIDIC contract** — invest clause-specific training for the PM and contract admin team before mobilisation, not after the first dispute.
4. **Saudi regulatory evolution is itself a risk** — Vision 2030 reforms shift compliance baselines mid-project; treat the contract as a living document, with monthly clause-impact reviews.
5. **Risk allocation in KSA is not what international bidders expect** — the lowest-bidder culture (Saudi public sector) "may lead to awarding the contract to small unqualified contractors, the thing which causes disputes and increases the cost during the project lifecycle" (cross-referenced via Alghamdi thesis). Plan for sub-tier insolvency and replacement-contractor claims.
