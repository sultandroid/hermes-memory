## 2–3 sentence summary with direct quote
HKA's eighth annual CRUX report quantifies that Saudi Arabia hosts ~39 % of the total MENA project pipeline by value and that "construction and engineering disputes already account for approximately 47.3 percent of the 2025 caseload at the Saudi Center for Commercial Arbitration (SCCA), the largest share of any sector." The article frames the Saudi litigation landscape — Board of Grievances for GTPL contracts, ministerial-approved arbitration, and the new codified regime — and warns that contractors entering KSA must understand a legal-institutional framework that has "fundamentally changed in recent years." It is the most current, data-anchored snapshot of the Saudi construction-disputes market available as of mid-2026.

> "Saudi Arabia sits at the centre of one of the world's most active construction markets. The Kingdom accounts for approximately 39 percent of the total MENA project pipeline by value, and construction and engineering disputes already account for approximately 47.3 percent of the 2025 caseload at the Saudi Center for Commercial Arbitration (SCCA), the largest share of any sector." (Khaireldin, HKA, 2026)

> "HKA's Eighth Annual CRUX Insight Report, analysing over 2,200 projects in 114 countries worth $2.43 trillion, found that time extensions sought by contractors globally amounted to 65.8 percent of planned schedules. The Middle East has historically exceeded this. HKA's Seventh Report recorded 80.9 percent across 480 projects in the region and 97.2 percent across the 133 Saudi projects within that dataset." (Khaireldin, HKA, 2026)

## 5-lesson extraction
1. **Quantify the dispute pool before you bid** — 47.3 % of SCCA 2025 caseload is construction. Treat dispute risk as a line item in tender pricing, not an actuarial afterthought.
2. **Forum selection is contractual architecture, not litigation tactics** — GTPL contracts route to the Board of Grievances by default; arbitration needs Ministerial approval. Negotiate the dispute clause before signing, not after the first EOT refusal.
3. **The Malmaison principle has GCC limits** — Saudi claimants cannot assume English-law extension-of-time entitlements will follow them into SCCA-seated arbitrations; the HKA series repeatedly flags regional divergence from common-law concurrent-delay doctrine.
4. **Schedule overruns above 90 % of planned duration are a structural feature, not a project defect** — Saudi CRUX data at 97.2 % EOT-to-baseline means a contractor's "delay damages" exposure model should assume the planned finish is unattainable; price contingencies on a probabilistic (P80) finish.
5. **Institutional reform has shifted the burden of legal literacy onto contractors** — recent changes to codified law, SCCA rules, and ministerial arbitration approvals mean foreign PMs need KSA-qualified counsel reviewing every variation and EOT notice, not just at arbitration stage.
