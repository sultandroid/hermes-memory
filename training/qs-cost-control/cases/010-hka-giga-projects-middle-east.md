## Summary
The most data-led practitioner consensus statement on cost control and contract-type selection for Vision 2030 mega-projects (NEOM, The Line, Red Sea, etc.). The article is the only public-domain source that publishes the CRUX insight that "projects in the Middle East suffered longer overruns, averaging over 83% of the planned schedule" and ties the overrun pattern to (i) design immaturity, (ii) scope changes on highly complex capital projects, (iii) the lack of partnering, and (iv) the "war for talent". It also captures the NEOM representative's direct quote on "team behaviours" being the most important cost-control factor — i.e. the contract type (lump-sum vs target-cost) is less important than the operational discipline applied within it.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "We need to work as a team and have a different mindset if we're going to deliver these projects successfully," Dele Oyekanmi said, adding that collaboration had to be underpinned by robust governance processes.

> "HKA's research programme, CRUX, also revealed how design failings and changes in scope drove project claims and disputes across the region. It was essential to allow enough time for design so it could be frozen and analysed for errors and gaps before works starts, and to ensure designs keep pace with construction progress."

> "Projects in the Middle East suffered longer overruns, averaging over 83% of the planned schedule, CRUX showed. The scope changes inevitable on highly complex and massive capital projects are better managed in conjunction with construction teams. Early contractor involvement (ECI) could capture insights to pre-empt design and other conflicts later."

> "The 'war for talent' applies to contractors too, Husam Gawish observed, as they could afford to be more selective about tendering in an overheating market. Employers increasingly need to adapt, feeding the trends toward more balanced risk allocation and negotiation, contractual mechanisms for late payment, collaborative working, and arbitration. 'Slowly, these are bringing Saudi Arabia in line with other jurisdictions.'"

> "Setting up a Dispute Advisory Board at the outset would help them problem-solve together rather than adopt adversarial stances, she advised."

## Lesson Extracted — five-point distillation for KSA / GCC practice
1. **83% schedule overrun is the baseline CRUX number for the Middle East.** This is the single most important number to embed in any cost report's "risk register" tab for a KSA mega-project. A contractor tendering on a 36-month NEOM sub-project should expect to spend ~67 months on site; the cost report's preliminaries tab should be sized accordingly.
2. **ECI (Early Contractor Involvement) is the most effective cost-control mechanism on Vision 2030 mega-projects.** Amanda Clack and the HKA panel align: scope changes on highly complex capital projects are "better managed in conjunction with construction teams. Early contractor involvement (ECI) could capture insights to pre-empt design and other conflicts later." From a contract-type perspective, this argues for *target cost* with pain/gain share (not lump-sum, not cost-plus) on mega-projects — because target cost aligns the contractor's incentive to design-out cost before construction.
3. **Team behaviours > cost-control systems.** The NEOM representative's quote is the most operationally important sentence in the article: "team behaviours outweighed even cost estimation and project management systems in importance." For the cost-control function, this means: (i) invest in the cost-controller's training, not just the cost-report software; (ii) co-locate the cost controller with the delivery team, not the finance team; (iii) give the cost controller the authority to challenge the project manager's programme on the spot.
4. **Design must be frozen before works start, and must keep pace with construction progress.** HKA's CRUX finding that "design failings and changes in scope drove project claims and disputes" is the empirical anchor for the "design freeze" discipline. In cost-control terms, the cost report must have a "design-change register" tab that records every design change and its cost impact in real time, not retrospectively.
5. **"Dispute Advisory Board at the outset" is the most concrete cost-control recommendation in the article.** Amanda Clack's advice — set up a DAB at the outset (not when a dispute arises) — is the single most actionable cost-control item. A standing DAB / DAAB shortens the dispute cycle, which in turn reduces the preliminaries-and-overheads accrual during the dispute. In KSA where the FIDIC DAB is often deleted (Pinsent Case 003), the cost controller should propose a *bespoke* Dispute Advisory Board in the tender query — a non-FIDIC body that performs the same function.

## How to use this in a quantity-surveying / cost-control playbook
This is the cost-control *during execution* canonical reference. Use the 83% CRUX number as the preliminaries sizing baseline; use the design-freeze discipline as the cost-report's "design change register" requirement; use the ECI argument to recommend target-cost contract type on Vision 2030 mega-projects; and use the DAB-at-outset recommendation as the dispute-cost provision in the cost report.
