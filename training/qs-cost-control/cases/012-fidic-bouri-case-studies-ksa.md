## Summary
Rafat Bouri's FIDIC-hosted case-study deck on the historical and current challenges of adopting FIDIC forms in Saudi Arabia. The deck documents (i) the pre-2008 Public Works Contract ("PWC") and its "employer-sided" disputes that fed the Board of Grievances, (ii) Royal Decree No. 155 of 9 June 2008 mandating FIDIC for Saudi public construction, (iii) the Saudi government-led initiative to localise the FIDIC forms (75 clauses used as-is, 45 clauses modified or added), and (iv) the application challenges in KSA of FIDIC Sub-Clauses 3.1 (Engineer's Duties and Authority), 3.5 (Determinations), 14.8 (Delayed Payment / "financing charges" / riba), and 20.2 (DAB Appointment). The deck includes a "Successful Applications" list — Haramain Rail, Riyadh Metro, Jabal Omar, Mecca Metro — that provides empirical context for how FIDIC actually performs on KSA mega-projects.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "Before the introduction of FIDIC Conditions of Contract, the Public Sector Projects used a Contract namely 'Public Works Contract' — Contractors had numerous complaints in the application of PWC (Employer Sided). Numerous disputes were issued to the Courts and Board of Grievances due to absence of Alternative Dispute Resolution mechanisms."

> "In the Royal Decree No. 155 dated 9 June 2008 item 5, it was decided to follow FIDIC's forms of contracts for the construction projects. This was mainly to: Increase efficiency of construction projects. Build on accumulated experience. Protect the interest of the parties."

> "The Saudi government carried out various sessions to reproduce a localized version of FIDIC to establish a legal reform of construction contracts in Saudi Arabia. As a result, 75 clauses were used as is, while 45 clauses were newly added, or modified. The localized version was internally issued as a trial, but not in effect yet."

> "Clause 3.1: Engineer's Duties and Authority — The Engineer power as a client's representative, and the Saudi Law. Authority to give Variations Instructions to Contractor. The implementation of the Engineer role as defined in FIDIC, and FIDIC's awareness by Local Consultants. The impartiality of the Engineer, and its independence decisions."

> "Clause 14.8: Delayed Payment '..Contractor shall be entitled to receive financing charges compounded monthly on the amount unpaid during the period of delay….' The Sharia'a Law and the interest rate. The Saudi Procurement Law. Finance Charges and its applications in Saudi. Examples of settled claims 'compensation requests' and its conclusion."

> "Clause 20.2: Appointment of the DAB — Employer's tendency. Contractor's focus. DAB as an extra charges on the project. Selected Adjudicators are not in a good position to make strong, reasonable, and correct decisions or awards because they are pure engineers without legal knowledge, or lawyers without construction or engineering background."

## Lesson Extracted — five-point distillation for KSA / GCC practice
1. **The pre-2008 PWC dispute pattern is the historical reason for FIDIC adoption.** "Numerous disputes were issued to the Courts and Board of Grievances due to absence of Alternative Dispute Resolution mechanisms" — the ADR-absence point is what made Royal Decree 155 politically possible in 2008. This is the institutional backdrop against which every KSA FIDIC-based dispute is now decided: the courts and the Board of Grievances are *the* fallback (HKA Case 009), but the policy direction since 2008 has been to keep disputes out of those forums by using FIDIC's ADR machinery. A cost controller should be aware that this institutional bias shapes how SCCA arbitrators and Commercial Court judges interpret the FIDIC clauses.
2. **45 of 120 substantive clauses in the Saudi-localised FIDIC are modified.** This is the single most important data point for any KSA cost-control practitioner: you cannot rely on standard FIDIC commentary alone. The Saudi-localised FIDIC is "internally issued as a trial" but is the working reference for many government clients. The cost controller must obtain a copy of the localised text and read the 45 modified clauses before pricing any variation under Sub-Clause 13.3.
3. **The Engineer's authority under Sub-Clause 3.1 is a Saudi-law-sensitive issue.** Bouri's bullet list — "The Engineer power as a client's representative, and the Saudi Law. Authority to give Variations Instructions to Contractor. The impartiality of the Engineer, and its independence decisions" — flags the recurring KSA tension: the Engineer is contractually independent but is paid by the Employer. In cost-control terms, this means variation pricing (Sub-Clause 12.3) and time-extension determinations (Sub-Clause 3.5) require *evidentiary independence* — the contractor must front-load the documentation so the Engineer's determination is compelled by the record, not by the Employer's preference.
4. **Sub-Clause 14.8 is the single most-debated KSA FIDIC clause.** Bouri's framing — "The Sharia'a Law and the interest rate. The Saudi Procurement Law. Finance Charges and its applications in Saudi. Examples of settled claims 'compensation requests' and its conclusion" — confirms the Pinsent Masons Case 003 finding that 14.8 is deleted in most KSA contracts. The cost-control response is the CTL Article 95/97 workaround (HKA Case 009): claim the cost of capital as compensation for loss under the CTL, not as financing charges under FIDIC.
5. **DAB appointment (Sub-Clause 20.2) is structurally weakened in KSA.** Bouri's bullet list — "Employer's tendency. Contractor's focus. DAB as an extra charges on the project. Selected Adjudicators are not in a good position to make strong, reasonable, and correct decisions or awards because they are pure engineers without legal knowledge, or lawyers without construction or engineering background" — explains why most Saudi FIDIC contracts delete the DAB (Pinsent Case 003). The cost-control response is to (i) check whether a DAB exists in the Particular Conditions, and (ii) if not, to flag the absence at tender stage and propose a bespoke Dispute Advisory Board (HKA Case 010).

## How to use this in a quantity-surveying / cost-control playbook
This is the only publicly-available Saudi FIDIC localiser's reference deck. Use the 75/45 modified-clauses statistic to justify the request for a copy of the localised text at tender stage; use the Sub-Clause 3.1/3.5 evidentiary-independence point to front-load the variation and EOT records; use the 14.8 deletion + CTL Article 95/97 workaround as the cost-of-capital recovery route; and use the DAB-deletion observation to propose a bespoke Dispute Advisory Board in the tender query.
