## Summary
Strata Risk Advisory's KSA-specific contractor-side guide to FIDIC claims under Vision 2030 mega-projects (NEOM, The Line, Red Sea, industrial cities, transport). The article walks through FIDIC Sub-Clause 20 (Claims, Disputes and Arbitration) and the three-stage 20.1 / 20.2 / 20.3 pathway, the notice and time-bar traps, the document-specific requirements (sub-clause on daywork, variations, final claims), and Strata's own service offering as dispute-readiness support. It is the cleanest single-source overview of how a KSA contractor must run a FIDIC claims process from bid to dispute.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "Clause 20.1 - Contractor's Claims: Requires the contractor to submit a detailed claim within a specified period (typically 28 days) after becoming aware of an event giving rise to the claim. The claim must include full supporting documentation and justification for the entitlement sought."

> "Clause 20.2 - Engineer's Determination: The engineer has the authority to make a determination on the claim within 42 days. If the contractor disagrees, they may refer the matter to the Dispute Adjudication Board (DAB)."

> "Clause 20.3 - Disputes and Arbitration: If either party disagrees with the engineer's determination, they may refer the dispute to the DAB (if established) or directly to arbitration. Arbitration typically occurs under ICC, LCIA, or local rules."

> "Many contractors believe they can raise a claim retrospectively after work is complete, only to discover that contractual timeframes have expired. FIDIC requires contractors to give notice of potential claims as soon as events occur that might trigger entitlements."

> "FIDIC contracts contain several 'time bars'—deadlines after which claims can no longer be pursued. These are strictly enforced and contractors cannot recover entitlements once the time bar has passed, even if the claim itself is meritorious."

## Lesson Extracted — five-point distillation for KSA / GCC practice
1. **The Sub-Clause 20.1 → 20.2 → 20.3 three-stage pathway is the contract spine.** Notice (28 days) → Engineer's determination (42 days) → DAB or arbitration. Strata's tabular exposition is the cleanest available. KSA cost controllers should produce a contract-administration flowchart with these three clocks marked; the cost report's "notices served" tab must mirror it.
2. **Time-bars are not negotiable in practice.** "Contractors cannot recover entitlements once the time bar has passed, even if the claim itself is meritorious" — Strata's wording is unusually direct. In KSA the *CTL Article 95 good-faith duty* is sometimes raised as a time-bar override (HKA Case 006), but this is a *defence*, not a *default*. The cost controller's discipline is to log notices daily, not retrospectively.
3. **The notice must include the contractual basis (which clause), the event, and the initial entitlement assessment — even if precise figures are not yet available.** Strata is explicit on all three: (a) contractual basis, (b) documentation, (c) initial assessment. This is a higher bar than the "I will claim later" letter that KSA contractors often serve. A notice that lacks the contractual basis can be argued to be a "non-notice" for Sub-Clause 20.1 purposes.
4. **The 20.2 engineer's determination is the *most under-used* cost-control mechanism in KSA.** Because most Saudi FIDIC contracts delete the DAB (Pinsent Case 003), the engineer's Sub-Clause 3.5 determination becomes the only binding interim decision before arbitration. The 42-day window is short; the cost controller should pre-prepare the determination inputs (variation pricing under Sub-Clause 12.3, daywork records, re-measurement surveys) so the engineer can determine within the window.
5. **The Vision-2030 mega-project context changes the stakes.** NEOM, The Line, Red Sea, industrial cities, transport — Strata lists five concurrent mega-project families. The cost-control implication is that any one of these projects that goes wrong becomes a *systemic* reference for the others: an arbitration award on Sub-Clause 20.1 time-bars in 2026 will be cited by every employer in 2027. Early, well-evidenced cost-control is the cheapest insurance against being the test case.
