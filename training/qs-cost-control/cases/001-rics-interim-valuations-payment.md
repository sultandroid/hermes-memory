## Summary
RICS's authoritative reference on how monthly interim valuations should be prepared, valued, certified and paid under the major UK standard forms, with a dedicated chapter on "Valuing work not properly executed" (§3.8) and "Valuing preliminaries when contract in delay" (§3.9). The document is the standard citation used in KSA / GCC quantum expert reports when the contract is silent on what the quantity surveyor may deduct, and it sets the industry baseline for "what goes into a payment certificate" — measured works, materials on/off site, preliminaries percentage, approved variations, less retention/previous payments/advances.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "Most construction contracts require interim payments to be paid to the contractor. This is to relieve the contractor of the burden of financing the whole of the works until completion; works which may take many months or years to complete. Valuation and payment are formal contractual processes and, therefore, they must be processed strictly in accordance with the contract conditions."

> "The value of work not properly executed by the contractor is to be deducted from the contractor's interim application by the quantity surveyor, if notified at the time of undertaking their valuation, or by the certifier prior to issuing their interim certificate or payment notice, whichever is applicable."

## Lesson Extracted — five-point distillation for KSA / GCC practice
1. **Interim ≠ final, but interim is the engine of the final account.** The RICS position is that every monthly IPC is a "snapshot" of work done to date plus materials on/off site, plus preliminaries, plus approved variations, less retention/previous payments/advances — and that "payment is a formal contractual process that must be processed strictly in accordance with the contract conditions". A KSA quantity surveyor who treats the interim as a goodwill estimate, rather than as a contractual mechanism under FIDIC Sub-Clause 14.3 (Application for Interim Payment Certificates), forfeits the discipline the contract was designed to impose.
2. **Deducting "work not properly executed" is a duty, not a discretion.** RICS makes the certifier the gatekeeper: defective work must be deducted *at the time of valuation*, not at final account. In FIDIC Red Book terms this maps onto Sub-Clause 11.1 (Completion of Outstanding Work / Remedying Defects) read with 14.3 — meaning the engineer's IPC and the contractor's payment application are the only places a defective-work deduction is contractually clean; raising it for the first time in the final account invites a "time-bar / crystallised" defence.
3. **Preliminaries during delay cannot be valued on the tender rate.** Section 3.9 ("Valuing preliminaries when contract in delay") is the single most-cited RICS section in delay-dispute quantum reports. The principle that the preliminaries rate is only a pricing mechanism, not a recovery mechanism, is the same principle HKA and Fenwick Elliott repeat in every GCC prolongation report — see Cases 005 and 007.
4. **Joint measurement is the rule, not the exception.** "The bills of quantities will be the subject of remeasurement once the design has been completed and an accurate quantity of work determined. It is important that measurement is carried out jointly and agreements reached on a progressive basis" (RICS *Valuing Change* §1.11, cited by the same Black Book). Saudi PWC and FIDIC-based contracts rarely require joint measurement; importing RICS as the "custom of the trade" via the Particular Conditions is a low-cost way to head off 60% of final-account disputes.
5. **Records are a precondition for the certificate, not a follow-up.** Section 2.9 (importance of records) frames contemporary records as the basis for entitlement, not as something to compile once a dispute is anticipated. This aligns exactly with HKA's CRUX finding that contractors in the Middle East "sabotage their claims by failing in their duty to give the notice specified in the contract" (Case 005) — the lesson is that the IPC process and the notice process must be run by the same person, on the same day, on the same set of contemporaneous records.

## How to use this in a quantity-surveying / cost-control playbook
Use this as the citation backbone for any IPC dispute that turns on what the certifier "should have" included or excluded. In KSA, where the contract is often a heavily-amended FIDIC, the RICS publication is the easiest non-party reference a tribunal will accept when interpreting "value of work properly executed".
