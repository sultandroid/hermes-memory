## Summary
Fenwick Elliott's comprehensive contractor-side guide to prolongation cost claims, structured around the two-category split (on-site preliminaries / off-site head office) and the entitlement-quantification separation. The article walks through the "on-site" preliminaries that are recoverable as actual cost (fencing, hoarding, cabins, utilities, site staff), the "off-site" head office overheads that are recoverable as either increased cost or opportunity cost, and the NEC3 / NEC4 specific clause machinery (Option C, Clause 11.2(33) "Completed work", Option W2.3(7)) that operates differently from FIDIC. The article is one of the most-cited contractor-side references in KSA / GCC quantum expert reports.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "Where the Contractor's works are delayed as a direct result of an Employer-risk delay event, the Contractor may be entitled to recover its head office overheads attributable to that delay. There are two categories of head office overhead claim: (i) Increased head office overheads — Where particular head office costs of the Contractor have increased as a result of the Employer-risk delay event, the Contractor is entitled to recover these additional costs. One example of such a cost is the cost of additional staff recruited by the Contractor because the project was in difficulties."

> "Head office, or off-site, overheads are the costs incurred by the Contractor as part of its normal business operations. Head office overheads include the costs of the Contractor’s head office staff, head office utilities (electricity, water, etc.), lease payments on the head office, and insurance. Head office overheads are distinguishable from the Contractor’s on-site overheads incurred on the project."

> "These are the Contractor's costs of maintaining its site establishment, and include fencing and hoarding for the site, cabins or site huts, and utilities. Where the project duration has been extended as a result of an Employer-risk delay event, these time-related preliminaries may increase. The Contractor is entitled to recover its additional costs in respect of these extended preliminaries. However, in order to do so, it will need to prove its actual additional costs incurred as a result."

> "Underlining the importance of keeping the above detailed records, the Contractor is also obliged under Option C to permit the Project Manager to inspect those records."

## Lesson Extracted — five-point distillation for KSA / GCC practice
1. **The two-category split is the contractor-side baseline.** Fenwick Elliott's framing — on-site preliminaries (recoverable as actual additional cost) vs off-site head office (recoverable as either increased cost or opportunity cost) — is identical to the Aceris / HKA / SCL Protocol framing. The point of convergence is what the cost controller must produce: actual-cost evidence, not a formula, for the on-site slice.
2. **The "additional staff because the project was in difficulties" head is the easiest HOOH line to win.** Fenwick Elliott singles this out as the cleanest example of "increased head office overheads" — and it is the line most often omitted in KSA prolongation claims because the contractor's HR system does not flag the cause of the recruitment. A cost controller should require every new head-office hire during a delayed project to be tagged with the project code and the cause.
3. **The cost-of-maintaining-site-establishment line items must be discretely evidenced.** "Fencing and hoarding, cabins or site huts, and utilities" — Fenwick Elliott's list — is recoverable only if there is a time-tagged cost record for each. In KSA, where site facilities are commonly hired (not owned), the hire invoices with the project code are the cleanest evidence. The cost report should have a separate "preliminaries — site establishment" tab and a "preliminaries — time-related only" filter.
4. **NEC3/NEC4 records clause is contractor-friendly *if* the contractor keeps the records.** Fenwick Elliott flags the NEC3 Option C obligation: the contractor must *permit the Project Manager to inspect* the cost records. The clause is symmetric: it gives the contractor the right to recover *Defined Cost* under Option C/D, but only if the records are contemporaneous and accessible. In KSA, NEC is currently the minority form (FIDIC dominates — see Case 003), but Vision 2030 NEC pilots are increasing and the records clause is the key entitlement lever.
5. **JCT and NEC final-account process comparison.** The Issue 93 article also compares the JCT final-account process to the NEC "final account" (which is really the final assessment of compensation events, not a single negotiated final account). The KSA relevance: where the contract is a hybrid (e.g. a JCT-style lump-sum amended FIDIC), the cost controller must understand which form's "final account" triggers apply, because they are not the same.

## How to use this in a quantity-surveying / cost-control playbook
This is the contractor-side canonical reference. Pair with HKA (Case 005) and Aceris (Case 006) to produce a prolongation claim that survives both an employer's "preliminaries rate × overrun" defence and a tribunal's "actual cost" requirement. The two-category split, the time-tagged hire-invoice discipline, and the NEC records-clause are the three operational disciplines the cost report must enforce from day one.
