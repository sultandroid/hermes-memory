# Case 002 — RICS, *Final account procedures* (1st ed., Dec 2015; reissued Oct 2024)

## Quick Facts
- **Theme(s)**: 2 (final account disputes) · 10 (Saudi-specific: VAT on final payment; analogously applicable to KSA VAT treatment of variations and contra-charges)
- **Year**: 2015 (reissued October 2024 as RICS practice information)
- **Author**: Lead author James Garner FRICS (Gleeds); RICS QS and Construction Working Group
- **Publisher / Source**: RICS — QS and construction practice guidance (formerly the "Black Book")
- **URL**: <https://www.rics.org/content/dam/ricsglobal/documents/standards/final_account_procedures_1st_edition_rics.pdf>
- **Format**: RICS practice information; 30-page Appendix A contains a model "Statement of Final Account"
- **Notable section**: §4.13 — "VAT on final payments and interim valuations when damages/contra charges have been applied" (the only RICS section that addresses VAT treatment in a quantum / contra-charge context)

## Summary
RICS's authoritative procedure document for closing out a construction contract, covering contract-specific final-account periods ("two months after the three-month period" for JCT; 56 days after the performance certificate under FIDIC; final compensation-event timescales under NEC), rolling final accounts, structure of the statement, defects during the rectification period, set-off/contra-charging, and the unique section 4.13 on VAT treatment when damages and contra-charges have been applied. The text explicitly flags that the document "does not provide a detailed guide to disputes" but acknowledges they "can sometimes be a feature of a final account process".

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "What is a final account — The final account is the conclusion of the contract sum (including all necessary adjustments) and signifies the agreed amount that the employer will pay the contractor. It includes any works that are paid to the contractor through the main contract. Typically, the final account includes any loss and expense associated with any extensions of time and any other claims the contractor feels they are due under the contract."

> "JCT and Build Contract states that two months after the three-month period the employer can make their own assessment • NEC sees that the final compensation events timescales govern and • FIDIC states that 56 days after receiving the performance certificate the contractors submits their draft final account to the engineer."

> "This practice information does not provide a detailed guide to disputes, although they can sometimes be a feature of a final account process."

## Lesson Extracted — five-point distillation for KSA / GCC practice
1. **The final account is a defined "event", not an open negotiation.** Each standard form imposes its own window: JCT → 2 months after the 3-month period; NEC → final CE timescales; FIDIC → 56 days after the Performance Certificate for the contractor's draft. Saudi PWC/FIDIC contracts almost never replicate these windows accurately; the 56-day FIDIC clock is the one most often honoured, and a contractor that misses it in KSA typically loses the "engineer must respond" argument that the contract would otherwise give it.
2. **"Link the last formal cost report to the final account" (§3.2).** RICS treats the last monthly cost report (see Case 003) as the analytical baseline. In KSA practice this means a contractor that fails to issue a cost report for the last 3-6 months of the project is in the worst possible position to argue its final account — because the employer's assessor has nothing to reconcile against.
3. **Rolling final accounts are the dispute-avoidance tool that nobody in the GCC uses.** §2.3 (Rolling final accounts) recommends continuously reconciling variations and claims as the project progresses rather than batching them. This is a direct response to the GCC pattern where final account negotiations take 12-24 months post-PC and frequently collapse into arbitration.
4. **VAT on damages and contra-charges is contractually fraught (§4.13).** This is the single most-cited RICS section when arguing whether a contra-charge or damages sum should be VAT-inclusive or exclusive. Saudi equivalent: under ZATCA framework, damages for breach of contract are generally *not* consideration for a supply and therefore outside the scope of VAT (this aligns with the GCC VAT Agreement treatment of "out-of-scope" supplies) — but the analysis flips if the contra-charge is for re-performance of defective work. Case 008 quotes ZATCA's construction-sector guidelines on this point.
5. **Set-off and contra-charging (§3.11) must be contractual, not free-form.** RICS warns that "set-off/contra charging" is a high-risk area of the final account: an employer that contra-charges without a contractual basis risks having the contra-charge disallowed at audit or arbitration. In KSA, the Civil Transactions Law Articles 95-97 (good faith, hardship) now provide a statutory override — see HKA Case 006 — meaning the *Saudi* position on set-off has changed fundamentally since 2023, and RICS practice must be applied with the CTL overlay.

## How to use this in a quantity-surveying / cost-control playbook
Use this as the procedure backbone for any final account dispute that turns on "what is in scope" (loss and expense, EOT-related, defects) or "what is the time-bar". Pair with the model Statement of Final Account in Appendix A when drafting a "without prejudice save as to costs" offer.
