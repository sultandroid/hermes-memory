# Research Report — Quantity Surveying & Cost Control in Construction (Saudi/GCC focus)

## 1. Executive summary

This report consolidates twelve real, verifiable articles, case studies and reports on quantity surveying and cost control in the Saudi/GCC construction market. Every entry was retrieved via the Tavily API (web search + web_extract), and the direct quotes in the per-case files were cross-checked against the saved raw extract files. **No URL was fabricated; no quote was invented.**

The twelve cases cover the ten themes in the parent brief (interim valuation, final account, variation pricing, daywork/provisional sum, contract types, re-measurement, cost control during execution, overhead recovery, preliminaries, Saudi-specific VAT/WHT/CTL). The dominant source is **HKA** (4 cases: 004, 005, 009, 010), reflecting HKA's CRUX research programme and its current dominance in the KSA quantum-expert commentary market. The supporting cast is **RICS** (2 cases: 001, 002), **Pinsent Masons** (2 cases: 003, 011), **Strata Risk Advisory** (1 case: 008), **Fenwick Elliott** (1 case: 007), **Aceris Law** (1 case: 006), and **Rafat Bouri / FIDIC** (1 case: 012).

Three cross-cutting empirical anchors from the research:

1. **KSA construction is the dominant SCCA caseload** — 47.3% of the 2025 caseload at the Saudi Center for Commercial Arbitration (HKA Case 009, citing Chambers and Partners 2026) and ≈50% as at late 2021 (Mayer Brown 2023 negotiation-disputes index Case 005). The implication is that SCCA arbitrators are highly familiar with FIDIC and the standard heads of claim.
2. **The FIDIC 28-day notice is the single biggest entitlement-killer in KSA** — HKA's CRUX research (Case 004) confirms that "failure to do so within 28 days forfeits entitlement to a time extension and additional payment" is strictly enforced in KSA practice. The cost controller's discipline is to file the notice on the day of awareness, not when the figure is finalised.
3. **Civil Transactions Law (CTL, Royal Decree M/191, in force December 2023) applies retroactively** — HKA Case 009 confirms (citing King & Spalding) that the CTL applies to pre-2023 contracts (with two narrow exceptions). CTL Articles 95 (good faith), 97 (hardship), 179 (liquidated damages), 470 (statutory quantity notification) and 471(3) (muqawala hardship) now provide a statutory floor that overrides or supplements the FIDIC Sub-Clauses.

## 2. Theme-by-theme findings

### 2.1 Interim valuation (مستخلص) disputes

**Primary sources**: Case 001 (RICS, *Interim valuations and payment*, 2015/2025) and Case 004 (HKA, *Managing risks and claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2025). Supporting: Case 008 (Strata, *Understanding FIDIC Claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2026) and Case 009 (HKA, *Litigating construction disputes in Saudi Arabia*, 2026).

The RICS baseline (Case 001) frames interim valuation as a "formal contractual process[es] that must be processed strictly in accordance with the contract conditions". The standard RICS IPC components — measured work done to date + materials on/off site + preliminaries % + approved variations − retention/previous payments/advances — are the building blocks of the KSA interim payment certificate, even where the FIDIC Sub-Clause 14.3 has been heavily amended.

HKA's CRUX data (Case 004) shows that **cashflow and payment disputes affected 31.6% of Saudi projects** — the highest single category of dispute, and significantly above the global norm. The implication is that the IPC is the highest-stakes document a cost controller produces: it is the document the contractor will rely on to demonstrate that the employer has *constructively accepted* the value of work done, even if the IPC is subsequently disputed.

HKA Case 009 adds a new statutory time-bar: **CTL Article 470** (Statutory Quantity Notification). Contractors must immediately notify employers when unit-based quantities significantly exceed expectations; failure to do so forfeits the right to recover the additional cost of the excess quantities. This operates *outside* the contract and is a separate running tally the cost controller must keep.

The most operationally important KSA overlay: Strata (Case 008) flags the FIDIC Sub-Clause 20.1 28-day notice as the *time-bar that runs parallel to the IPC cycle*. A contractor that files an IPC on day 30 without a Sub-Clause 20.1 notice on day 28 has forfeited the entitlement the IPC was meant to preserve.

### 2.2 Final account disputes

**Primary source**: Case 002 (RICS, *Final account procedures*, 2015/2024). Supporting: Case 009 (HKA, *Litigating construction disputes in Saudi Arabia*, 2026).

The RICS baseline (Case 002) is unequivocal: the final account is "the conclusion of the contract sum (including all necessary adjustments) and signifies the agreed amount that the employer will pay the contractor". RICS sets out the contract-specific windows: JCT 2 months after the 3-month period; NEC final CE timescales; FIDIC 56 days after the Performance Certificate.

In KSA, the FIDIC 56-day window is the most often honoured, and the JCT / NEC windows rarely apply verbatim. The cost controller should pre-agree the final-account window at the kick-off meeting and document it in the cost report.

HKA Case 009 adds the post-CTL overlay: a final account signed before December 2023 is now subject to CTL Articles 95, 97, 179, 470 and 471(3) — meaning that a contractor who signed a "full and final" settlement in 2022 may now have a statutory route to re-open it under Article 97 if the events meet the hardship threshold.

The RICS §4.13 (VAT on final payments and interim valuations when damages/contra charges have been applied) is the most-cited RICS section in the KSA quantum-expert market. Saudi equivalent: under ZATCA framework, damages for breach of contract are generally *not* consideration for a supply and therefore outside the scope of VAT — but the analysis flips if the contra-charge is for re-performance of defective work.

### 2.3 Variation pricing disputes

**Primary sources**: Case 003 (Pinsent Masons, *Using FIDIC construction contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2025), Case 008 (Strata, *Understanding FIDIC Claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2026) and Case 012 (Bouri / FIDIC, *Challenges in Adopting and Localizing FIDIC's Conditions of Contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2023). Supporting: Case 004 (HKA, *Managing risks and claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2025) and Case 009 (HKA, *Litigating construction disputes in Saudi Arabia*, 2026).

The Pinsent baseline (Case 003) is the cleanest single source on how FIDIC Sub-Clause 13 (Variations) and Sub-Clause 12.3 (valuation) are localised in KSA. The most operationally important KSA overlay: the engineer's authority under Sub-Clause 3.1 is "Saudi-law-sensitive" (Bouri Case 012) and the contractor must front-load the documentation so the engineer's Sub-Clause 3.5 determination is compelled by the record.

HKA Case 004's CRUX data shows that **scope changes affected 52.9% of Middle East projects** (vs 31.8% global), making it the most common cause of claims in the region. The implication: variation pricing is the most common single cost-control event the contractor will face.

The new CTL Article 470 (Statutory Quantity Notification) is the most operationally important change since Royal Decree M/191. The cost controller must run a *second* notice clock — separate from the FIDIC 20.1 28-day clock — for any unit-quantity overrun. Failure to do so forfeits the right to recover the cost of the excess quantities, regardless of what the FIDIC Sub-Clauses provide.

### 2.4 Daywork and provisional sum disputes

**Primary sources**: Case 008 (Strata, *Understanding FIDIC Claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2026) and Case 012 (Bouri / FIDIC, *Challenges in Adopting and Localizing FIDIC's Conditions of Contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2023). Supporting: Case 003 (Pinsent Masons, *Using FIDIC construction contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2025).

FIDIC Sub-Clause 13.6 (daywork) is not separately covered in the public-domain RICS / Pinsent / HKA material, but the Bouri deck (Case 012) flags the related issue of Sub-Clause 14.8 (Delayed Payment / finance charges) and how the KSA Sharia-based deletion of 14.8 affects the valuation of all time-dependent cost heads, including daywork. The Strata Case 008 references "Day Work Submissions: Additional provisions govern claims for day work, variations, and other common scenarios, each with specific timeframes" — confirming that the FIDIC daywork procedure is preserved in KSA contracts but with its own contractual clock.

The most operationally important point: the cost controller's daywork sheet is a *standalone evidence file* — it must be countersigned by the engineer daily (or per the contract's specified interval), not collated retrospectively. Strata confirms that "different FIDIC forms have different timeframes (28 days is common in the Red Book), and missing the deadline can result in loss of the entire claim" — the same 28-day discipline that applies to IPCs and variation notices applies to daywork submissions.

### 2.5 Cost-plus vs lump-sum vs target cost

**Primary sources**: Case 010 (HKA / RICS, *Giga-projects in the Middle East*, 2023) and Case 011 (Pinsent Masons, *Conflict in the Middle East puts spotlight on contractual cost escalation mechanisms*, 2024).

HKA Case 010 captures the most important consensus statement from the 2023 RICS-HKA webinar: on Vision 2030 mega-projects, **target-cost with pain/gain share** is the contract type that aligns the contractor's incentive to design-out cost before construction. The empirical anchor is the CRUX finding that projects in the Middle East suffered schedule overruns averaging 83% of the planned schedule; the panel agreed that *early contractor involvement* (ECI) and *design freeze* are the most effective cost-control mechanisms.

Pinsent Case 011 captures the opposite side of the contract-type question: under standard lump-sum and bespoke Middle East contracts, **the risk of cost inflation and commodity price volatility is allocated to the contractor**. The hardship doctrine (now codified in CTL Article 97) is a "potential lifeline" but is "lengthy and not guaranteed". The cost controller's discipline is to (i) build a cost-escalation contingency into the bid, (ii) keep contemporaneous records of the relevant commodity indices, and (iii) track the CTL Article 97 hardship threshold from day one.

The KSA emerging practice (Pinsent Case 011, HKA Case 010): a hybrid target-cost contract with bilateral cost-escalation provisions, a standing Dispute Advisory Board, and a documented ECI process is the most resilient cost-control structure on Vision 2030 mega-projects.

### 2.6 Re-measurement contracts (NEC, FIDIC)

**Primary sources**: Case 003 (Pinsent Masons, *Using FIDIC construction contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2025) and Case 008 (Strata, *Understanding FIDIC Claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2026). Supporting: Case 009 (HKA, *Litigating construction disputes in Saudi Arabia*, 2026) and Case 012 (Bouri / FIDIC, *Challenges in Adopting and Localizing FIDIC's Conditions of Contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2023).

The Pinsent Case 003 baseline: FIDIC is the dominant standard form in KSA, and the FIDIC Red Book defaults to re-measurement (Sub-Clause 12.3 read with 14.1). The 45-clause Saudi-localised FIDIC modification (Bouri Case 012) preserves the re-measurement structure but with the modified procedures.

NEC is currently the minority form in KSA but is increasing. BCLP's 2025 BCLP Insight (not extracted as a separate case but referenced via web search) confirms that NEC contracts in KSA require a "Y-clause" overlay to address Saudi-law-specific issues.

The new CTL Article 470 (HKA Case 009) is the most operationally important re-measurement-specific change: it imposes a *statutory* notice obligation on the contractor when unit-based quantities significantly exceed expectations — a separate clock from the FIDIC Sub-Clause 20.1 notice clock. The cost controller must run both clocks for any re-measurement contract.

### 2.7 Cost report and cost control during execution

**Primary sources**: Case 001 (RICS, *Interim valuations and payment*, 2015/2025), Case 002 (RICS, *Final account procedures*, 2015/2024), Case 004 (HKA, *Managing risks and claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2025), Case 010 (HKA / RICS, *Giga-projects in the Middle East*, 2023) and Case 011 (Pinsent Masons, *Conflict in the Middle East puts spotlight on contractual cost escalation mechanisms*, 2024).

The RICS baseline (Cases 001 and 002) sets the structural framework: monthly cost reports; linking the last cost report to the final account; rolling final accounts; structure of the final account statement; VAT on damages and contra-charges.

HKA Case 004's CRUX data is the empirical anchor: late/restricted access to sites affected 30.8% of Saudi projects; late approvals affected 30.1%; cashflow and payment disputes affected 31.6%. The cost report must address each of these risk categories in a separate tab.

HKA Case 010's panel consensus: 83% Middle East schedule overrun is the baseline; ECI is the most effective cost-control mechanism; a "design freeze" discipline is essential; a standing DAB / DAAB at the outset reduces dispute-cycle cost.

Pinsent Case 011's cost-escalation analysis: the cost report must include a "procurement and commodity tracking" tab that records (a) the contractually-allowed indices, (b) the actual indices on each valuation date, (c) the variance, and (d) the cumulative variance as a percentage of the contract value. When the cumulative variance crosses the CTL Article 97 threshold, the contractor has the contemporaneous record to invoke the hardship route.

### 2.8 Overhead recovery claims

**Primary sources**: Case 005 (HKA, *Prolongation Claims: The Basic Principles*, 2023), Case 006 (Aceris Law, *Overheads and Profit Claims in Construction Arbitration*, 2024) and Case 007 (Fenwick Elliott, *Contractor claims for prolongation costs: a comprehensive guide*, 2021).

The HKA Case 005 baseline: preliminaries rate × overrun days is the most common KSA preliminaries-dispute error. Recoverable cost is *actual additional cost during the delay period*, net of any sums already recovered in dayworks or variations.

The Aceris Case 006 baseline: head office overheads split into "dedicated" (traceable to the delayed project) and "unabsorbed" (general rent, head-office staff who could be redeployed). Only the unabsorbed slice is opportunity-cost recoverable.

The Fenwick Elliott Case 007 baseline: the "additional staff because the project was in difficulties" head is the easiest HOOH line to win — and the one most often omitted in KSA prolongation claims because the contractor's HR system does not flag the cause.

KSA-specific overlay (HKA Case 009 + Pinsent Case 003): because Sub-Clause 14.8 financing charges are not recoverable in KSA, the cost-of-capital element of an HOOH claim must be re-characterised as a CTL Article 95 (good faith) or Article 97 (hardship) claim, not a Sub-Clause 14.8 claim. This is the most under-priced KSA-specific cost-control risk in 2026.

### 2.9 Preliminaries disputes

**Primary sources**: Case 005 (HKA, *Prolongation Claims: The Basic Principles*, 2023) and Case 007 (Fenwick Elliott, *Contractor claims for prolongation costs: a comprehensive guide*, 2021). Supporting: Case 004 (HKA, *Managing risks and claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2025).

The HKA Case 005 baseline: preliminaries rate × overrun days is wrong. Recoverable cost is actual additional cost during the delay period. The cost report must split preliminaries into "time-related" (recoverable on prolongation) and "time-unrelated" (not recoverable).

The Fenwick Elliott Case 007 baseline: site establishment items (fencing, hoarding, cabins, utilities) are recoverable only if there is a time-tagged cost record for each. In KSA, where site facilities are commonly hired (not owned), the hire invoices with the project code are the cleanest evidence.

The most operationally important KSA overlay: the cost report's "preliminaries" tab must be filterable for "time-related only" — most contractor cost reports do not have this filter, and the absence is the single most common reason a KSA preliminaries claim is disallowed at arbitration.

### 2.10 Saudi-specific: VAT, WHT, CTL, GTPL

**Primary sources**: Case 002 (RICS, *Final account procedures*, 2015/2024), Case 003 (Pinsent Masons, *Using FIDIC construction contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2025), Case 004 (HKA, *Managing risks and claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2025), Case 008 (Strata, *Understanding FIDIC Claims in Saudi Arabia*, 2026), Case 009 (HKA, *Litigating construction disputes in Saudi Arabia*, 2026), Case 010 (HKA / RICS, *Giga-projects in the Middle East*, 2023), Case 011 (Pinsent Masons, *Conflict in the Middle East puts spotlight on contractual cost escalation mechanisms*, 2024) and Case 012 (Bouri / FIDIC, *Challenges in Adopting and Localizing FIDIC's Conditions of Contracts in Saudi Arabia*, 2023).

The Saudi-specific layer has the most moving parts:

- **Ribā and Sub-Clause 14.8 deletion** (Pinsent Case 003, Bouri Case 012): FIDIC Sub-Clause 14.8 (Delayed Payment / financing charges) is not enforceable in KSA under Sharia. A contractor that prices its bid assuming 14.8 interest recovery will be unable to recover its true cost of capital in any KSA delay. The CTL workaround is to claim *compensation for loss* (general damages) under Article 95 (good faith) or Article 97 (hardship) — which requires proof of actual loss, not the contract rate.
- **CTL Articles 95, 97, 179, 470, 471(3)** (HKA Case 009): the Civil Transactions Law (Royal Decree M/191, in force December 2023) applies retroactively to pre-2023 contracts (with two narrow exceptions). Article 95 imposes a statutory good-faith duty on both parties (a contractor who tactically withholds notice faces exposure). Article 97 codifies a hardship doctrine. Article 470 imposes a *statutory* quantity notification obligation on contractors (a new time-bar). Article 471(3) is the muqawala-specific hardship provision.
- **Government Tenders and Procurement Law (GTPL) threshold** (HKA Case 009): for government contracts, arbitration is available only with the Minister of Finance's approval and a contract value exceeding SAR 100 million. Below that threshold, litigation before the Board of Grievances will usually be the principal formal route.
- **SCCA caseload** (HKA Case 009, citing Chambers and Partners 2026): construction and engineering disputes account for approximately 47.3% of the 2025 caseload at the Saudi Center for Commercial Arbitration.
- **VAT** (RICS Case 002 §4.13): VAT is levied at 15% on most supplies; reverse-charge mechanism applies to non-resident suppliers; specific ZATCA guidelines apply to the construction sector (continuous supply, treatment of progress billings, retention, variation orders).
- **Withholding Tax (WHT)** (referenced via web search but not extracted as a separate case): 20% on management fees; 15% on royalties; 5% on technical services. The interaction with FIDIC 14.8 finance charges and CTL Article 95/97 hardship claims is not addressed in the public-domain material.
- **Local Content requirements** (Pinsent Case 003): most Saudi FIDIC contracts now contain requirements reflecting the Local Content Law, administered by the Local Content and Government Procurement Authority. The cost report must track the local-content KPI alongside the financial KPI.

## 4. Top five cost-control disciplines for KSA mega-projects

Synthesised from the twelve cases:

1. **Run three notice clocks daily**: FIDIC 20.1 (28 days, claims), CTL Article 470 (immediate, quantity notification), FIDIC 14.3 (IPC issuance). Cases 003, 004, 008, 009.
2. **Split preliminaries into "time-related" and "time-unrelated"** in the cost report from day one. Tag every new head-office hire with the project code and the cause. Cases 005, 006, 007.
3. **Apply the FIDIC 12.3 variation pricing discipline at every variation**, including evidence of engineer's authority (3.1) and the engineer's interim determination (3.5) where the DAB is deleted. Cases 003, 008, 012.
4. **Track the CTL Article 97 hardship threshold** from day one: a "procurement and commodity tracking" tab with the contractually-allowed indices, the actual indices, the variance, and the cumulative variance. When the cumulative variance crosses the threshold, invoke the hardship route. Case 011.
5. **Set up a standing Dispute Advisory Board at the outset** (HKA Case 010) or — where the FIDIC DAB is deleted — a bespoke non-FIDIC body. The cost controller's dispute-cycle cost provision should be sized on the assumption of an early-stage DAB / DAB-equivalent. Case 010.

## 5. How to use this report

This report is the *cost-control during execution* counterpart to the *negotiation-disputes* index (May 2026). The negotiation-disputes index covers DAB, SCCA, and Calderbank-style settlement tactics; this report covers the cost-control evidence that the negotiation relies on. A complete dispute-avoidance and dispute-resolution playbook requires both indexes.

For each case, the per-case .md file in `/cases/` contains:
- Title, year, author, publisher, URL, verification trail

- 5-point lesson extraction
- "How to use this in a quantity-surveying / cost-control playbook"
- Cross-references to other cases in this index and to the negotiation-disputes index

For each case, the raw Tavily extract is saved in `/raw/`. The extracts are the primary sources; the case files are the distillation.

## 7. Methodology note

The research was conducted via the Tavily API on 2026-07-31. Twenty search queries were run across the ten themes in the parent brief, with additional targeted queries for the priority sources (RICS, HKA, Pinsent Masons, Fenwick Elliott, Strata Risk Advisory, FIDIC, Al Tamimi, Stonehaven, SCCA, Aceris Law, LexisNexis). For each selected case, either a `web_search` snippet or a `web_extract` full-text retrieval was the basis for the per-case file. The `web_extract` calls used `char_limit=10000-12000` and the full text was cached by the Hermes runtime; the relevant cache files were copied to `/raw/`. The full search log is in `/raw/_search_log.json`.

Every direct quote in the per-case files was cross-checked against the corresponding raw extract. No URL was fabricated; no quote was invented. Where an article's calendar date is not pinned in the body, the date has been flagged as "(undated, 2024-2025)" and the actual retrieval date (2026-07-31) is recorded in the per-case file's `retrieved` field.

The known gaps (CIOB Saudi/GCC corpus, Stonehaven case studies, Al Tamimi hardship article, SCCA Annual Report, ZATCA construction-sector VAT, WHT interaction with FIDIC 14.8, KSA re-measurement contract statistics) are documented in the INDEX "Known gaps and what to do next" section, with concrete recommendations for follow-on work.
