# QS & Cost Control — Top Lessons

## From Articles 001 + 002 (RICS) — Interim Valuations and Final Accounts

1. **The interim valuation is the most important monthly document in any construction contract.**
   A signed IPC is a payment obligation; an unsigned or disputed one is a payment claim. The RICS standard methodology protects the Contractor against Employer under-certification.

2. **The final account must be agreed and signed, not just issued.**
   A unilateral "Final Account Statement" by the Employer is not a final account in law. Both parties must agree on the final sum, OR a quantum assessor / adjudicator must determine it. Anything else is a continuing claim.

## From Articles 003, 004, 008 (Pinsent, HKA, Strata) — FIDIC in Saudi

3. **FIDIC variation pricing (Cl. 12 / Cl. 13) is the single most disputed clause in Saudi construction.**
   The new rates vs existing rates decision is the most common arbitration head. **Operational rule:** maintain a comprehensive rate library from day one; document the basis-of-rate for every BOQ line.

4. **HKA 2025 Saudi claim statistics:** the #1 cause of claim is inadequate scope definition at tender stage. **Drafting consequence:** at the tender stage, push for explicit exclusions and clarifications; an unclear tender scope is a claim seed.

## From Articles 005 + 007 (HKA + Fenwick Elliott) — Prolongation

5. **Prolongation cost = preliminaries × delay period.** Document the preliminaries breakdown in the original tender, not after the dispute arises.
   "Preliminaries" includes site staff, plant, scaffolding, temporary works, security, insurance — not just the project manager's salary. **Operational rule:** the preliminaries schedule must be detailed enough to apportion by day.

6. **Concurrent delay = no time-related cost.**
   If both the Contractor and the Employer caused delay during the same period, the Contractor is entitled to EOT but not to prolongation cost. The *Malmaison* approach is the default in UAE; Saudi courts are catching up.

## From Article 006 (Aceris Law) — Overheads and Profit

7. **Head office overheads (HOOH) recovery requires a specific formula, not a guess.**
   The most widely accepted is the **Emden formula** (or Hudson's formula). The Contractor must show (a) the contract was delayed by an Employer-risk event, (b) the Contractor was unable to deploy the resources elsewhere during the delay, (c) the formula calculation is reasonable.

8. **Profit on prolongation is generally NOT recoverable.**
   Profit is a return on capital and effort, not on time. The Contractor recovers preliminaries, HOOH, and financing charges during delay, but not profit.

## From Article 010 (HKA — Giga Projects) — Risk Pricing

9. **Mega-project preliminaries are 12-18% of contract value (vs 8-12% on standard projects).**
   The complexity and duration of NEOM / Red Sea / Diriyah projects requires more staff, more plant, more security, more insurance. **Bidding consequence:** if your preliminaries on a mega-project are below 12%, you've under-priced the risk.

## From Article 011 (Pinsent — Cost Escalation) — Saudi Macro Risk

10. **Material cost escalation in Saudi construction 2022-2025 ranged 20-40% on key items (steel, cement, copper, aluminium).**
    Fixed-price contracts signed before 2022 are exposed. **Drafting consequence:** for new contracts, include an escalation clause tied to a published index (e.g. Saudi Arabia CPI, or commodity-specific).

## From Article 012 (FIDIC — Bouri Case) — Saudi FIDIC

11. **Bouri (FIDIC publication, 2010) is the foundational case-study collection for FIDIC in Saudi Arabia.**
    The lessons from Bouri predate the 2025 Civil Transactions Law but remain relevant for FIDIC Yellow / Silver applications in Saudi.

## Cross-cutting

12. **The cost claim is won or lost on documentation, not math.**
    The Contractor's claim with the best backup (timesheets, invoices, plant returns) wins. The most rigorous calculation with thin backup loses.
