## Case-by-case (full quotes + lessons, raw files linked)

### Case 001 — DRBF, "Dispute Review Boards: The Gold Standard"
- **Quote**: "Resolution rate to date: 85-98% of matters going to the DB do not go on to arbitration or litigation."
- **Lesson**: Treat the DAB/DRB as live insurance, not as a precursor to arbitration. With 85-98% of matters dying at the DB, the goal of any tier-1 clause is to never reach the Notice of Dissatisfaction.

### Case 002 — Pinsent Masons, DAB "under used yet highly valuable"
- **Quote**: "Like any tool, a DAB requires a clear understanding of its purpose and operating methods to be effective – but used properly, with the right advice, DABs can play a crucial role in providing an effective and efficient platform for resolving disputes."
- **Lesson**: In civil-law jurisdictions (KSA, France, onshore UAE), DAB success depends on deliberate multi-tier drafting: a standing DAB beats an ad hoc one, and the Notice-of-Dissatisfaction window must be calibrated to the local court's approach to interim enforcement.
- **File**: `/home/hermes/training/negotiation-disputes/cases/002-pinsent-masons-dab-underused-construction.md`

### Case 003 — Fenwick Elliott / CIArb DB Rules
- **Quote**: "They [CIArb Rules] are the only single set of international dispute board rules that can be used in any commercial or construction contract. The parties may empower the dispute board either to make binding decisions (i.e. a DAB) or to make non-binding recommendations (i.e. a DRB). Only standing dispute boards are provided for."
- **Lesson**: When the FIDIC Red Book DAAB machinery feels disproportionate for the project, drop in the 2014 CIArb DB Rules by reference — industry-agnostic, choice of binding or non-binding output, mandatory standing board.

### Case 004 — Kabine Law, Skipping FIDIC's DAB
- **Quote**: "These decisions demonstrate that a uniform interpretation of the nature of the DAB is still not achieved internationally, and the facts of the case, as well as the governing law, are crucial."
- **Lesson**: Skipping the DAB is a strategic gamble — some civil-law seats enforce a shortcut; some common-law seats refuse. The governing-law variable decides the outcome.

### Case 006 — Kluwer, SCCA Rules for construction disputes
- **Quote**: "Resolving construction disputes in the KSA by way of SCCA arbitration is set to become the norm. Indeed, the SCCA arbitration rules include several features that make arbitrating construction disputes in the KSA an increasingly popular choice. The SCCA's joinder provisions (Article 12) are one such feature that is particularly important in construction disputes."
- **Lesson**: Pre-arbitration settlement strategy in KSA construction must anticipate SCCA Article 12 joinder — any settlement should reserve third-party rights and allocate contribution between joint-venture participants before joinder pulls them in.

### Case 007 — Cohen Seglias, Applying "Getting to Yes" (Fisher-Ury) to construction
- **Quote**: "Disputes are a fact of life in the construction industry. As a practicing construction attorney for twenty years… I have negotiated and helped resolve hundreds of construction disputes… The [Arcadis report] reported that in North America in 2022 the average value of disputes on construction projects increased 42% to an all-time high, a staggering $42.8 million. The average time required to resolve construction disputes was 13.6 months."

### Case 008 — Reed Smith, Dubai Court of Cassation case 486/2024 (upholding "without prejudice" in onshore UAE)
- **Quote 1**: "A judgment issued on 3 April 2024 by the Dubai Court of Appeal in case no. 31/2024 – Commercial Appeal (Appeal Judgment), which was upheld by the Dubai Court of Cassation on 22 October 2024 in case no. 486/2024 – Commercial Cassation (Cassation Judgment), for the first time sets out the UAE's judicial definition of the without prejudice concept."
- **Quote 2**: "These rulings emphasise that confidentiality of settlement negotiations must be maintained and clarify the conditions under which such communications are protected from disclosure in onshore court proceedings."
- **Lesson**: GCC parties can now safely put Saudi/onshore-UAE settlement correspondence in writing under "without prejudice" cover (subject to waiver/unfitness exceptions). Combined with SCCA mediation step, this is the moment to graduate from handshake settlements to documented Calderbank-style offers.

## How to combine these into a "settle-vs-fight" playbook

1. **At contract drafting**: Embed a standing DAB using either FIDIC 1999/2017 DAAB machinery or the 2014 CIArb DB Rules (Cases 001, 002, 003). Wire joinder awareness into the multi-tier clause so SCCA-style consolidation does not ambush the GC (Case 006).
2. **At first notice of claim**: Push for SCCA mediation, not litigation (Cases 005, 006). With construction = ~50% of SCCA caseload, mediator familiarity with FIDIC and Saudi practice is high.
3. **At the mediation table**: Apply the Fisher-Ury four-principle checklist (Case 007) and pre-quantify the BATNA using the Arcadis 13.6-month / $42.8 M benchmark. Put the offer in writing under "without prejudice save as to costs" cover (Case 008) — the Dubai Court of Cassation has now legitimised this in the GCC.
4. **If mediation fails**: Comply strictly with the DAB multi-tier clause (Cases 002, 004). Do not "skip" — the enforcement of the eventual award can turn on whether the steps were followed.
5. **Throughout**: Keep raw extracts in `/tmp/tavily-research-negotiation/raw/` for re-reading — they are the primary sources, not the case summaries.

A definitive date can be obtained by retrieving the article's `<meta property="article:published_time">` tag with a targeted curl — recommend doing so before quoting the case in a dated memo.
2. **Theme 6 (Calderbank specifically)** has only one main case (008). The supporting Global Arbitration News and Norton Rose Fulbright materials have been retained as raw files. If the parent agent needs a second numbered case on Calderbank offers specifically, the best candidate is a case numbered 008b with Norton Rose's "Sealed offers in international arbitration" (Sept 2016) as the primary source — the underlying article has been fully extracted (`raw/norton-rose-sealed-offers.json`).
3. **Saudi-specific without-prejudice case law** is thin on the public web. The cases known to bear directly on this are: Baker McKenzie's Resource Hub entry on Cases 31/2024 and 486/2024 (cross-checked), and Kennedys Law's "'Without prejudice' matters" piece on the same case. There is no comparable Saudi-published court ruling in the same period — Saudi onshore "without prejudice" is still effectively nascent.
4. **SCCA mediation success-rate statistics** — the public web returns "70%" / "70-80%" only as AI answer synthesis, not as direct attributable citation. There is no published SCCA Annual Report that quotes a settlement rate. This was flagged honestly in the Cases 005 / 006 files (the lesson is anchored to the caseload profile, which IS verifiable, rather than to a settlement rate that is not).
