## Summary
The second of two Mayer Brown overviews of Saudi arbitration under the new SCCA regime. Quantifies SCCA's caseload by sector: "construction cases comprised about half of the SCCA's caseload" as at late 2021, followed by banking & finance, then real estate, then a long tail of 24 other sectors. The piece frames the new 2023 SCCA Arbitration Rules against Vision-2030 construction volumes ("giga-projects") and argues that the SCCA — alongside the Dubai-seated DIFC-LCIA pathway — will be the centre of gravity for KSA and MENA construction arbitration.

## Lesson Extracted
With construction ≈ 50 % of SCCA caseload, pre-arbitration settlement tactics tuned to SCCA procedure will move the needle more than tactics aimed at any other sector. This argues for (a) embedding SCCA mediation step clauses, (b) using the SCCA Expedited Procedure for sub-SAR 1 M claims, and (c) using SCCA's joinder provisions (Article 12 of the 2023 Rules — see Case 006) deliberately during negotiation so the mediator knows who must sign.

## How to use this in a pre-arbitration playbook
Use this when pitching SCCA mediation to a client who thinks of SCCA only as an "arbitration forum". Half of SCCA's caseload is construction — the institutional fit is unusually high, and SCCA mediators are likely to know FIDIC. Pair with Case 006 (joinder mechanics) and Case 008 (write your settlement offers down, GCC "without prejudice" is no longer a dead letter).
