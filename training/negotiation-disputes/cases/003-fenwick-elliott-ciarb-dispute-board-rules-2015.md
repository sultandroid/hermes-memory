## Summary
Reference text on the 2014 Chartered Institute of Arbitrators (CIArb) Dispute Board Rules, the first single set of international DB rules designed to be inserted into any commercial or construction contract — including non-construction industries — and to provide either binding decisions (DAB-style) or non-binding recommendations (DRB-style). Gould's overview maps the CIArb Rules against the FIDIC DAB machinery, the ICC DRB/DAB/CDB Rules, the ICE procedure, and the AAA rules, highlighting that CIArb's main innovation is industrial flexibility and a stronger emphasis on dispute avoidance. The article is regularly cited by counsel drafting bespoke DB clauses for GCC mega-projects when the FIDIC Red/Yellow/Silver Book regime is seen as too heavy.

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "They [the CIArb Rules] are the only single set of international dispute board rules that can be used in any commercial or construction contract. The parties may empower the dispute board either to make binding decisions (i.e. a DAB) or to make non-binding recommendations (i.e. a DRB). Only standing dispute boards are provided for."

## Lesson Extracted
When the FIDIC Red Book 1999/2017 DAAB machinery feels disproportionate to the size or industry of the project, drop in the 2014 CIArb DB Rules by reference. They are industry-agnostic, accommodate either binding or non-binding outputs, and require a standing board — exactly the configuration the DRBF statistics (Case 001) credit with an 85-98 % no-escalation rate.

## How to use this in a pre-arbitration playbook
Use this when counsel is resisting the FIDIC DAAB on grounds of fit (e.g. a supply-and-install contract that isn't pure Red Book construction). The CIArb Rules are an off-the-shelf alternative that the courts will recognise as a contractually-agreed DRB/DAB process and that the DRBF's empirical record supports.
