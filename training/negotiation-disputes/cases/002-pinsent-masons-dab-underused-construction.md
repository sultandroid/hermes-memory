## Summary
Follow-up write-up from a Pinsent Masons-hosted Paris panel on DABs. The piece distinguishes standing DAB (continuous, project-long) from ad hoc DAB (constituted only once a dispute has emerged), and positions the DAB between mediation (advisory) and arbitration (binding). The framing argument is that DABs "are often relatively unfamiliar or imperfectly understood, especially in civil law jurisdictions". The article is the strongest recent practitioner-level justification for embedding a standing DAB in GCC / civil-law construction contracts where parties are reluctant to commit to a full arbitration-administered FIDIC DAAB.

## Lesson Extracted
In civil-law jurisdictions (KSA, France, onshore UAE), DAB success depends on deliberate multi-tier drafting: a standing DAB beats an ad hoc DAB, and the Notice-of-Dissatisfaction window must be calibrated to the local court's approach to interim enforcement. Under-using the DAB is a drafting failure, not a market failure.

## How to use this in a pre-arbitration playbook
