## Summary
DRBF presentation positioned to introduce Dispute Boards — both Dispute Review Boards and Dispute Adjudication Boards — as "project insurance" for major infrastructure works. The deck historicises the practice (first international DB: El Cajon Dam, Honduras 1981; FIDIC DAB Supplement 1992; World Bank mandatory threshold from 1995), then quantifies outcomes: in the DRBF's 2003 catalogue of 1,062 projects (worth > US$77.7 billion), only 28 matters — roughly 2 % — proceeded beyond the board. The presentation cites a broader "Resolution rate to date: 85-98 % of matters going to the DB do not go on to arbitration or litigation." World-Bank-grade mega-projects (LaGuardia Terminal B, Texas SH 99, NYC Meadowlands) are itemised to show the DB has scaled from US highways into multi-billion-dollar P3 aviation and rail work.

## Lesson Extracted
Treat the DAB/DRB as live project insurance, not as a precursor to arbitration. With 85-98 % of matters dying at the DB, the goal of any tier-1 dispute clause is to never reach the Notice of Dissatisfaction — the DB is the place where interests, BATNAs and technical records meet in real time.

## How to use this in a pre-arbitration playbook
When the contractor or employer asks "why pay for a DAB at all?", show them this 85-98 % no-escalation figure. Frame the annual DAB retainer (~US$40-75k per year for a standing board on smaller projects; per the Consensusdocs citation embedded in the DRBF reading list) as insurance against the 13.6-month / US$42.8 M average dispute (Arcadis 2023 — see Case 007).
