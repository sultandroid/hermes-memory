# Case 008 — Dubai Courts Uphold the "Without Prejudice" Principle

## Summary
Analysis of two landmark UAE onshore-court rulings — Dubai Court of Appeal case 31/2024 (3 April 2024) and Dubai Court of Cassation case 486/2024 (22 October 2024) — which for the first time upheld "without prejudice" privilege in onshore UAE proceedings. Until these rulings, the standard advice from Gulf lawyers had been "UAE onshore courts do not recognise the concept of 'without prejudice'" — parties were told not to put settlement offers in writing. The Article 486/2024 ruling explicitly conditions protection on the genuineness of the attempt to settle, drawing the UAE closer to the English/FIDIC tradition (and to the Calderbank "without prejudice save as to costs" device).

## Direct Quote (verbatim from source)
> "These rulings emphasise that confidentiality of settlement negotiations must be maintained and clarify the conditions under which such communications are protected from disclosure in onshore court proceedings."

## Lesson Extracted
GCC parties can now safely put Saudi/onshore-UAE settlement correspondence in writing under "without prejudice" cover with reasonable confidence of protection (subject to the recognised exceptions — clear waiver, an admission of liability, or unfitness). Combined with SCCA's optional mediation step, this is the moment to graduate from handshake settlements to documented Calderbank-style offers. KSA onshore courts remain slower to follow, but the direction is set.

## How to use this in a pre-arbitration playbook
Use this to justify putting "without prejudice save as to costs" correspondence in writing with GCC counterparties. Until 22 October 2024 the standard advice was "don't write it down" — that advice is now backwards. Draft the offer using the Norton Rose and Global Arbitration News templating rules: label it clearly, make it sufficiently precise, and bifurcate the offer from the merits so the tribunal only sees the costs consequence after award.
