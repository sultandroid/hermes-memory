# CASE 09 — SASO Technical Regulations: Building-Materials Compliance in KSA

**Theme:** SASO standards
**Year:** 2025 (article); regulations updated continuously since SASO's 2021 expansion
**Author:** Tabseer / Saudi conformity-assessment consultancy
**URL:** https://tabseer.co/en/saso-technical-regulation-for-building
**Source tier:** Specialist conformity-assessment consultancy (third-party assessment body)

## Summary

Tabseer's guide is the most-cited practitioner overview of SASO's building-materials compliance regime, covering the **SASO Technical Regulations for Building Materials** (notably the bricks, tiles, ceramics, insulation, cement, steel and electrical product series). The article documents the *three-track* compliance model: (a) **SASO IECEE** for electrical products, (b) **SABER** (online platform) for product conformity assessment and shipment clearance, and (c) **Quality Mark** for high-risk/critical products. It is explicit that *"failure to comply with the SASO standards list for building materials can lead to shipment rejection, financial penalties, and delays in entering the Saudi market."* Most importantly for construction contracts: SASO compliance is checked at *port of entry* and at *project site* — a non-compliant material can be ordered off-site, and the contractor cannot recover the cost from the Employer.

## Five-Lesson Extraction

1. **Vocabulary — SASO vs SBC vs SFDA:** SASO = Saudi Standards, Metrology and Quality Organization (now under SASO–Saudi Building Code Center split; see Case 01). SBC = Saudi Building Code (technical). SFDA = Saudi Food & Drug Authority (food/pharma). The three operate in parallel; the SBC *adopts* SASO standards for materials testing methods.
2. **Evidence — Certificate-of-conformity (CoC) trail:** Every imported building material must have a CoC (or Quality Mark for high-risk products). Retain the CoC, batch number, and supplier's test report in the project's Materials Approval Register. *Absence* = defective work in a defect-liability arbitration.
3. **Procedural — SABER + SASO IECEE workflow:** Pre-shipment: get a Product CoC via SABER (online). Electrical products: SASO IECEE. At port: SASO inspector checks CoC vs shipment. At site: consultant's materials engineer checks CoC vs as-delivered. Three checkpoints, three chances to fail.
4. **Strategic — Subcontractor liability allocation:** Push the SASO/SABER obligation *up the supply chain*. The supplier provides the CoC; the subcontractor warrants CoC validity; the main contractor warrants compliance in the head contract. A pass-through clause without back-to-back warranty is unenforceable against the Employer.
5. **Principle — "Rejection at port" principle:** A material that fails SASO at Jeddah Islamic Port is not the Employer's problem — the contractor bought the wrong material. This is the cleanest cost-allocation principle in the entire KSA construction regulatory stack. Document the *intended-use* and the *CoC scope* on every purchase order.
