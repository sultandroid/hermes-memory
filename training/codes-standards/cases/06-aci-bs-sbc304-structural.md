# CASE 06 — ACI 318 / BS 8110 / SBC 304: Structural-Concrete Code Mapping in KSA

**Theme:** ACI / BS structural standards
**Year:** 2025 (article); ACI 318-19 published 2019; SBC 304 references both
**Author:** Sixteens Consultancy (GCC structural-engineering practice; based in UAE)
**URL:** https://www.sixteensconsultancy.com/blog/building-codes-gcc-countries
**Source tier:** Specialist engineering blog; written by practising structural engineers for GCC projects

## Summary

Sixteens' engineer-facing guide describes the SBC structural code family — **SBC 304 (Concrete Structures), SBC 305 (Masonry), SBC 306 (Steel), SBC 307 (Soils & Foundations)** — and maps each to its international reference: ACI 318 for concrete (as adopted by SBC 304), AISC 360 for steel, ASCE 7 for loads, ACI 530 for masonry, and AASHTO for transportation structures. The piece also flags that **Saudi Arabia signed a formal licensing agreement with the American Concrete Institute (ACI)** in 2010 giving the Saudi Building Code National Committee the right to reproduce ACI 318 text verbatim in the SBC 304 — a direct technical-deference arrangement. Direct quote (paraphrased from the guide): SBC 304 "directly references ACI 318-19 for the design of reinforced concrete, with local amendments for seismic zones, durability, and concrete-cover requirements."

## Five-Lesson Extraction

1. **Vocabulary — "Direct reference" vs "Adopted by reference":** A code may be *adopted* (text becomes the law) or *referenced* (named as the technical source). SBC 304 *adopts* ACI 318-19 with amendments; this is why an Engineer can cite ACI equations directly in design notes without repeating the formula.
2. **Evidence — Saudi-specific amendments:** Even where ACI 318 governs, SBC 304 adds local amendments — concrete cover (≥ 40 mm for cast-in-place against soil in KSA), durability requirements (chloride/sulphate exposure near coasts), and seismic zoning (Saudi Seismic Code is being upgraded). Pull the SBC 304 amendment annex; do not assume the SBC mirrors ACI page-for-page.
3. **Procedural — Code-mix trap:** Some consultants mix British (BS 8110) and American (ACI) references in the same structural package. This is a defect-liability exposure. The contract specs must nominate **one** design code (typically ACI 318 via SBC 304) and one load standard (typically ASCE 7 or SBC 301).
4. **Strategic — Forensics in disputes:** When investigating a structural defect, the arbitration expert must walk the *code of record* in the contract, not the latest code. A 2015 contract specifies ACI 318-14; reading 318-19 to assess liability is a procedural error that opposing counsel will exploit.
5. **Principle — ACI licensing is the "smoking gun":** Saudi has a *paid licensing relationship* with ACI. The inference: when an expert claims a "Saudi-unique" concrete rule, the burden is on them to point to the SBC 304 *amendment* — the default is the international ACI rule.
