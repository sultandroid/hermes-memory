# CASE 02 — FIDIC Red Book 1999 vs 2017: Practical Difference in GCC

**Theme:** FIDIC Red Book 1999 vs 2017 — practical difference in GCC
**Year:** 2026 (article); commentary on 2017 FIDIC editions
**Author:** Lexilio Editorial (construction-law advisory; GCC-experienced authors)
**URL:** https://lexilio.co/resources/blog/fidic-2017-vs-1999-what-changed
**Source tier:** Specialist legal blog; widely cited by GCC contractors

## Summary

Lexilio's clause-by-clause comparison of the 1999 and 2017 Red Books frames the change as "the most significant rewrite in FIDIC's history" and explains why the GCC — particularly Saudi Vision 2030 megaprojects (NEOM, KAFD, Red Sea) — is moving from "bespoke 1999 hybrids" to the 2017 Silver Book. The article highlights the **structural split of Clause 20** (claims) from new **Clause 21** (disputes), the **50% word-count increase** in core conditions, the replacement of DAB with **DAAB (Dispute Avoidance / Adjudication Board)**, and the proliferation of strict time-bars. Direct quote: *"The 2017 forms are 'harder' contracts: they contain more time-bars, more 'deemed' rejections, and more obligations that, if missed, can lead to the total loss of entitlement."*

## Five-Lesson Extraction

1. **Vocabulary — DAB → DAAB:** The Dispute Adjudication Board (1999) has been renamed Dispute Avoidance / Adjudication Board. The semantic shift is operational: the DAAB is *encouraged to issue informal recommendations* before formal decisions. Track both.
2. **Evidence — Version-drift exposure:** If a contractor's commercial team uses 1999 process habits on a 2017 contract, the *"right to do so may be lost"* through missed sub-clauses. Demand contract-version training for any team mobilised on a Vision 2030 site.
3. **Procedural — Clause 20.1 notice windows:** "The claiming Party shall give a Notice to the Engineer, describing the event or circumstance giving rise to the cost, loss, delay or extension of time…" — notice is now a *standalone* trigger with its own strict window, no longer bundled inside the dispute pathway.
4. **Strategic — KSA owner playbook:** Saudi owners are *moving away from bespoke 1999 hybrids*; contractors who price assuming 1999-friendly variation cost-recovery (Engineer's 28-day determination, etc.) will misprice 2017 risk. Re-price the preliminaries and the bonds.
5. **Principle — "Pathway to recovery" clarity:** The 2017 split was designed to *"prevent claims from festering into multi-year arbitrations."* In Saudi practice, that means the contractor's contemporaneous-records discipline (clause 20.1 supporting details) is now determinative, not the arbitration bundle. Build the records system *before* mobilisation.
