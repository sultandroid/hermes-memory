# CASE 01 — Saudi Building Code (SBC) Governance, Royal Decrees & 2024 Update

**Theme:** Saudi Building Code (SBC) — most-cited sections in disputes
**Year:** 2026 (publication year; covers Royal Decrees through 2025)
**Author:** GLA & Company (regional law firm, UAE/GCC focus)
**URL:** https://practiceguides.chambers.com/practice-guides/construction-law-2026/saudi-arabia/trends-and-developments
**Source tier:** Chambers Global Practice Guides (peer-reviewed by Chambers editors; widely cited in legal community)

## Summary

This Chambers practice guide is the most up-to-date, citable legal summary of the SBC's current statutory footing. It traces the chain of Royal Decrees that give the code force (M/43/2017, amended by M/15/2019 and M/88/2024), explains the institutional reorganisation that moved oversight from SASO to a new Saudi Building Code Center under MOMAH (Council of Ministers Decision No. 656, 4 March 2025), and flags the new enforcement teeth: a 9 October 2024 amendment to Article 9 of the SBC Implementation Law now requires an **occupancy certificate as a prerequisite for the full activation of electrical current** — a direct compliance lever. The SBC 2024 update replaced the 2018 version as mandatory from 30 June 2025 and added three new structural codes (Saudi Seismic Design Code for Steel; Repair/Rehabilitation of Existing Concrete Structures; GFRP-Reinforced Concrete). Direct quote: *"The SBC 2024 update, which replaces the 2018 version, became mandatory from 30 June 2025 following a 180-day transitional period."*

## Five-Lesson Extraction

1. **Vocabulary — NCTL vs SBC:** The two pillars of Saudi construction law are the New Civil Transactions Law (NCTL, Royal Decree M/191/2023) — substantive contract/tort law — and the SBC, the technical/regulatory code. Disputes almost always invoke **both**: NCTL for liability/quantum, SBC for the standard-of-care benchmark.
2. **Evidence — Occupancy-certificate-as-licence:** Document the SBC compliance file at handover (completion certificate, Civil Defense approval, MOMAH inspection sign-offs). Without it, the Employer cannot legally energise the building — turning "form over substance" into a real cost lever against the contractor.
3. **Procedural — Transitional-window risk:** A 180-day transition between code editions is a defect-liability hot zone. Specify the applicable SBC edition by clause (not by reference to "the SBC"). Note the 30 June 2025 cutoff for the 2024 update.
4. **Strategic — Choice-of-forum:** For cross-border contracts, the guide warns that *"It is always therefore advisable to seek counsel in the jurisdiction of governance, in addition to counsel in the jurisdiction where the parties are domiciled and where the project will be executed."* SCCA arbitration under the SBC framework is materially different from ad hoc DIAC/ICC arbitration.
5. **Principle — "Two major pieces of legislation" framing:** Treat the SBC as quasi-statute. A code violation is not a mere contractual defect — it is a public-law non-compliance that exposes the employer to municipal penalties and the contractor to license/sanction risk. Frame any delay/dispute claim around *which* SBC clause was breached and *when*.
