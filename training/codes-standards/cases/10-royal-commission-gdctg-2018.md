# CASE 10 — Royal Commission for Jubail & Yanbu: General Design Criteria & Technical Guidelines (RCY-GDCTG 2018, 4th Ed.)

**Theme:** Royal Commission standards (Yanbu, Jubail)
**Year:** 2018 (publication, 4th Edition Revision 1.00)
**Author:** Royal Commission for Jubail & Yanbu (primary regulatory authority)
**URL:** https://kh.aquaenergyexpo.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/General-Design-Criteria-Technical-Guidelines.pdf
**Source tier:** Primary regulatory document — the binding design standard for the Jubail/Yanbu industrial cities

## Summary

The **RCY-GDCTG 2018, 4th Edition Revision 1.00** is the controlling design standard for any construction project in the Royal Commission's Jubail and Yanbu industrial cities. It is structured in five sections: (1) Introduction, (2) Applicable Codes and Standards, (3) General Design Criteria and Standards, (4) Civil Design, and (5) Structural Design — with dedicated subsections on site conditions, environmental quality, roads, storm drainage, sewerage, water distribution, structural concrete codes, and structural steel codes. The document's Section 2 makes the operative legal point: in RC territory, **the GDCTG prevails over the SBC wherever they conflict** — the Royal Commission is a separate regulatory authority with its own jurisdiction. The standard is enforced by the Royal Commission's Technical Support Services – Structural Engineering (TSS-SE) group, which reviews and approves every structural design package. Direct quote (Section 2.1): *"The Codes, Standards, and Manuals listed herein are the minimum requirements for the design of permanent and temporary facilities within the Royal Commission's jurisdiction."*

## Five-Lesson Extraction

1. **Vocabulary — "Royal Commission" jurisdictional exclusivity:** The Royal Commission for Jubail & Yanbu (RCJY) is a *territorial regulator* with its own planning, environmental, building-permit, and operating-licence authority. Inside RC territory, RCJY rules *override* MOMAH/SBC rules. A building that complies with the SBC but violates the GDCTG is non-compliant.
2. **Evidence — TSS-SE structural review:** The Royal Commission's *Technical Support Services – Structural Engineering* (TSS-SE) group must approve every structural package before construction. The approval stamp + comment trail is the legal evidence that structural design is *code-compliant* in RC territory.
3. **Procedural — Section 2 codes/standards list:** The GDCTG Section 2 is a *closed list*. If a code is not listed there, it is not approved for use on an RC project. Mixing in non-listed standards (e.g. Eurocode when only ACI 318 is listed) is a procedural defect.
4. **Strategic — Industrial-cities premium:** Yanbu and Jubail are Saudi Arabia's primary petrochemical and industrial zones (Sadara, SABIC, Aramco joint ventures). Working in RC territory is a *specialty market* — contractors need separate prequalification, separate safety training, and separate environmental compliance (Section 3.2 — environmental quality standards). Pricing must reflect the *premium regulatory environment*.
5. **Principle — "Minimum requirements" wording:** Section 2.1's "minimum requirements" language is crucial: the GDCTG sets a *floor*, not a *ceiling*. In a defect-liability dispute, a contractor cannot rely on "we met the GDCTG" if the *Employer-spec* required a higher performance. The GDCTG is the regulatory floor; the contract specs are the contractual floor.
