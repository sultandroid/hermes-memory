# CASE 04 — FIDIC Silver Book (EPC/Turnkey): Risk Allocation in KSA Megaprojects

**Theme:** FIDIC Silver Book (EPC/Turnkey) — risk allocation in mega-projects
**Year:** 2026 (article); commentary on 1999 and 2017 Silver Books
**Author:** Strata Risk Advisory (Saudi-based risk-management firm)
**URL:** https://www.stratasaudi.com/fidic-claims-saudi-arabia
**Source tier:** Specialist GCC risk advisory; written for Vision-2030 contractors

## Summary

Strata's 2026 guide maps FIDIC-claim risk against Saudi Vision-2030 megaprojects (NEOM, The Line, Red Sea, new economic zones), and is explicit that *"FIDIC contracts introduce a unique framework for managing disputes and claims — one that differs fundamentally from traditional Fixed-Price or Cost-Plus arrangements."* The article identifies the four operative risk-shifts that turn an EPC Silver-Book project into a claims minefield: strict notice, contemporaneous documentation, escalation protocols, and the *contractor's* full design risk under Sub-Clause 5.1 (Design Responsibility). The piece stresses that the 2017 Silver — like the 2017 Red and Yellow — now requires the Engineer/Employer DAAB pathway rather than ad-hoc arbitration, and that missing notice bars is the single largest cause of contractor entitlement loss. Direct quote: *"Contractors who fail to navigate these procedures correctly risk losing legitimate entitlements to time and cost adjustments, even when circumstances clearly warrant them."*

## Five-Lesson Extraction

1. **Vocabulary — EPC vs Turnkey:** Under Silver Book, "Turnkey" means the Contractor takes *total* responsibility: design, construction, procurement, commissioning, performance-test. The "single point of responsibility" allocation places ground-condition risk (Sub-Clause 4.7) on the Contractor — unlike the Red Book where the Employer bears Unforeseen Physical Conditions.
2. **Evidence — "Strict notice requirements":** Build a 7-day "warning radar" — every event with cost/schedule potential triggers a notice within 7 days of becoming aware (1999 §20.1; 2017 has even shorter windows). Keep the notice register and contemporaneous records co-located with the programme.
3. **Procedural — Performance-test liability:** Silver-Book warranties run from the *Performance Certificate*, not taking-over. Track the defects-liability period from the right starting event. Saudi industrial projects (e.g. Yanbu, Jubail) often have 24-month warranty plus 12-month performance demonstration — push back on a single "warranty" label.
4. **Strategic — NEOM / The Line pricing:** These projects push technology, schedule and interface risk onto the Contractor. Strata's list of "scale, technical innovation, regulatory evolution" requires *interface-management premium* in the bid, not just a contingency. Contractors who under-price interface risk on a Silver Book lose money twice (preliminaries overrun + claim denial).
5. **Principle — "Even when circumstances clearly warrant them":** Saudi arbitral practice is unforgiving on procedural defaults. Substantive merit is irrelevant if Sub-Clause 20.1 notice is late or the contemporaneous record is thin. The Silver Book is a *documentation regime* dressed as a contract — operate it that way from day one.
