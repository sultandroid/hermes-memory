# CASE 03 — FIDIC Yellow Book (Design-Build) 2017: When It Applies in KSA

**Theme:** FIDIC Yellow Book (Design-Build) — when does it apply in Saudi
**Year:** 2017 (article) — covers the launch of FIDIC 2017 Yellow/Red/Silver
**Author:** Euan Lloyd, Partner & Head of Construction & Infrastructure, Al Tamimi & Company (Abu Dhabi)
**URL:** https://www.tamimi.com/law-update-articles/new-fidic-2017-yellow-book-a-new-claims-procedure
**Source tier:** Al Tamimi & Company is the largest Middle East law firm; widely cited in GCC construction arbitration pleadings

## Summary

Lloyd's article, published the day FIDIC released the Second-Edition Yellow Book, identifies three changes that matter in KSA design-build procurement: (1) the **50%-longer contract** (Second Editions are "about 50% longer than the First Edition"); (2) the **new early-warning mechanism** (Sub-Clause 8.4) requiring either party to notify the Engineer of any event "known or probable" that may affect cost, time or quality; and (3) the **unified claims procedure** under Sub-Clause 20.1 — Employer and Contractor claims are now treated symmetrically, replacing the 1999 asymmetry where 2.5 governed Employer claims and 20.1 governed Contractor claims. Direct quote: *"The distinction between Employer claims and Contractor claims has been removed. All claims are now made under Sub-Clause 20.1 of the Second Edition."*

## Five-Lesson Extraction

1. **Vocabulary — "Second Edition" vs "Yellow Book 2017":** "Yellow Book" is shorthand; the formal title is *Conditions of Contract for Plant & Design-Build*, Second Edition 2017. The 1999 version is the "First Edition." Specify the edition in any contract — silence invites a default to the older, more contractor-friendly drafting.
2. **Evidence — Advance-warning trail:** Maintain a single project-wide "early warning register" with date-stamped entries (issuer, recipient, Engineer response, mitigation proposal). Sub-Clause 8.4 is a *standalone* obligation — failure to warn is a separate breach even if no damage results.
3. **Procedural — 3.7 Engineer neutrality:** The Engineer must now *"act neutrally"* under Sub-Clause 3.7. In Saudi public projects where the Engineer is the Employer's own consultant, the contractor can argue that neutrality is structurally impossible and seek substitution — but only by following the contractual mechanism.
4. **Strategic — When to use Yellow in KSA:** The Yellow Book is the right form where the Contractor designs *and* builds, the Employer brief is output-based, and an Engineer is retained to certify (typical: industrial plants, PV solar, certain PIF infrastructure). For pure *construction* with an Employer design, use the Red Book; for *turnkey single-point EPC* (no Engineer), use the Silver Book. Mismatched form = mismatched risk.
5. **Principle — "Dispute avoidance" theme:** Lloyd notes the drafting *"is consistent with the dispute avoidance theme which runs throughout the Second Edition."* In SCCA arbitration, a party that ignored an early-warning opportunity is judged harshly on causation and contributory conduct — even where the underlying claim succeeds on liability.
