# Codes & Standards — Top Lessons

## From Articles 001 + 010 (SBC 2024 + Implementation Law) — Saudi Statutory Code

1. **SBC 2024 is mandatory from 30 June 2025. Anything designed before that, on a project that completes after, is potentially non-compliant.**
   Transition was 180 days. Use this lever in any design-defect claim.

2. **Article 9 (amended 9 Oct 2024) is the strongest enforcement lever in the SBC arsenal.**
   An occupancy certificate is now a prerequisite for full electrical activation. A building that is "complete" but not SBC-compliant cannot be legally energised, transferred, or occupied. **Drafting consequence:** in any handover or defect-liability claim, the SBC-compliance audit is your friend.

3. **SBC 801 (fire protection) is NFPA-derived — but the Saudi version diverges in the High-Rise provisions.**
   Always check the SBC 801 / NFPA cross-reference table before relying on a US fire-engineering standard. The Saudi High-Rise Chapter is unique.

## From Article 002 (Lexilio on FIDIC 2017) — Practical Difference

4. **The 2017 Red Book is "the most significant rewrite in FIDIC's history." 50% longer, more prescriptive, more time-bars.**
   Clause 20 (claims) is split from new Clause 21 (disputes). The DAB is renamed DAAB. **Drafting consequence:** a 2017 contract managed with 1999 habits is a commercial catastrophe. The 7-day notice window is the new normal; missing it strips entitlement even where the cause is sound.

5. **The 2017 forms are "harder" than 1999 hybrids. Vision-2030 owners are moving decisively to 2017.**
   If you work on NEOM, Red Sea, Diriyah, or PIF projects, your contract will be 2017 by default. Update your playbook.

## From Article 003 (Al Tamimi on FIDIC 2017 Yellow) — Design-Build

6. **The 2017 Yellow introduces Sub-Clause 8.4 advance-warning and unifies the claims procedure.**
   Employer and Contractor claims are now symmetric under Sub-Clause 20.1. The Engineer under Sub-Clause 3.7 must act **neutrally** (not as the Employer's agent — a material change from 1999).

7. **Decision rule for FIDIC colour in Saudi:**
   - **Yellow (Design-Build)** — Contractor designs AND builds, Employer brief is output-based, Engineer retained to certify (industrial plants, PV solar, certain PIF infrastructure)
   - **Red (Construction)** — pure construction with Employer design
   - **Silver (EPC/Turnkey)** — single-point turnkey with no Engineer (NEOM, Aramco, SABIC mega-projects)
   - Mismatch = mismatch of risk = dispute

## From Article 004 (Tamimi/Pinsent on Silver Book) — EPC Risk Allocation

8. **Silver Book transfers nearly all design and performance risk to the Contractor.**
   The "single point of responsibility" promise is real — but so is the scope of the Contractor's risk. **Drafting consequence:** price the risk premium, not just the direct cost. On Silver Book projects, contingency typically runs 15-25% vs 8-12% on Red Book.

## From Article 007 (Mostadam) — Saudi Green Building

9. **Mostadam is mandatory on most PIF-funded projects and increasingly required by major private clients.**
   Pearl 1 (basic) → Pearl 2 (intermediate) → Pearl 3 (advanced) → Pearl 4 (net-zero). Each level adds 5-10% CapEx but unlocks 15-25% OpEx savings over 20 years.

## Cross-cutting

10. **A code violation is not a mere contractual defect — it is a public-law non-compliance that exposes the employer to municipal penalties and the contractor to licence/sanction risk, and that can be used as a defect-liability cause of action in SCCA arbitration.**
    This is the doctrinal peg — the SBC is enforced through both civil and administrative channels.
