# Material Substitution as a Control Lever (Contractor Strategy)

## The pattern (Aseer flooring example, Aug 2026)

A material substitution request (e.g. replacing FI_FL_01/02 Mosaic-Effect Porcelain Tiles
with epoxy + marble) was raised by a CG reviewer (Mansour El Rezeny), not by the owner.
The contractor (Samaya) emailed the designer (NRS / Jim Richards) asking for a technical
opinion on the substitution.

**The email was NOT a commitment — it was a control lever.** The contractor's intent:

- Get the designer's **technical opinion on record** as a reference point.
- If the designer **approves** → the contractor is still NOT obligated to proceed. It can
  refuse from its own side and close the matter.
- If the designer **rejects** → the contractor has a **technical basis** to close the
  matter ("NRS rejected it, done").

Either way the contractor holds the decision. The suggestion originated from CG, not the
owner, so there is no owner mandate forcing the change.

## Why the contractor may want to refuse

**Approving one material change opens a precedent for many more.** Once a substitution is
accepted, any party (CG, a supplier, a team member) can argue "we changed the flooring,
why not change X, Y, Z too?" Each change costs:

- Time to re-approve samples (sample approval is a real schedule hit)
- Time to update drawings / plans
- Pressure on an already-tight programme

On a compressed schedule, opening the door to broad material substitution is a
programme risk. The contractor's stance: not everything needs to be imported; local
substitutes are preferred where they fit, but changes are gated deliberately, not
rubber-stamped.

## Decision rules

1. **Source of the request matters.** A suggestion from CG/reviewer ≠ an owner mandate.
   The contractor can decline a CG-suggested change from its own side.
2. **Get the designer's opinion on record first.** It is ammunition either way — approval
   gives you cover to proceed if you want, rejection gives you a technical reason to close.
3. **Approval ≠ obligation.** Designer approval does not force the contractor to change.
4. **Precedent control.** Treat each accepted substitution as opening the door to more.
   Gate changes deliberately; weigh the schedule cost of re-approval before accepting.
5. **Local vs import.** Prefer local substitutes where they fit the spec and schedule —
   reduces import lead-time pain — but only via the controlled change path.

## Email pattern (Ali → Jim, 13-Aug-2026)

- Subject: "Aseer Museum _ Inquiry About Replacing Material"
- Body: reference attached photo, ask about replacing specified finishes (FI_FL_01/02)
  with an alternative, note the suggestion was raised by [CG reviewer] for consideration
  and technical review, ask the designer to advise whether the alternative can be
  considered and approved.
- Designer reply (Jim): "yes this looks good" but asks for colour variations (cream +
  dark grey) and data sheets / material reference before final sign-off.

**Note:** a positive-but-conditional designer reply ("looks good, but send data sheets")
is NOT final approval. The change is still open until the designer confirms on the
actual material data. Track it as pending, not approved.
