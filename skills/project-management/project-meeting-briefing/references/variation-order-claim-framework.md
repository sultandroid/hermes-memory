# Variation Order (VO) Claim Construction — Aseer / Samaya

How to build a defensible Variation Order claim for a construction/museum project, from the FF-pump claim session (2026-08-30). Reusable for any "the owner/consultant changed something and it's costly" situation.

## The core claim logic (lead with this)
> **The impact/change originates from the OWNER (MoC), not from the contractor.**
Prove the change is driven by owner-side requirements (a missing prior approval, architectural comments, a consultant/Civil Defence requirement) — NOT by the contractor's scope. If the contractor is not the source of the change, it is a variation, not their scope.

Defense line (verbatim, usable in a meeting):
> "Bring the Civil Defence approval and do what you want — you'll get Civil Defence approval. We are NOT changing the pump, NOT changing fire hydrant locations as you claim — Civil Defence was already approved before. We did not approve this. But now they are claiming against us AND making architectural amendments."

## The four pillars of a strong claim
1. **Hidden / undiscovered item** — the item was not identified by the design/assessment report (e.g. Blue House said "equipment" generally, never named the pumps). Contract has a hidden-defects clause.
2. **Costly** — the item's cost exceeds the relevant BOQ line item (e.g. pump ≈ SAR 350K induction / 650K vertical turbine, alone exceeding the whole fire-fighting BOQ line).
3. **Document-priority hierarchy** — resolve conflicts by contract order: **tender drawings → BOQ → specs → Employer Requirements**. The Employer Requirement beats the Design Base Report. Quote the hierarchy explicitly.
4. **Owner obligation gap** — a missing owner-provided item (building license, prior Civil Defence approval) is the root cause of the added requirements. Civil Defence treats the building as NEW because no prior approval exists; providing it is the OWNER's obligation, not the contractor's.

## "Present the problem, not the solution" tactic
When a costly solution exists (e.g. smoke curtain ≈ SAR 300–400K) but the comment is architectural, **do NOT propose the solution** — present the problem only, so the request comes from the architect/consultant and the cost lands in their design scope, not the contractor's. Proposing the solution yourself makes you own the cost.

## Verbatim contract quotes (Aseer Contract 0010003521)
Always quote the clause text verbatim with source (article + line), not a paraphrase. Key clauses:
- **Hidden defects / unusual obstacles** — Article Six: "In case the Contractor encounters - during the execution of the Works - any material difficulties or unusual obstacles/hidden defects that were not forceable, the Contractor must notify the Government Entity's Representative within (10) ten days..." (notify within 10 days or lose the right).
- **Change orders** — Article Four: "The Government Entity may issue change orders in accordance with the Article entitled 'Increase and Decrease of Obligations'..." and "The Contractor shall not make any changes to the Services unless it is instructed in writing to do so by the Government Entity's Representative."
- **Increase/Decrease cap** — Article Six: increase change orders ≤ 10% of total contract value; decrease ≤ 20%; additional works must be within scope and not change the nature/financial position; contractor approval needed if no corresponding BOQ item.

## Report structure (Samaya DOCX)
1. Executive Summary → 2. Background → 3. The Claim → 4. Contractual Basis (verbatim clauses, each with source) → 5. Document Priority Analysis (table) → 6. Owner Obligation Gap → 7. Core Claim Logic (impact from owner) → 8. Cost Breakdown (table) → 9. Supporting Evidence (screenshots) → 10. Requested Relief → 11. Attachments.

## Pitfalls
- **10-day notification window** — the hidden-defects clause forfeits the right if not notified within 10 days of discovery. Note the date the defect was revealed.
- **Quote verbatim, never paraphrase** — a paraphrase loses the contractual force. Attach screenshots of every clause.
- **Don't tie the report to a pending approval** — prepare it now, update the numbers (e.g. pump capacity) after the approval lands.
- **The report is a claim, not a complaint** — every section must trace to a contract clause or document, not to frustration.
